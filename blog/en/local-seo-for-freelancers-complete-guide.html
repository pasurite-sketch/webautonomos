What is local SEO, and why does it matter for your business? If you're a freelancer or run a small business in Spain, local SEO helps customers near you find your business when they search for what you offer: an electrician in Elche, a hairdresser in Valencia or a plumber who covers Elda and Petrer. This guide explains how it works and what you can do yourself, without a big marketing budget.

Potential customers in your area look online for local businesses like yours, often from their mobile. If you don't appear in local search, they contact someone else. A clear local SEO strategy puts your business in front of them when they're ready to get in touch.

What is local SEO?

Local SEO, short for local search engine optimisation, is the work of making your business visible online when someone nearby looks for a product or service you offer, on Google Maps and in the ordinary search results. It combines your Google Business Profile, your website, your reviews and consistent business information across the web.

Traditional SEO aims to bring traffic to a website from search engines in general. Local SEO focuses on local search: searches made with a location in mind, whether or not the user types the name of a town. It's a branch of digital marketing that can improve the visibility of any local business serving customers in a specific area.

Where local results appear

Imagine someone in Alicante types “emergency plumber Alicante” or “plumber near me” on their phone. A local search like this can show your business in three places:

  • The local pack, or Map Pack: a map with a few nearby businesses, often close to the top of the results, each with its rating, opening hours and a button to call.
  • Google Maps, where people look for places near their location, check routes and compare local businesses.
  • Local organic results: the pages listed below the map, where your website competes with other websites.

Our guides explain how to rank in the Map Pack and how to get your business listed.

What local SEO includes

Local SEO brings together several elements that support each other:

  • your Business Profile, the free listing that shows your business in local results;
  • your website, with pages for your services and the towns you cover;
  • customer reviews and your replies;
  • your NAP: name, address and phone number, identical everywhere;
  • local citations and links from other sites;
  • local content written for your customers.

How does local SEO work?

Google says its local results are based mainly on three key factors: relevance, distance and prominence. Local SEO aims to improve the two you can influence, relevance and prominence, so that your business can be matched with relevant local searches.

Relevance: matching the search

Relevance is how well your business matches what someone is looking for. The right categories, a clear list of services and pages that name your trade and town all help. A carpenter in Elda who lists “fitted wardrobes” and “kitchen units” is relevant for more queries than one who only writes “carpentry”.

Distance: how close you are

Distance is how far your business is from the location named in the search or, if none is named, from the searcher's location, which is why two people a few streets apart may see different results. You can't change it, but you can ensure your location or service area is correct.

Prominence: how well known you are

Prominence reflects how well known your business is, online and offline. Google uses information from across the web, such as links, articles and directories, and states that review count and review score factor into local ranking: more reviews and positive ratings can improve it. Your position in the web results also counts, so SEO best practices apply too.

The search engine also states that there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Be wary of anyone who sells you a top position by a fixed date.

Local SEO vs traditional SEO: what's the difference?

The main difference is reach. Traditional SEO tries to rank a website for everyone who looks up a topic, wherever they are, while local SEO focuses on target customers near your business, so you compete in your own town rather than across the whole country.

Local SEO doesn't replace traditional SEO: it builds on it. If you sell online across Spain, traditional SEO matters more; if your customers come from Valencia and nearby towns, focus on local SEO first.

Why is local SEO important for small businesses?

Local SEO is important because it puts your business in front of customers who are already looking for what you do, close to where you work. It's a form of local marketing that lets local businesses win clients without depending only on paid advertising.

  • Local visibility: you can appear in local search when someone in your city searches for your service.
  • Direct contact: from the local pack, a customer can call you or ask for directions in one tap.
  • Trust: real photos, a clear website design and positive reviews can increase the confidence of potential clients.
  • Traffic: more visits from people interested in your services.
  • Lasting presence: your pages and your listing keep working when you're not paying for ads.

Mobile search

Many of your customers will look you up on their mobile, often on the move. On a small screen, the local pack and features like the call button come first, so complete details and a mobile-friendly design make it easier for them to contact you.

Which businesses need local SEO?

Any business serving customers in a specific area can benefit from local SEO, whether customers come to you or you go to them. The priorities depend on your type of business.

Trades that travel to customers

A plumber or electrician covering multiple towns, such as Alicante, Elda and Petrer, works at customers' homes. For this kind of service-area business, the key is to list the towns you really cover, describe each service and ask for a review after every job.

Shops, salons and professionals with premises

A hairdresser in Valencia, a shop in Elche or an accountant (gestor) based in Alicante has a fixed location, so the address, opening hours and photos matter to anyone searching nearby. Check your details in local listings. Reviews and useful content build trust with clients who don't know you yet.

Google Business Profile: the starting point

Your Google Business Profile, formerly Google My Business, is the free listing that shows your business on Maps and in local search results. It's the first step in any local SEO strategy.

Create or claim it, then verify it

Set it up, or claim it if your business already has a listing, so you don't end up with a duplicate. Then complete the verification you're asked for: a verified business can show up in local search results.

Complete every section

The search engine says complete and accurate information makes it easier to match your business with relevant searches. Include the key information:

  • the primary category that best describes your main activity, plus specific secondary categories;
  • opening hours, with special hours for holidays;
  • a phone number you answer and a link to the right page of your site;
  • a description of your services and the towns you cover;
  • your services or products, with a short explanation of each;
  • attributes and other profile features, such as accepted payment methods.

See our optimisation tips and how to choose your business categories.

Your real name, and your location or service area

Use the name customers know, as it appears on your sign and invoices. Google's guidelines don't allow you to include keywords or towns, so “Fontanería López” is fine but “Fontanería López cheap plumber Valencia” is not. If clients visit your location, show the exact address; if you travel to them, hide it and set a service area.

Photos and updates

Real photos are important: add pictures of your premises, your van and finished jobs. Updates can include news or offers; they aren't among Google's ranking tips, but they show that your business is active. See how photos and videos help and how to publish posts.

NAP and local citations: the same details everywhere

NAP stands for name, address and phone number. Ensure these details are identical wherever your business appears: search engines gather business data from multiple sources, and matching details make it clear which ones are right. Include exactly the same details on:

  • your website's footer and contact page;
  • your Google Business Profile;
  • your social media profiles;
  • online listings such as Páginas Amarillas and trade association sites.

An example of inconsistent details

If your site says “Avenida de la Constitución 12” and a directory says “Av. Constitución, 12, bajo”, or you give a landline in one place and a mobile in another, you show two versions of the same business. Choose one format and use it everywhere.

What is a local citation?

A local citation is any mention of your NAP on another website, such as a directory, a trade association or a local news site. It's important to review your listings from time to time and correct outdated ones; our guide to local citations and NAP explains how.

Reviews and online reputation

Online reviews matter for local SEO: Google states that the number of reviews and your rating count towards local ranking, and that more reviews and positive ratings can improve it. They also help potential clients decide whether to contact you.

How to ask for reviews

  • Ask every customer once the job is done, not only the ones you think are happy.
  • Send your review link the same day by WhatsApp or email.
  • Print it as a QR code for your counter or your invoices.
  • Let customers write in their own words.

You'll find more ideas in how to get more reviews.

What review policies forbid

Google's rules on fake engagement forbid:

  • fake or bought reviews, or reviews that don't reflect a genuine experience;
  • discounts, gifts or free services in exchange for a review;
  • discouraging negative reviews, or asking only satisfied customers.

Content that breaks these rules can be removed, so build your reputation honestly.

Respond to every review

Respond politely to each review, including negative ones: other customers will read your replies, and they shape how people see your brand. With a complaint, stay calm, acknowledge the problem and offer to sort it out. See how to reply to negative reviews.

Keyword research: the words your customers use

A local keyword combines your service with a location: “electrician in Elda”, “emergency plumber Petrer” or “hairdresser Alicante”. Use these terms where they're relevant, in your titles, headings, content and profile description, for each town you target, but never in your business name.

How to find local keywords

  • Type your service into the search bar and note the suggestions and related searches.
  • Ask your customers what they typed to find you.
  • Check in Search Console which searches already show your site.
  • Look at the words local competitors use.

Our guide to local keyword research goes further.

Searching in English, Spanish or Valencian

In Spain, customers may search in Spanish, English or Valencian, and many place names have two forms: Alicante and Alacant, Elche and Elx, Valencia and València. If you work with English-speaking clients on the Costa Blanca, include terms such as “English-speaking electrician Alicante”, as long as they're true.

What role does your website play in local SEO?

A website isn't required for a Business Profile, but it's useful: your official site is another source of information about your business, your pages can rank in local search results, and it's where clients check your services before they call. For local SEO, it should include:

  • a page for each main service, with the service and the town in the title;
  • the towns or neighbourhoods you cover;
  • the same NAP as your profile;
  • a contact form, a WhatsApp button and a visible phone number;
  • a responsive web design that loads quickly, with HTTPS;
  • local content that answers your customers' questions.

Service pages and local content

Give each main service its own page, such as “Boiler repair in Alicante”, and add location pages only for towns where you really work: copied pages with the town swapped add nothing. Content on local topics, such as renovating an old flat in Valencia, shows your experience and can increase your traffic.

Links, directories and social media

Links and mentions from other websites show how well known your business is. For a local business, the useful ones are close to home:

  • suppliers, partners and clubs you sponsor;

How do you measure local SEO?

You can measure local SEO for free with three tools: Search Console, Google Analytics and your Business Profile performance data. Check them once a month to see what to improve and where to focus your marketing efforts.

  • Search Console: its Performance report shows the queries that display your site, with clicks, impressions and average position.
  • Google Analytics: data on your website traffic and where it comes from.
  • Profile performance: your visibility on Search and Maps, the search terms that showed your business, and calls, direction requests and website clicks.

Check results from another location

Results depend on location, so look up your service from your phone in different neighbourhoods and nearby towns, not only from your premises. Local rankings move from day to day; the trend in local search over several months is what counts. These basic metrics show what's working.

How to do local SEO: a step-by-step plan

You can start in an afternoon and keep going with a little time each month. Follow this order:

  1. Create or claim your Business Profile, verify it and complete every section.
  2. Make your NAP identical in all your online listings.
  3. Choose your local keywords and check your titles, headings and texts.
  4. Give each main service a page and publish local content.
  5. Ask every customer for a review and respond to all of them.
  6. Look for links from relevant local websites.
  7. Add photos and updates to your listing regularly.
  8. Measure your results each month and improve what isn't working.

How long does local SEO take?

There's no fixed timeline. How quickly your rankings change depends on the competition in your town and industry, your starting point and how regular your efforts are. Treat local SEO as an ongoing part of your marketing, not a one-off task.

Local SEO best practices and common mistakes

Many local SEO problems come from small mistakes that hold back your rankings. Each one has a simple best practice as the fix:

  • keywords or town names in your business name: use your real trading name;
  • duplicate profiles: claim and keep just one, so your visibility isn't split;
  • outdated information, such as old holiday hours: update your details whenever something changes;
  • different details on each platform: one NAP everywhere;
  • unanswered or bought reviews: ask honestly and reply to all of them;
  • town pages with copied content: write for each place you really serve;
  • no measurement: check your figures every month.
