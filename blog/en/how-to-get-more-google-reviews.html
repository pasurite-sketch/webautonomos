How do you get more Google reviews? Make leaving one quick, ask every customer at the right moment and answer the reviews you receive. None of it requires paying anyone or bending the rules. If you're a freelancer or run a local business in Spain, this guide covers each stage, from your review link to the wording of your request and what to do with a bad review.

When someone in Valencia searches for a hairdresser or an electrician, Google shows each one with its rating and how many reviews it has, often before anyone opens a website.

Why online reviews matter

Reviews do two jobs: they help people choose someone they don't know yet, and they count in local search rankings.

What Google says about local ranking

According to Google's own documentation, local search results are based mainly on three factors: relevance, distance and prominence, which is how well known a company is. Google says that review count and review score factor into local ranking, and that more reviews and positive ratings can improve it. Our guide to the Google Map Pack explains the other factors.

You'll also read that recent reviews count for more, or that the words reviewers use, such as “emergency plumber” or “quick service”, help the search engine understand what you do. These are observations by local SEO specialists, not official rules: plausible, but unconfirmed.

What reviews tell your future clients

Customers often read reviews before making a purchase or booking a job. They look at the rating, how many reviews you have, how old they are and how you answer them. Detailed opinions with polite replies from the owner say more than a perfect score from a handful of reviewers.

Set up your Google Business Profile first

Before you start collecting reviews, check that your profile (formerly Google My Business) is claimed, verified and complete. It's what appears when someone searches for you, and it's where you answer reviews.

Fill in your name, address or service area, phone number, opening hours, categories, services and photos, and add your website address. If anything is missing, our tips to optimise your profile go through each section.

How to create your Google review link

A review link is a direct link to your review form, so nobody has to search for your business on Google Maps. Google gives you a short link:

  1. Sign in with the Google account that manages your Business Profile and open it.
  2. Choose the option to ask for reviews.
  3. Copy the link and keep it somewhere easy to find, such as your phone's notes or your email signature.

Before sending it to anyone, open it yourself to check that it works. Customers need a Google account to post a review, so not everyone will be able to. Add the link to your website as well, for instance on your contact page, so visitors who already know you can share their opinion.

When should you ask for a review?

It's best to ask straight after the job or appointment, while the experience is fresh, and to include every customer, not only the ones who seemed happy.

Choose the right moment

The right moment is when the work is done and the customer has seen the result: the hairdresser after the final look in the mirror, the electrician in Elche once the new circuit is tested, the carpenter in Alicante when the wardrobe is fitted. Wait a few days and your request gets lost among other messages. Make it part of how you close each job, like handing over the invoice.

Ask everyone and accept a no

Don't filter: picking only the people you expect to be satisfied goes against Google's policies. Asking everyone also gives a more honest picture: a few critical opinions among many good ones look more genuine than a wall of perfect scores. If someone says no or doesn't answer, respect it. One request is enough; never pressure anyone.

How to ask for a review on each channel

Use whichever channel works best with each client. A good request is short and includes their name, a reference to the job, one sentence asking for an honest opinion, your direct link and a word of thanks, whatever they decide.

By WhatsApp or text message

WhatsApp is a natural choice if customers already use it to contact you. For example:

“Hi [name], thanks again for choosing us for your bathroom. If you have a minute, an honest review on Google would help other people in Valencia find us: [link]. Thanks!”

With WhatsApp Business you can save this text as a quick reply and share it in seconds; our WhatsApp setup guide explains how. A text message works the same way for anyone who doesn't use WhatsApp.

In person

Face to face, keep it natural and personal: “I'm glad you're happy with it. Reviews really make a difference for me. Would you mind leaving me a Google review? I'll send you the link now.” If they agree, share it before they leave.

By email

Email suits clients you mainly work with in writing, such as another company or anyone who requested a written quote. Keep it brief: a subject such as “How did we do?”, two or three sentences, the link on a line of its own, and your contact details in the signature. Send it the same day or the next.

You can also post the link on your social media now and then. A general invitation to all your followers is fine, because it doesn't single anyone out.

Use a QR code or an NFC tag at the counter

For customers you see in person, a QR code or an NFC tag makes it easy to open your review form without typing anything.

Where to display it

Turn your review link into a QR code (many generators are free) and test it yourself before printing. Good places for it:

  • the counter or reception desk;
  • invoices, quotes and business cards;
  • a sticker on your van;
  • a note you hand over with the finished job.

NFC cards and stands

An NFC card or stand opens your review link when someone holds a compatible phone close to it. It's handy at a counter, but some phones have NFC switched off, so keep the printed version next to it.

Responding to reviews, good and bad

Try to reply to every review, briefly and in your own words. Google says that replying shows customers you value their feedback, and anyone choosing between you and a competitor will read your answers too.

Positive reviews: keep it short

Use the person's name, mention the job and keep it to a couple of lines: “Thank you, [name]! It was a pleasure fitting your new kitchen. We're here whenever you need us. – [Your name]”

Negative reviews: stay calm

A bad review stings, but a calm, polite answer reassures anyone who reads it later. A simple structure:

  1. Wait until you're calm before writing.
  2. Acknowledge the feedback and apologise if something went wrong.
  3. Explain the facts briefly, without arguing.
  4. Offer to sort it out by phone or email.

For example: “We're sorry the repair didn't go as you expected. We'd like to understand exactly what happened and fix it. Could you call us on [phone]? – [Your name]”. Our guide on replying to negative reviews covers harder cases.

Fake reviews and spam

You can't delete a review yourself. If one breaks the rules, for instance because it's spam, offensive or written by someone who was never a customer, report it from your profile. Google checks reports against its policies, which can take several days. In the meantime, answer politely that you can't find any record of that person.

Google's review rules: what not to do

Several practices aren't allowed. Don't:

  • offer incentives such as discounts, gifts or free services in exchange for a review;
  • review your own business or ask your staff to do it;
  • buy reviews, or ask people who were never customers to post them;
  • ask only happy customers, or discourage negative reviews;
  • post reviews about your competitors.

Google can remove reviews that break these rules and may restrict your profile. If you show reviews on your own website, EU consumer rules, which apply in Spain, also say you should explain whether and how you check that they come from genuine customers.

Keep a steady flow of new reviews

Make asking a habit rather than a one-off campaign. A regular flow keeps your listing current for anyone reading it, and it looks more natural than a sudden burst.

Many past customers, few reviews?

Start with your latest clients, then spread the requests over several weeks instead of messaging everyone on the same day. Skip anyone from years ago who may barely remember you.

A weekly routine

Once a week, read your latest reviews, answer any you've missed and check that your link still works.

Frequently asked questions

How many Google reviews do I need?
There's no magic number. Look at the businesses that appear next to yours in local search, and keep collecting new reviews regularly rather than aiming for a figure and stopping.
How do I create a link for customers to leave a review?
In your Google Business Profile, choose the option to ask for reviews and copy the short link it gives you. You can share it by WhatsApp, text or email, or print it on your invoices.
Can I buy Google reviews or offer a discount in exchange for one?
No. Fake or bought reviews and incentives such as discounts or gifts are against Google's rules: such reviews can be removed and the listing restricted. You can thank a customer after they've posted a review, as long as nothing depends on it.
How do I respond to a negative review without making it worse?
Wait until you're calm, acknowledge the problem, apologise if something went wrong, explain the facts without arguing and offer to continue the conversation privately. Never argue in public: other people will read your answer.
How do I remove a fake or spam review?
You can't delete it yourself, but you can report it from your profile if it breaks the rules, for example because the author was never a customer. Google checks reports against its policies, which can take several days. Meanwhile, answer politely that you have no record of that person.
How long does it take for a review to appear?
Many reviews appear quickly, but each one goes through automated checks and some can take several days to show. If a review never appears, it may have been filtered as a possible breach of the rules.
Do reviews affect my position on Google Maps?
Yes. Google says review count and score factor into local search ranking, as part of what it calls prominence, alongside relevance and distance. The idea that recent reviews or the words reviewers use count for more comes from specialists, not from official guidance.
Do I need review management software?
Not to start with. For many freelancers and small firms, a free Google Business Profile and a saved template are enough. Paid tools such as Podium or Birdeye send review requests by text or email and gather your reviews in one place.
What if a customer says no?
Respect their decision and don't insist: some people prefer not to post online. Keep asking everyone once, just after the service, without picking and choosing: some will leave a review, others won't.
