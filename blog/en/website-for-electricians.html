If you're a self-employed electrician in Valencia, Alicante or elsewhere on the Costa Blanca, word of mouth may already keep you busy. So is a website worth the effort? This guide answers that question, then covers what to include, how local SEO works, what it costs and how to start.

Do electricians need a website?

Yes, if you want customers who don't already know you. No law requires one, but a website is the simplest way to show people in your area what you do, which towns you cover and how to reach you, at any time of day.

Internet use is part of daily life: in Spain, 96.3% of people aged 16 to 74 have used the internet in the last three months, according to the INE (Encuesta TIC-H 2025). Many of your customers will search for you on their mobile, and what they see there helps them decide whether to call you or a competitor.

Word of mouth and your online presence work together

Word of mouth is still great marketing, but people often check a tradesperson online before calling. They want to see that your business is real, read a few reviews and confirm that you cover their area. If they find nothing, the recommendation can go to waste.

Isn't a Google Business Profile or social media enough?

Both are useful, and they work best alongside a website. Google Maps shows your reviews and a call button, and Facebook or Instagram are a great way to show your latest project to your followers. But neither leaves much room to explain each service or the towns you cover, and the platform sets the rules. Your website is your digital shopfront, the one place online that you control, and the centre of your digital marketing.

When a website matters less

If all your work comes from a building company that subcontracts you, it's less important. Once you want direct customers, such as homeowners, holiday-let owners, shops, offices and other businesses, or comunidades de propietarios (residents' associations), it's often where they check you out first.

Four ways a site helps you win work

A good website keeps working while you're on a job or taking a day off. For small businesses like yours, it brings four things:

  • Visibility: it helps you appear in local search results for searches such as "electrician near me" or "emergency electrician Alicante".
  • Trust: pictures of your work and customer reviews show potential customers that you're a real, reliable business.
  • Fewer wasted calls: your pages answer what customers ask every week, such as whether you issue the boletín (the electrical installation certificate) or install EV chargers, so those who call already understand what you offer.
  • Independence: enquiries come straight to your business, not through lead platforms where you may pay for leads and compete with other businesses for the same customer.

Build trust before the first call

A strong first impression matters, because letting a stranger in to work on the electrics is a question of trust. Your name, your team, your qualifications and experience, examples of finished installations and genuine reviews help a potential customer choose you over an unknown company. Use the same logo and colours as on your van, so your brand is recognisable.

Win emergency call-outs

When someone has no power on a Sunday evening, they want someone fast. A call button that works with one tap, a WhatsApp button and a line saying whether you take emergency call-outs make it quick to get hold of you.

What should an electrician's website include?

At minimum, it should include your services, the towns you cover, an easy way to call or message you, pictures of recent installations, customer reviews and the legal pages required in Spain. A simple, clear structure is enough to start; these are the essential pages:

  • Home page: your business name, what you do, where, and call and WhatsApp buttons
  • Service pages: one for each main service
  • About: you and your team, with your qualifications
  • Areas: the towns you cover
  • Gallery: recent jobs with short captions
  • Customer reviews, with a note on how you check them
  • Contact page with a short form, and the legal pages

Dedicated service pages

A single line saying "all electrical work" doesn't help anyone. Give each main service a dedicated page that explains what the job involves, where you do it and how to book it, for example:

  • New installations and rewiring projects for homes and businesses
  • Consumer unit (fuse box) replacements and fault finding
  • Boletines: electrical installation certificates
  • EV charging points
  • Repairs and breakdowns
  • LED lighting, home automation and solar installations

Doing this gives search engines a specific page to show for each search, which helps SEO, and gives customers exactly the information they came for.

The towns you cover

List the towns where you work, such as Valencia, Alicante, Elche, Elda or Benidorm, and say if you charge for travel outside your usual area. If you cover several, a short page for each can help, as long as its content is useful rather than the same text with a different place name.

Pictures of your work and customer reviews

Photos of a tidy consumer unit or a finished installation show your standards better than any slogan, so skip generic stock images. Choose your best, well-lit ones, add a caption saying what you did and where, and leave out anything that identifies a customer's home unless they agree.

Ask every customer for a review once the work is done, not only the ones who seemed happy. If you show reviews on your own pages, EU consumer rules, which apply in Spain, say you must explain whether and how you check that they come from real customers. Read how to get more Google reviews.

Contact options and legal pages

Make getting in touch effortless: a number that calls with one tap, a WhatsApp button (see how to set up WhatsApp Business) and a simple form that sends each request to your email. Ask only for a name, a phone or email, the area and a short description of the problem.

In Spain, a business website also needs a legal notice (aviso legal) and a privacy policy under the LSSI and the GDPR, plus a cookie policy if it uses cookies that aren't strictly necessary, such as analytics. Don't skip them: they matter as soon as your form collects personal data.

Design that brings in calls

Your site doesn't have to be spectacular. It has to load quickly, read well on a small screen and lead visitors straight to a call or a message.

Mobile-first design

Many of your customers will find you on their mobile, so design for the small screen first:

  • A call button at the top: one tap to phone you
  • A WhatsApp button that stays in view
  • One next step in each section: "Call now" or "Ask for a quote"
  • Large buttons, short paragraphs and readable text

Then open it on your own phone and tap every button.

Speed and simple navigation

Compress your pictures, keep the menu short and avoid heavy sliders and pop-ups. Speed keeps visitors around and helps your SEO, since page experience is one of the signals Google uses; see how speed affects your Google ranking.

Write content for customers, not for other electricians

Use everyday words: customers may search for a "fuse box" rather than a "consumer unit". Avoid vague slogans such as "comprehensive electrical solutions" and say in plain terms what you do, where and for whom. These tips on writing content when you're not a writer are a good place to start.

In English, Spanish or both

If you moved from the UK, some of your customers may be British expats who search in English, while others search in Spanish or Valencian. Content in both languages lets your business reach both. Local SEO: how customers find you on Google

Customers often find local tradespeople on Google in two places: on Google Maps and in the ordinary search results. Local SEO is what helps your business appear in both when someone nearby searches for an electrician. In short, it means making it obvious to Google what you do and where.

Local keywords: service plus location

Local searches often combine a service and a place: "electrician in Elche", "boletín eléctrico Valencia" or "electrician near me", and British customers may type the same search terms they used in the UK. Use those words naturally in your titles, headings and text, so you appear for these searches and not only for your brand name; to choose them, see how to choose local keywords.

Google Business Profile and the map pack

The map pack is the block of local businesses that Google often shows above the regular results for local searches. To appear there, start with a complete, verified listing with the key details:

  • The right primary category: electrician, not a generic term
  • Your service areas and opening times
  • Pictures of recent jobs
  • The same business name, address and phone number as on your website

The two support each other: Google Maps gets you seen, and your website gives people the detail they need. Read how to appear in Google's local results and optimise your profile.

Reviews and replies

Reviews help both your local visibility and the decision to call. Ask for a review after each visit, send the review link by WhatsApp and reply to every review, including critical ones (see how to respond to negative reviews). Don't offer discounts in exchange or ask only happy customers: Google's policies forbid both.

Rankings take time

No SEO provider can promise you a position on Google. Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, so you build visibility over months rather than days; see how to rank in local search.

Is a blog worth it?

Not at first: for local SEO, your main pages matter more. Once they're in place, short articles that answer what local customers search for, such as which EV charger suits a home garage or what a customer should check before rewiring an older flat, can bring extra visits and leads from Google search, give useful information and build trust in your business. Blog content is also a low-cost part of your digital marketing.

What it costs in Spain

In Spain, a professional website costs around €800 to €2,000 from a freelancer and €1,500 to €4,000 from an agency, before maintenance, according to Social Media Pymes (2026). Building it yourself costs less money but more time away from your business.

Mistakes that cost electricians calls

These problems turn visitors away without you noticing:

  • A number hidden in the footer, or one you can't tap on a mobile
  • No list of the towns you cover
  • Vague descriptions such as "all electrical work"
  • No reviews and no pictures
  • Out-of-date information: an old number or a service you no longer provide
  • A different phone or address on your pages and on Google Maps

Someone who needs a repair often opens several sites in a row and contacts the ones that seem trustworthy. If yours doesn't show how to call you, your area and what you do straight away, small details like these can lead potential customers to call someone else. How to get started

You don't need everything to be perfect before you launch:

  1. List the services you offer and the jobs you want more of.
  2. Decide which towns you cover.
  3. Have a few good pictures ready.
  4. Claim or complete your Google Business Profile, with consistent details.
  5. Ask your last few customers for a review.
  6. Choose how to build it: yourself, with a professional, or with a done-for-you service.
  7. Once it's live, count the calls and messages it brings each month.
