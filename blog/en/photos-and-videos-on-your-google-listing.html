Google Business Profile photos and videos: what to upload and why it matters

Google Business Profile photos can be the first thing people see when they compare local businesses on Google Maps. If you're a freelancer or run a small or medium-sized business in Spain, from Valencia and Alicante to Elche or Elda, this guide explains which images your listing (formerly Google My Business) needs, the official rules, what to do about customer uploads and which mistakes to avoid.

Why do Google Business Profile photos matter?

Because they let people check at a glance that your business exists and looks the way you describe it. Images help someone comparing listings understand what you do faster than any description.

Google My Business (GMB) is the former name of Google Business Profile (GBP); the free listing is the same, with your name, opening hours, ratings and a gallery.

What customers see before they get in touch

Think of someone looking for an electrician in Valencia or a hairdresser in Alicante. On the map, they compare you with your competitors: names, ratings and pictures. A well-kept gallery gives them more reasons to call you; a listing with only a logo leaves them relying on what others have posted.

What Google says about photos and local ranking

Google's local search advice says you can show customers what you offer and tell the story of your business with photos and videos. It also explains that local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence (how well known a business is), and that you can't pay for a better position.

Images aren't an SEO trick, then: they complete your listing and help you make the most of the visibility you already have. To improve the rest, see our tips to optimise your Google Business Profile and our Google Maps guide.

Which types of photos do you need?

Google separates your logo, your cover photo and business photos. Its tips then suggest shots of the outside, the interior, products, staff at work and the team, plus rooms, food and common areas where relevant:

Type What it shows Tip
Logo Your brand mark Same as on your quotes and van
Cover photo Top of your listing A sharp shot of what you do
Exterior Façade and entrance From the street, at different times of day
Interior Where you see customers Tidy, well lit, true to life
Products What you sell Popular items, evenly lit
At work You or your staff on the job One shot per service
Team The people behind the business With each person's consent
Rooms and common areas Bedrooms, terraces, waiting rooms As on an ordinary day

Logo and cover photo: your business identity

Your logo helps customers recognise you instantly, so use the same one everywhere. The cover image sits at the top of your listing: choose one that best represents what you do, such as your shopfront, your signwritten van or a finished job.

Google may still show another image first, such as a customer's, if it considers your cover low quality.

Exterior and interior: help people find the door

Photograph the building and its entrance as people see them when they arrive, from each direction they come from. If your business location is hard to spot, such as a unit with no shop window on an industrial estate in Elda, a clear shot of the door can reduce the calls you receive from lost visitors.

Inside, show the space where you see customers, tidy and well lit. Google's tips ask for images that truthfully show what it's like to stand inside your business.

Products, services and your team at work

Finished jobs show your services: a renovated bathroom in Valencia, a plumber fixing a leak or the new season's shoes in a shop in Elche. Show each service in shots that match your primary category (see how to choose your categories).

A photo of your team, or of you if you work alone, puts a human face on the business. Ask for permission before posting images of staff or customers: under the GDPR, a recognisable face is personal data.

What are Google's photo requirements?

Google accepts JPG or PNG photos of up to 5 MB, and its help page gives 720 × 720 pixels as the recommended resolution, not a minimum. Images that are too small or too heavy are rejected, so check the file size before uploading.

In focus, well lit and true to life

Google's quality rule asks for photos that are in focus and well lit, without significant alterations or excessive use of filters or AI: images that represent reality. Use daylight or good lighting, clean the lens and skip heavy filters that change the colours. Cropping doesn't change what a picture shows; turning a small workshop into a showroom with AI tools does.

Your own photos, not generic images

Google's tips for posting media to Maps ask you to upload relevant photos you've taken yourself at the location and to avoid screenshots, stock photos, collages and heavily edited images. Generic shots don't show your premises or your work. Leave prices and slogans off your images: to promote an offer, use a post.

Videos: rules and ideas

Videos can last up to 30 seconds and weigh up to 75 MB, at 720p or higher. A short clip shows what a still image can't: the atmosphere of the location and your services in action. Ideas to film:

  • A walk from the door to the counter or reception.
  • Your staff working on an everyday task, without posing.
  • A finished job: a fitted kitchen or an installation up and running.
  • A product in use rather than on a plain background.

Record in good light and keep the camera steady: Google's tips note that stable videos are more helpful than shaky or out-of-focus ones. Skip music and promotional text.

How to add photos and videos

It takes a few minutes on Google Search or Maps, signed in with the account that manages your Business Profile. Follow these steps:

  1. Look up your business's name and city.
  2. Choose Photos, then Add photos.
  3. For a logo or a cover photo, choose that option instead.
  4. Select one or more images or videos.

The Maps app on mobile devices works the same way.

What happens after you upload

Google checks each file against its content policy, so new uploads don't appear immediately. Each one shows a status:

  • Pending: still processing, or your profile isn't verified yet.
  • Not approved: it breaks a policy.
  • Live: visible to customers.

Nothing appears until you verify your profile, and it can take a day or two, so be patient before uploading again. If a file isn't accepted, fix the problem (blur or text) and try again.

To remove one of your own, select it in Photos and click Delete. Uploads by other users follow a different process.

What can you do about customer photos?

Any Google user can post photos on your listing, and you can't remove them yourself. You can flag content that breaks Google's content policy; Google examines each request and decides what to remove. How you respond depends on the photo:

  • Good and true to your business: leave it, it's the voice of someone who chose you.
  • Real but unflattering: don't flag it; upload recent shots of your own to balance the gallery.
  • Offensive, off-topic or showing personal information: report it once.
  • Next to a negative review: reply calmly (see our guide to handling negative feedback).

To get more images from customers, ask each one for feedback by WhatsApp or email after the job and suggest adding a picture, without offering anything in return (see our guide on getting more reviews).

How to flag an image

Open your listing on Maps, go to Photos, select the image and choose Report a problem. Pick the reason, such as a privacy concern, and submit. The review can take several business days, and sending several requests for the same image may slow it down.

How often should you upload new photos?

There's no required frequency. Your gallery should reflect your business as it is today, so upload images when there's something real to show and remove those that are out of date:

  • When you start: the basics of each type, without waiting for perfection.
  • After each job: the result, with the customer's permission if their home or face appears.
  • When something changes: a renovation, a new shopfront or seasonal products.
  • Every few months: update the gallery and remove old or duplicate images.

Photos in your posts

Posts on your profile (updates, offers and events) let you share fresh content with a photo and a button that can link to your site, giving visibility to your latest work (see our guide to publishing posts).

Mistakes and myths about GBP photos

These mistakes cost you trust:

  • Generic images bought online or generated by AI instead of your own premises.
  • Dark or blurry shots, or filters that change the colours.
  • Prices or slogans written on top of your images.
  • Images of premises or staff that no longer exist.
  • Recognisable customers shown without their permission.

Myths about photos and local SEO

Several popular SEO tips find no support in Google's own advice:

  • “Geotagged photos send local SEO signals.” Google's local ranking advice doesn't mention geotags.
  • “Keyword file names boost your SEO.” Descriptive names keep your archive organised; Google's advice doesn't mention them either.
  • “Daily uploads push you up the Map Pack.” No frequency is set; our guide to the Map Pack explains what does count.
  • “You need a minimum number of photos.” None is required.

Photos are one part of your local SEO, alongside categories, ratings and consistent contact details: our local SEO guide puts them in order.

Keep your listing consistent everywhere

Use the same name, location and phone number on your Google Business Profile, your site and your social media (the NAP of local citations), and share the same photos across them.

You don't need a photographer: your mobile, good light and a little order are enough. Keep your gallery up to date with real photos and the people who find you will have good reasons to trust you, though no one can promise you a top position on Google.

Frequently asked questions

How many photos should my Google My Business listing have?
There's no required number. What matters is a complete gallery of real images: logo, cover, premises, work or products, and team. After that, add new ones when there's something to show.
What size and format should Google Business Profile photos be?
JPG or PNG files of up to 5 MB, with a recommended resolution of 720 × 720 pixels; images that are too small or too heavy are rejected.
Can I upload videos to my Business Profile?
Yes, videos of up to 30 seconds and 75 MB, at 720p or higher, to show your premises or your staff at work.
Can customers add photos to my listing, and can I delete them?
Any user can add photos to your listing, but you can't remove them. You can flag those that break the platform's policies, and Google decides after a review.
Do photos help my business rank higher on Google Maps?
Google recommends showing what you offer with photos and videos, but local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence. Photos complete your listing; they're no SEO shortcut.
Can I use stock photos or AI images on my listing?
It's best not to. Google asks for photos that represent reality and advises against heavy edits or images you didn't take yourself; images made with AI don't show your real premises either.
