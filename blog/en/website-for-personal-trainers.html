Many people look up a fitness coach online before they book a first session, and your website has to show them, in seconds, who you help, how you work and how to start. This guide covers what your website should include, website design tips, costs and mistakes, with examples for personal trainers in Valencia, Alicante or Elche.

Why do personal trainers need a website?

Personal trainers need a website because it is the one place they control: it presents their services, pricing and experience, appears in search results and answers questions while they focus on their sessions. Many fitness businesses use Instagram to attract followers and their site to convert them into clients.

Things people usually want to know

  • who you work with, such as beginners or runners;
  • which service fits them: one-to-one sessions, online coaching or group fitness classes;
  • where you train: a gym, a park or at home;
  • your pricing and how to book a first session.

What should a personal trainer website include?

You need a homepage, an about page, your services and pricing, a contact page and the legal texts. You don't need dozens of pages: a clear structure makes everything easy to find in one or two clicks.

Homepage

Be specific: say who you help, where you train and how to start, with a strong headline and one clear button that helps convert visitors. The flow then continues with your services and a few lines about you.

About page

List only the qualifications you can prove, for example a degree in sports science (Ciencias de la Actividad Física y del Deporte), a vocational qualification (ciclo formativo) or one obtained in the UK: rules on sports professions vary between regions. See building your personal brand.

Services and pricing

Give each service its own section: one-to-one training, online coaching, group classes or monthly plans, with what each includes and your class times. Clear pricing avoids back-and-forth; if you'd rather not publish it, explain how to ask.

Getting in touch

Show your phone number, a WhatsApp button and a short booking form that asks only for a name, an email address or phone number, and the type of training and fitness goals they have in mind. If you use a booking system, link to it. It's better to leave health questions for the first session: health data is sensitive.

Personal trainer website design: examples and tips

Good design for a PT website is about being clear, not effects.

A strong first screen

The first screen typically combines a photo, a headline and a button. For example: “Strength training for beginners in Elche, at the gym or by video call”, with “Book your first session”. Visitors should know in seconds whether you're the right coach.

Your own photos rather than stock images

Stock images appear on many other sites; pictures of you coaching or of your fitness studio show what a session actually looks like. Ask clients for written permission before they appear in photos. With WebAutonomos, we use the pictures you send us and those on your Google Business Profile.

Colours, fonts and mobile

Two or three colours, readable fonts and white space give your fitness website a consistent look. Make sure it's built for mobile, as many potential clients will look you up on their phone, and that pages load fast (see website speed and search rankings).

Marketing: local SEO and social media

Searches in your town

For SEO, put your town and services in your page titles and texts, using the searches people actually type: “best personal trainer in Valencia”, “trainer near me” or “English-speaking PT in Alicante”. Use the same name, address and phone number everywhere, including your Google Business Profile (step-by-step article). Social media and newsletters

Link your site from your Instagram bio; a free download, such as a beginner's workout plan, can act as a lead magnet (see our email marketing guide).

Trust, promises and legal texts

Testimonials with permission

Real testimonials and reviews help you build trust. Publish them only with each client's written permission, link to your Google reviews (see how to get more Google reviews) and, in any case, never invent or edit one.

No promises of results

Describe what you do, not what each client will achieve: no promises of weight loss, health improvements or a body transformation, and no diet plans unless you're qualified.

Legal texts

Your site collects contact details, so you need a privacy policy (GDPR), a legal notice (aviso legal) required by Spain's LSSI and, if you use analytics, a cookie policy. WebAutonomos includes these legal texts.

Common website mistakes personal trainers make

  • relying only on Instagram;
  • no pricing and no clear way to book;
  • speaking to everyone instead of specific clients;
  • generic images instead of pictures of you;
  • promising results;
  • a fitness website that is hard to use on a phone.
