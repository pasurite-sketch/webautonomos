Websites for coaches: how to get clients with a professional site

As a coach, your product is trust. Before hiring you, your potential clients look you up on Google, read your story and decide whether you're the right person to guide them. If you don't have a professional website, that decision is made without you. In this guide we explain why a coach needs their own website, what it should include and how to turn a visitor into your next client.

Why a coach needs their own website (and Instagram isn't enough)

Social media is rented ground: you don't control the algorithm, your followers or the rules. Your website, on the other hand, is yours. It's the only space where you can explain your full method, show your results and guide the visitor towards a specific action without distractions. Instagram gives you visibility; your website is what turns that visibility into clients.

Trust is your product: how to convey it

A client doesn't buy coaching sessions, they buy a transformation and they need to believe you can help them achieve it. Your website should convey that trust with an honest, warm 'About me' page, real client testimonials, your training and certifications, and a clear explanation of your method. The more specific you are about who you help and how, the more trust you build.

What a coach's website should include

The essentials: a clear presentation of who you are and who you help, your programmes or services with what each one includes, client testimonials, a resources or blog section that shows your expertise, and several easy ways to get in touch (form, WhatsApp and, if you work by appointment, a booking button). All designed so the visitor knows within 10 seconds whether you're right for them.

In person, online or hybrid? How it affects your website

If you work in person in your city, your website should reinforce local SEO: mention your area, show up for searches like 'coach in your city' and look after your Google listing. If you work online for all of Spain or the Spanish-speaking world, the focus changes: you compete for terms like 'online life coach' and your website has to work just as well for someone who finds you 500 kilometres away. Defining your model first avoids building the wrong website.

From visitor to first session: getting clients

The most common mistake is having a beautiful website that invites no action. Every page should lead to something: booking a free discovery session, downloading a guide in exchange for an email, or messaging you on WhatsApp. A clear, repeated call to action converts far more than a form hidden at the bottom. Make the first step easy and un-intimidating.

Common mistakes coaches make with their website

The most frequent ones: trying to speak to everyone and connecting with no one, filling the site with text and no clear action, copying other coaches' generic messaging instead of showing your own voice, and not updating testimonials or resources. A clear, specific, living website is worth more than a perfect but cold one.
