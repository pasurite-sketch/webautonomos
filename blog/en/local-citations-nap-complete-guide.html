Local Citations and NAP: What They Are and Why They Matter for SEO

If you're working on local SEO and haven't heard about local NAP citations, you're missing one of the most important signals Google uses to rank businesses in search results. NAP stands for Name, Address, Phone, and the consistency of this data across the internet is crucial for Google to trust your business. In this guide, we explain what local SEO citations are, where to create them, and how to keep them updated, with practical examples for freelancers and small businesses in Spain's Comunidad Valenciana.

What are local NAP citations and how do they work

A local citation is any online mention of your business data: name, address, and phone number. There are structured citations (your NAP in a directory like Yelp or Yellow Pages) and unstructured citations (mentions in a press article or blog). Google crawls all this information and cross-references it with your Google Business Profile. If all the data matches, Google gains confidence that your business is real and legitimate.

Why NAP consistency is vital for your local SEO

According to BrightLocal's study, citation signals represent approximately 7% of Map Pack ranking factors. In competitive sectors, that 7% can be the difference between appearing in the top 3 or being left out. If your business name varies between platforms, the address changes, or you mix landline and mobile numbers, Google sees potentially different entities and reduces its trust.

The best Spanish directories for creating local citations

Priority directories for freelancers in Spain include: Google Business Profile, Paginas Amarillas (Yellow Pages), QDQ, Yelp Spain, Cylex, Europages, Hotfrog, 11870.com, Vulka, and Infoisinfo. Additionally, look for industry-specific directories like Habitissimo (home renovation) or Doctoralia (healthcare), and municipal portals from local city councils.

How to create and maintain your local citations step by step

Step 1: Define your official NAP in a document and copy it verbatim every time you register. Step 2: Create profiles in priority directories filling in all possible information. Step 3: Audit your existing citations by searching your business name and phone number in Google with quotes. If you find outdated or incorrect data, contact each platform to update it.

NAP errors that are holding back your ranking

The most common errors are: using different business names across platforms, mixing landline and mobile numbers, inconsistent address format, not including the postal code, having duplicate citations in the same directory, and not keeping your URL consistent. Create a spreadsheet with all directories where you're registered to audit your citations every 6 months.

Local citations are a fundamental piece of your local SEO strategy. It's not about being in many directories, but about your NAP being exactly the same in all of them. Start with the top 5 directories, gradually add the rest, and audit them periodically. Frequently asked questions

What does NAP mean in local SEO?
NAP stands for Name, Address, Phone. These are the three basic details of your business, and they must appear identically on every online platform where you are listed: your website, Google Business Profile, directories such as Páginas Amarillas, social media and anywhere else. NAP consistency is an important local ranking factor.
How many local citations does my business need?
For a freelancer or a small local business, between 20 and 40 quality local citations are enough to compete in most sectors. The quality and consistency of the citations matter more than the number. Start with the ten main Spanish directories, then add ones specific to your sector. You do not need to be in 200 directories; you need to be correct in the ones that count.
What happens if my NAP is not consistent across platforms?
If Google finds different details for your business on different platforms — a different phone number in Páginas Amarillas and on your website, say — it loses confidence in your information and may lower your local ranking. Customers can also get confused and call the wrong number. Find and fix any inconsistency as soon as you can.
Do local citations still matter in 2026?
Yes, local citations remain a relevant local SEO factor in 2026, although their relative weight has fallen compared with reviews and content. According to the BrightLocal study, citations account for roughly 7% of local ranking factors. They are not the leading factor, but they are still needed as a signal that your business is legitimate and present online.
Can I create local citations myself, or do I need a professional?
You can do it perfectly well yourself. Most directories allow free registration and the process is simple: create an account, fill in your business details and verify the profile. What matters most is being meticulous about NAP consistency. Set aside an hour a week for a month and you will cover the 20 to 30 main directories without difficulty.
