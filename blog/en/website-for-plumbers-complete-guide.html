When a pipe bursts in Valencia or a boiler breaks down in Alicante, homeowners grab their phone and search. What they find in the next few seconds decides whether they call your plumbing business or a competitor. This guide explains what to include on a plumbing website, page by page, with practical tips: your services, the towns you cover, contact options, photos and testimonials, design, local SEO and the legal texts required in Spain. It's for plumbers and heating engineers who work in English, Spanish or both, in Valencia, on the Costa Blanca or elsewhere in Spain.

What should a plumbing website include?

At a minimum, a plumbing website should include a clear homepage, a page for each core service, the towns you cover, a phone number and a WhatsApp button, a short contact form, real photos of your work, genuine customer reviews and the legal texts required in Spain. A blog, location pages and an FAQ can come later.

Word of mouth is still great, but even recommended customers often check a plumbing company online before they call. Before building anything, think about the person who will land on your website. A potential customer with a leak or a broken boiler wants four answers in a few seconds:

  • Do you cover my town?
  • Can you deal with my problem, and how soon?
  • How do I get in touch?
  • Can I trust you in my home?

If your site doesn't answer those questions quickly, they go back to the search results and choose someone else.

Emergency repairs and planned projects

Not every visitor is in the same situation. Someone who types "emergency plumber Valencia" has a problem right now and will often call the first company that looks reliable and available. Someone searching for a new boiler in Elche or planning a bathroom renovation in Elda takes more time: they compare quotes, read testimonials and look at photos of previous jobs before deciding.

Your site has to serve both: a call button one tap away for anyone in a hurry, and detailed information about your services and your process for those who are comparing.

Your homepage: what visitors should see first

Your homepage is often the first page a potential customer sees. It has one job: to show in a few seconds that you're the right plumbing business for them, while making it easy to get in touch.

What to show before anyone scrolls

  • your business name and logo, the same as on your van;
  • one line that says what you do and where, such as "Plumbing repairs, boiler servicing and bathroom installations in Elche";
  • a call button and a WhatsApp button;
  • your main services, each linked to its own page;
  • your average rating, or two or three short testimonials.

Keep it simple

Many plumbing websites open with a slider of stock images and a long welcome message; you need neither. Short, specific headings, clear buttons and a few great photos of real installations are more effective than a clever slogan. Give visitors the key information first, then lead them to the next step: call, send a message or read more about a service.

Plumbing services: give each one its own page

Your plumbing services are the core of what you offer. The more specific they are, the easier it is for potential customers to recognise their problem and for a search engine to show your pages for relevant searches. It's better to describe your main services well than to list twenty of them in one line.

Group your plumbing services by customer

  • Homeowners: leak repairs, blocked drains, taps, cisterns, water heaters and boilers.
  • Installations: bathrooms, kitchens, pipework and boiler replacements.
  • Residents' associations (comunidades de propietarios): upkeep of downpipes and the building's shared pipework, or a regular maintenance plan.
  • Shops, bars and offices: plumbing repairs and installations for commercial clients.

Give each main service a dedicated page that explains what it involves, who it's for and how to book it, with content about that specific problem.

A plumbing company in Elche, for example, could separate its services for homes from those for residents' associations and shops, so everyone finds their case at once. Bathroom renovations deserve their own page, because customers who plan one take time to compare and want to see examples.

Emergency call-outs: promise only what you can deliver

Emergency plumbing needs its own page, or a clearly marked block that's visible straight away, with your phone in view. Say which problems you deal with, such as burst pipes, leaks or blocked drains, and when you can come out. It's important to be honest here: if your business offers weekend call-outs, say so, and if you don't go out at night or on public holidays, say that too. People who know what to expect call with more confidence, and you avoid calls you can't take.

Only advertise the work you're authorised to do

Plumbing on its own is lightly regulated in Spain, but gas, heating, hot water and air-conditioning installations must be carried out by an authorised installer company (empresa instaladora habilitada). There's no national plumbing licence, although some regions have their own rules, so check with your region's industry department. Describe your authorisations accurately and leave out anything you aren't allowed to do. You'll find a summary of the Spanish rules for plumbing and heating businesses on our website.

The towns you cover

Potential customers looking for a plumbing service want to know at once whether you come to their home. Being clear about where you work avoids calls from places you don't cover and attracts the right ones.

Name each town

Don't stop at "the whole province". List the towns and villages you cover and, if the list is long, group them by district. In a big city such as Valencia, name the neighbourhoods; for a small town, its name is enough. Customers can check at a glance, and searches that include their town's name are more likely to reach your site.

Example: a plumbing business based in Elche

Imagine a plumbing business based in Elche that also covers Elda and Alicante. Its areas page can name each town, mention the services available there, explain where the van sets off from and say whether there's a travel charge outside the usual area. Another, based in Valencia, might focus on the city and its metropolitan area. What matters is that your website tells the truth about where you can get to.

Location pages: useful, not copied

For a growing plumbing business, a separate web page for each town can help, as long as each one adds something relevant: local information, recent installations in that town, how you organise visits there. Copying the same content and changing only the town's name helps neither visitors nor your SEO.

About page: introduce yourself and your team

Letting a stranger into your home is a question of trust, so people want to know who you are before they call. Use your about page to share:

  • your name, a photo of you or your team, and your van;
  • your experience in the plumbing industry and the kind of jobs you take on;
  • your authorisations, if you do gas or heating installations;
  • the languages you speak with clients;
  • your process: first contact, visit, quote, the repair itself and tidying up afterwards.

If you work alone, say so: many customers like dealing with the same person from the first call to the end of the job. If you have a team, introduce each member with a photo and a line, so people know who might knock on their door.

In English, Spanish or both

Some of your customers may be British expats who search in English, while others use Spanish or Valencian. If you work in both languages, your website should too, with content properly adapted for each group of readers. Photos, reviews and testimonials

Real photos and genuine feedback from customers build trust: they show the quality of your work better than any slogan and help a potential customer choose you over an unknown company.

Real photos of finished installations

Use your own photos: a replaced boiler, a renovated bathroom, a neat repair under a sink. Stock images are easy to spot and make a business look less real. Choose sharp, well-lit shots, add a short caption saying what you did and in which town, and check that no faces, door numbers or other personal details appear unless the client agrees. There are more tips in our article on photos and videos for your listing.

How to show customer feedback

Show your rating and a few genuine testimonials, and link to your Google reviews so visitors can read the rest. Never invent or buy reviews: fake ones mislead customers and are prohibited by law. If you publish testimonials on your own site, EU consumer rules, which apply in Spain, say you must explain whether and how you check that they come from real customers.

Ask for a review after every job

Ask every client for a review once the repair is finished, not only the ones who seemed pleased, and share the review link by WhatsApp while the visit is fresh in their mind. Reply politely to every review, including critical ones, because other visitors read your replies too. Each new review also helps your local visibility. Don't offer discounts in exchange: Google's policies forbid it. Read how to get more Google reviews and how to answer negative ones.

Contact information and quote requests

Every obstacle between a visitor and the call is a chance for them to leave, so getting in touch must be instant, especially on a mobile.

Phone and WhatsApp in one tap

Put your phone number in the header as a click-to-call link, so one tap starts the call. WhatsApp is a great option for clients who would rather write or want to send a photo of the problem, so add a button for it. Repeat your contact details in the footer of every page. If you haven't set it up yet, here's how to set up WhatsApp Business for free.

A short contact form

A short web form is easier to fill in than a long one, and a few fields are enough for you to reply or book a visit:

  • name and phone;
  • town;
  • type of problem or project;
  • a box to describe the request.

Answer quickly, and say on your contact page how soon you usually reply. If you already use an online booking system for routine visits such as boiler servicing, add a link to it next to your number; see how to choose an online booking system.

Quotes and prices

Explain your quote process: whether you visit first, whether the visit has a cost and whether you provide a written quote. In the Comunitat Valenciana, homeowners are entitled to a detailed written quote before work starts, unless they waive it in their own handwriting. You can show indicative prices or explain how you charge (per hour, per project or after a visit); if your prices are aimed at homeowners, they must include VAT (IVA).

Answer common questions before they call

An effective FAQ covers what customers want to know every week: whether the first visit costs anything, which towns you cover, whether you take card payments, how long a typical repair takes. It saves time on the phone, which means the customers who do call already understand what your business offers.

Website design that brings in calls

Your site doesn't need to be spectacular. Good web design for a plumbing business is about conversion, turning visitors into enquiries: a clean design that loads fast, reads well on a small screen and leads to a call or a message.

Design for the phone first

Many of your customers will search for you on their mobile, often in a hurry, so start with a site built for the small screen:

  • a call button at the top of every page;
  • a WhatsApp button that stays in view;
  • large buttons, short paragraphs and readable text;
  • one clear call to action in each section, such as "Call now" or "Ask for a quote".

Then open your site on your own phone, on mobile data rather than Wi-Fi, and check that it loads straight away, that your number is readable without zooming and that every button works.

Keep it light and fast

Compress your photos before uploading them, avoid heavy sliders, autoplay videos and pop-ups, and keep the menu short. A simple design loads sooner and is easier to understand. You can test your speed with PageSpeed Insights, a free tool from Google, and here's how speed affects your ranking.

Write for customers, not for other plumbers

Use the words your customers use: they type "blocked drain" or "leaking tap", not technical terms. Avoid industry jargon and vague slogans such as "comprehensive plumbing solutions", and say in plain terms what you do, where and for whom. Local SEO: how customers find your plumbing business

Local SEO (search engine optimisation for local searches) helps your plumbing business appear when someone nearby looks for a service you offer, both on the map and in the ordinary search results. In short, it shows search engines what you do and where.

Service and town keywords

Think about how customers search when they need you: "plumber in Elche", "blocked drain Alicante", "boiler repair Valencia" or simply "plumber near me". British customers may type the same search terms they used in the UK, while Spanish customers look for "fontanero" and the name of their town. Use those combinations naturally in your page titles, headings and text, without repeating them artificially. To pick them, see our guide on how to choose local keywords.

A complete Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is the listing that appears next to the map in local results. Complete it with the right primary category, your services, opening hours, phone, photos and, if you work at customers' homes, your service area. Your listing and your website support each other: the map gets you seen, and your site gives people the information they need. Then read how to optimise your listing.

The same contact details everywhere

Your business name, address and phone number should be identical on your website, your listing and any directory where you appear. This is important for local businesses, because differences can confuse search engines and customers alike. Our guide to local citations and NAP explains how to keep them aligned.

Useful content and a blog

Once your main pages are in place, a blog is a great way to answer real questions customers have, for example:

  • tips for looking after boilers and water heaters;
  • quick tips on saving water at home;
  • what to do about a leak until help arrives;
  • how to tell whether a boiler needs a service.

This kind of useful, relevant content gives customers more reasons to visit and can bring extra visitors.

Legal pages a plumbing website needs in Spain

A business website in Spain needs a few legal texts. They aren't decoration: they inform visitors and protect you.

  • Legal notice (aviso legal): your name or company name, your NIF (or NIE if you're a foreign national), your address, an email and another direct way to contact you, plus your Registro Mercantil information if you trade as a limited company (S.L.).
  • Privacy policy: what personal data you collect through the site or WhatsApp, what you use it for and how customers can exercise their rights.
  • Cookies: if you use analytics or advertising cookies, visitors must be able to accept or reject them.
  • Prices: if you show them to homeowners, they must include VAT.

Common mistakes that cost plumbing businesses calls

Many plumbing websites lose calls for the same few reasons:

  • Hiding your number in the footer or behind a menu.
  • Not saying which towns you cover.
  • Vague services, with no difference between emergencies and planned repairs.
  • Stock photos instead of your own installations.
  • Invented testimonials, or a rating nobody can check on your listing.
  • Forms with too many fields.
  • A slow site on a mobile.
  • Different information on your website and your listing.
  • Missing legal texts, or texts copied from another business.
  • Not answering messages.

Fixing these doesn't mean starting again: start with your phone number, your towns and your photos.
