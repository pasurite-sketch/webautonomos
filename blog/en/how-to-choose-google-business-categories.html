Google Business Profile categories tell Google what kind of business you run and shape the searches your profile can appear in. Choosing the wrong one is like hanging a bakery sign over your car repair shop: people looking for what you really do won't find you. If you're a freelancer or run a small business anywhere in Spain, from Valencia and Alicante to Elche or Elda, this guide explains how to pick your primary and secondary categories, with examples, common mistakes and a simple way to check the results.

What are Google Business Profile categories?

Categories are labels from a predefined list that describe what your business is, such as Plumber, Electrician or Cafe. You choose one primary category and up to nine additional ones, and Google uses them to judge which searches your profile is relevant to.

Google Business Profile, formerly Google My Business, is the free tool that lets a local business appear on Google Search and Maps. You can't type a category freely: you select it from Google's business categories, which run to thousands and change from time to time.

Why categories matter in local search

Google's help pages explain that local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. Your category is an important relevance signal you control: it's the piece of data that tells Google plainly what type of business you are.

When someone in Valencia types "plumber near me", Google compares that query with local listings nearby. If you've picked Plumber, you're a clear match; with a general option such as Contractor, Google has less reason to show you.

Features that depend on your category

Your category also changes the features available on your profile. Google's help pages give examples like these:

  • Restaurants: links for online orders, reservations and a menu.
  • Health and beauty businesses: a booking button.
  • Hotels: a list of amenities.
  • Many trades: suggested services that match their category.

So a wrong category can also hide tools your customers expect to see.

Primary and additional categories: what's the difference?

Your primary category sums up your core business: it's shown on your listing, just below your business name, and it carries more weight than the others.

Additional categories, often called secondary categories, support it with other services or departments, and they're generally not shown on the listing.

For example, an electrician in Alicante who also installs air conditioning is still an electrician first, so Electrician leads and an air conditioning option can go in as an extra.

How to choose your primary category in four steps

These steps apply to a new listing and to one you already have.

Step one: describe your business in one sentence

Write down what you do and where, the way a customer would say it: "I fix leaks and blocked pipes in Valencia."

Google's guidelines suggest picking categories that complete the sentence "this business is a…" rather than "this business has a…". A plumber is a plumber; a car park or free Wi-Fi is something a business has.

Step two: search the options Google offers

Start typing in the category box and Google suggests matching options. Try your trade, your star service and a synonym or two, then pick the most specific option that still represents your core business: Google itself recommends Nail salon rather than the broader Salon for a nail bar.

Step three: research the competitors who rank near you

Search for your trade and town on Google Maps, such as "carpenter Elda", and look closely at the listings that appear first: the category under each name, the services they highlight and the words customers repeat in their reviews.

Their secondary categories aren't visible, but SEO tools can show them: GMB Everywhere, a Chrome extension, displays the primary and secondary categories beside the profiles on Google Maps. A shared category is a good clue, but keep only what matches your own business.

Step four: pick the option that fits best

Between a general option and a precise one, go for the precise one if it matches what you do day to day. An electrician who chooses Contractor competes in a vague category, while Electrician matches what people type.

Put simply, select the category that best describes your business as a whole, not the side job that pays best.

How many categories do you need?

As few as possible: Google's guidelines ask you to describe your core business with the smallest number of categories, so there's no target number to reach.

Before selecting one, ask yourself: if a customer found me through this category tomorrow, could I do the job?

  • Add it if it's a real service you provide regularly.
  • Skip it if it's a product or a feature, such as parking or Wi-Fi: that information belongs in the attributes and other sections of your profile.
  • Skip it if it's a broader version of your primary one: Google explains that a Pizza restaurant may also appear for people looking for restaurants or Italian restaurants, so adding Restaurant is redundant.
  • Skip it if it belongs to another business, such as the shopping centre where your shop is.

Departments and separate businesses at one location

Google's help uses the example of a grocery store with its own bakery and deli counter: the grocery category goes first, and the other two can be added as extras.

A health club with an independently operated cafe, by contrast, shouldn't add Cafe, because that cafe is a separate business that can have its own profile.

Is there a full list of Google Business Profile categories?

Not an official one. Google doesn't give a total, there are thousands of categories, and options are added and removed regularly. The place to confirm a category is the drop-down list in your own profile.

Several SEO resources keep their own lists of Google business categories. PlePer, for instance, publishes a searchable list you can filter by language and country.

Such lists are handy for ideas, but names change, so check the drop-down before relying on one.

Do you need Spanish category names?

No. Categories work across languages, and Google generally shows each one in the user's language: if you manage your profile in English and select Plumber, Google users who browse in Spanish should see the Spanish name, Fontanero. That's important for English speakers in Spain, because you can work in the language you prefer.

Common mistakes with Google Business Profile categories

These mistakes come up often among local businesses and are easy to avoid.

Adding too many categories

Ticking every option that looks close to your trade doesn't improve your ranking. It blurs the picture Google has of your business and brings calls for jobs you don't do.

Choosing a category that doesn't fit

A locksmith who picks a broad security or key-cutting option instead of Locksmith competes poorly when people look for a locksmith. Go for the word your customers would type.

Putting keywords in your business name

Adding extras such as "cheap emergency plumber Valencia" to your business name is an old SEO trick that goes against Google's guidelines and can get your profile suspended.

Use the name on your signage and invoices, and let your category and services do the describing.

Never looking at them again

Your business changes, and so do the options Google offers. Check now and then that your categories are still accurate, for example when you grow into a new service, and make an update if a closer option appears.

How to change your business category

You can update your Google Business Profile category at any time from Edit profile, in the Business category section. The change can take a while to appear, and Google may ask you to verify your business again.

  1. Sign in to the Google account that manages your Google Business Profile and open it on Google Search or Maps.
  2. Select Edit profile.
  3. Next to Business category, click or tap the edit icon.
  4. Type in the Primary category box and pick an option from the drop-down list.
  5. For extra categories, use Add another category; to remove one, use the remove icon next to it.
  6. Save your changes, then check a few days later that they have been published.

What can happen after a change

  • Google may review the change before publishing it.
  • You might be asked to verify your business again.
  • Your visibility in local search can go up and down for a while.
  • Features linked to the old category, such as a menu or a booking button, can disappear, and new ones can appear.

So decide calmly: it's important not to change them every few weeks.

The rest of your profile matters too

Your category opens the door, but local SEO depends on all the information on your profile. Complete, accurate details improve your local visibility and help people choose you over the business down the road.

  • Services: Google suggests some based on your category; fill in the ones you offer and describe them in your own words.
  • Products: if you sell or install something specific, show it with a photo and a short description.
  • Attributes: details such as wheelchair access or Wi-Fi; the ones available depend on your category.
  • Reviews: ask satisfied customers for a review and reply to every one.
  • Photos and posts: upload real photos of your jobs and post news or offers regularly.
  • Hours and contact details: important information such as opening hours, phone number and links to your social media, kept up to date.

How to check the results

Give any change a few weeks, then open the Performance section of your profile. Its data shows the searches people used to find you, along with calls, website clicks and direction requests.

Then look up what you do and where from your phone, as a customer would, and note where you appear in the local search results. More visibility doesn't always mean more calls: if the enquiries don't match what you offer, go back to the steps in this guide.

Frequently asked questions

How many categories can I set on Google Business Profile?
One primary category and up to nine additional ones. You don't have to use them all: Google recommends using as few categories as possible, and each one should match a service your business provides regularly.
Can I change my primary category after creating the listing?
Yes. Open your Google Business Profile, go to Edit profile and change it in the Business category section. Google may review the change and ask you to verify your business again, it can take a while to appear, and your visibility may vary in the meantime, so avoid changing it often.
How do I find out which category my competitors use?
Open your competitors' profiles on Google: the main category appears just below each business name. The others are generally not shown, but browser extensions such as GMB Everywhere can display them.
Can I create a custom category if I cannot find mine?
No. Google doesn't allow custom categories: you can only pick from its predefined business categories, which run to thousands. If you can't find your exact activity, choose the closest one, even if it's broader, and put the missing information in your description and services.
Do secondary categories affect my local ranking?
Yes, although they carry less weight than the primary one. Well chosen, they help Google connect your profile with related services you offer; for example, a plumber who also repairs water heaters can add a related option if it's a regular part of the job.
Are categories the same as SEO keywords?
No. Categories are fixed options that describe what type of business you are; keywords are the words people type when they look for you. They should match, but keywords belong in your description, your services and your website, never in your business name.
