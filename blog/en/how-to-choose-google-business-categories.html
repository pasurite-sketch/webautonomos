Google Business Categories: How to Choose the Right Ones for Your Business

Google Business categories are one of the biggest factors determining whether your business shows up on Google Maps when a customer searches for you. Choosing the wrong category is like putting a "bakery" sign on your car repair shop -- Google won't show you to the right people. If you're a freelancer or small business owner in Spain's Comunidad Valenciana, this article teaches you how to strategically choose your primary and secondary categories so you appear in exactly the searches that matter.

What are Google Business categories and why do they matter so much

According to a BrightLocal study, Google Business categories are the number one factor determining which Map Pack searches you appear in -- above reviews, geographic proximity, and website content. Google has over 4,000 predefined categories, and an electrician in Alicante who chooses "Contractor" instead of "Electrician" is competing in a generic category where Google doesn't know exactly what they do.

Primary vs. secondary categories: the difference that changes your visibility

Google lets you choose 1 primary category and up to 9 secondary categories. Your primary category carries the most weight in the local search algorithm and is the one visible on your listing. The golden rule: it should describe your main activity, not everything you do. Secondary categories aren't publicly shown but Google uses them internally to decide whether to show you in certain searches.

How to choose the perfect primary category for your business

Follow these 3 steps: First, identify your main activity in one phrase. Second, search for your category in the Google Business Profile panel trying different keywords. Third, analyze what your best-positioned competitors use by searching on Google Maps. If the top 3 results all use "Plumber," that's almost certainly the category you should use too.

Secondary categories: strategy to cover more searches

The goal is to cover the maximum number of relevant searches without diluting your profile. For example, an electrician in Valencia could use "Electrician" as primary and "Electrical repair service" and "Security system installation company" as secondary. Don't use all 9 categories just because you can -- 3 to 5 well-chosen ones work better than 9 barely relevant ones.

Common mistakes when choosing categories and how to avoid them

The most common mistakes are: choosing a category that's too generic, confusing primary with secondary, adding categories for services you don't offer, not reviewing categories periodically, and ignoring what competitors use. A real case: Antonio, a locksmith in Valencia, switched from "Lock service" to "Locksmith" and went from position 8 to position 3 in the Map Pack in just three weeks.

Google Business categories are a fundamental piece of your local visibility. Choose your primary category precisely, add strategic secondary ones, and review them every 6 months. Frequently asked questions

How many categories can I set on Google Business Profile?
Google lets you select one primary category and up to nine secondary ones, ten in total. Using all ten is not advisable unless they are all genuinely relevant to your business. The ideal is to choose between three and five categories that really describe what you do.
Can I change my primary category after creating the listing?
Yes, you can change the primary category at any time from your Google Business Profile dashboard. Go to Edit profile, then Business category, and change it. The change can take a few days to appear in Google Maps. It is best not to change it too often, as it can affect your ranking temporarily.
How do I find out which category my competitors use?
Search for your competitor in Google Maps, click their listing and look just below the business name: that is where the primary category appears. To see their secondary categories you can use free tools such as the Chrome extension GMB Everywhere, or look them up in PlePer. That will give you ideas about which categories work in your sector and area.
Can I create a custom category if I cannot find mine?
No, Google does not allow custom categories. You can only choose from its predefined list, which runs to more than 4,000 options. If you cannot find your exact category, choose the one closest to your main activity. If you install automatic doors, for instance, you might use Door supplier or Door repair service.
Do secondary categories affect my Google Maps ranking?
Yes, secondary categories influence your visibility, though less than the primary one. Each secondary category lets you appear in additional related searches. If your primary category is Plumber and you add Water heater repair service, you can appear when someone searches for water heater repair in your area. Only add categories for services you genuinely offer, though.
