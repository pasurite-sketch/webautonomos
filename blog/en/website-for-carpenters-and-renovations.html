Joinery website design is not about flashy effects. Done well, it shows your work clearly, explains what you make and install, and makes it easy for local customers to ask for a quote. Whether you are a carpenter or joiner building bespoke furniture in Alicante or you run a renovation company in Valencia, this guide covers the pages your website needs, how to present your portfolio, what to write, how local SEO works and how to get started.

What makes good joinery website design?

Strong joinery website design puts your finished work first, says clearly which services you offer and where, and gives visitors a quick way to get in touch from any device. Colours, fonts and layout should support those three jobs, whether your style is modern or traditional.

Joiners, carpenters and carpinteros

In Britain, joiners usually make doors, windows, stairs and fitted furniture in a workshop, while carpenters install and repair timber on site. In Spain, both trades are called carpintero, and many workshops do both. Choose your words with your audience in mind: Spanish speakers will type carpintero or carpintería into Google, while English speakers on the Costa Blanca may try joiner, carpenter or kitchen fitter. A website in both languages lets you reach both groups.

What a customer checks before calling

Before they ring you, potential customers looking for a joiner online want quick answers to a few questions:

  • Do you make or install what I need? Bespoke kitchens, built-in wardrobes, doors, stairs or a full renovation.
  • Do you work in my town? The places you cover should be easy to find.
  • Is your work well made? A portfolio of real photos of finished jobs answers that better than any slogan.
  • Can I trust you? Reviews, your experience and a real address all help.
  • How do I reach you? A visible phone number and a WhatsApp button.

The basic elements of any professional website apply to you too (see what a small business website should include); this guide adds what is specific to joinery and renovation.

Instagram and Facebook can attract attention to your latest jobs, and some customers will check them too, but the platform decides who sees each post. Your website is the base of your online presence: it gathers your services, the towns you cover and your reviews in one place, it can appear in search results and you can link your social media profiles from it. Which pages does a joinery website need?

Five pages are enough to start: home, services, project gallery, about and contact. A simple website design like this is easier to keep up to date than a sprawling site with little to say.

  • Home: who you are, what you make or renovate, the area you cover and a button to ask for a quote.
  • Services: a short section for each type of work, from bespoke furniture to full renovations.
  • Project gallery: your portfolio of finished jobs, grouped by type.
  • About: your story, your workshop, your team and your expertise.
  • Contact: phone, WhatsApp, email and the towns you work in.

Home: answer the first questions at a glance

Visitors should understand within a few lines what you do and where you do it. A clear headline such as “Bespoke kitchens and fitted wardrobes in Elche and Alicante” says more than a vague slogan. Add one strong picture of a finished job, and make sure everything works on mobile devices as well as on a computer. Useful features such as call and WhatsApp buttons should stay within easy reach.

Services to show on a carpentry and joinery website

Tell each visitor what you make, what you install and what you renovate, in concrete sentences such as: “We make bespoke kitchens and built-in wardrobes in our Elda workshop and install them in homes across the province of Alicante.” Give each service its own section or page so that readers, and search engines, understand exactly what you do. Bespoke furniture, kitchens, doors and stairs

For many joiners, bespoke furniture is the core of the business: shelving, beds, dressing rooms, tables, bathroom units and decorative pieces. Someone ordering a fitted kitchen or built-in wardrobes also wants to see how you use the space, so show layouts that combine function and design, with drawers, sliding doors and hardware. Explain your process, from the first visit and the design to making and fitting, and say whether you install what you make.

If you make doors to measure, supply lacquered doors, craft timber staircases or panel walls, list it clearly. Say what your site joinery includes, such as hanging doors, fitting skirting boards and window frames, so that builders and homeowners can tell whether to call you.

Full renovations of flats and houses

If your renovation company coordinates other trades (bricklayers, plumbers, electricians and painters) and you refit kitchens and bathrooms, say so, and present each type of building work with its starting point, what was done and the result. The project gallery: the heart of your site

Your project gallery is where potential customers judge the quality of your work, so give it a prominent place, open it with your best jobs and show each one in detail. Use only photos of your own work: stock images don't show what you can do. Pictures don't need to be taken professionally, but they should be sharp, well lit and tidy.

What to photograph on each job

  • The space as it was, if it is a renovation.
  • The finished piece or room, in good light and without clutter.
  • Close-ups of joints, hardware, edges and the grain of the wood.
  • Your workshop and your team at work.

Create a short write-up for each project in your portfolio: what the customer needed, the solution you designed, the materials and the town. If you draw up plans or renderings first, show them next to the finished result. Short descriptions of your images (alt text) help visitors who use a screen reader and support your SEO in image searches. Compressing your pictures, part of image optimisation, keeps the site fast.

Before and after, with permission

Before-and-after shots are a simple way to show what a renovation or a restored piece has changed. If a recognisable person appears in a photo, you need their consent under Spanish image rights law: ask for it in writing, and never publish a client's address. Wood, finishes and experience

Someone ordering a piece of furniture wants to know what you will make it from and how you look after the finish. Explain it in simple words, because in carpentry the details matter.

Timber and boards. Solid wood, plywood, MDF, melamine or lacquered panels: say what you work with and what each material is suited to. Solid timber stands up well to daily use, while boards make it easier to keep the budget under control for interior furniture. Explain what works best in a kitchen, a bathroom or a bedroom.

Finishes and care. Varnish, lacquer or oil: describe what each finish brings (visible grain, a smooth colour, a warm feel) and how to care for the wood so that it lasts a long time. This kind of detail shows your expertise, helps customers compare quotes and explains the value of a high standard of finish.

Experience, workshop and team. People invite you into their home, so they want to know who will turn up. Mention your years in the trade, where you trained and who is on your team, and add a photo of you at work. If you have your own workshop, show it: seeing where the furniture is made can build trust.

Local SEO for carpenters, joiners and renovation firms

Local SEO (local search engine optimisation) aims to increase your chances of appearing in the results when someone nearby searches for your trade, such as “joiner Jávea” or “carpintero en Elche”. Local SEO rests on three things: a website that says clearly where you work, a complete Google Business Profile and genuine reviews. Done well, it can bring traffic from customers looking for a joiner near them.

Name your towns and the search terms people use

Mention your town and the places you cover in your titles, your service descriptions and the footer, with the same name, address and phone number as your business profile (known as NAP). If you are based in Elda but work across the province, say so. Think about the searches someone nearby would type, such as “joiner Alicante”, “fitted wardrobes Torrevieja” or “carpintero a medida Elche”, and use those search terms in your titles and texts where they are relevant, in English and in Spanish if you serve both.

A sentence such as “We work in Elda, Petrer, Elche and Alicante” is enough: there is no need to repeat a town in every heading, and Google's spam policies list keyword stuffing, including blocks of town names. Read more about how to choose local keywords and how to rank your website in local searches.

Separate town pages? Only with content of their own

If you work in several towns, separate pages only make sense when you have content specific to each town, such as projects you have completed there or work you only take on there. Pages that are identical apart from the town name are what Google's spam policies treat as doorway pages. If you have nothing specific to say about a town, one page that covers your whole service area and lists the towns is the better option.

Your Google Business Profile

The profile is free for local businesses and can appear on Google Maps and in local search results. If you don't have one yet, create it, then fill it in fully and keep your services, pictures and opening hours the same on your profile and on your website. Turning visits into quote requests

Website traffic only turns into business when visitors take the next step, so keep your phone number and a WhatsApp button in view, especially on mobile, and make it simple to write to you. Every website we build has a contact form that sends enquiries straight to your email, and a WhatsApp button that lets potential customers message you directly.

Many of your customers will look you up on their mobile, so your site should be designed for small screens first, load quickly (speed also plays a part in SEO: see why speed affects your ranking) and use responsive web design, which adapts the layout to all devices. What to ask in each enquiry

To prepare a quote quickly, ask each person to provide their name and number, the type of job, the town and a short description of the idea. Many prefer to send a picture of the space and its measurements by WhatsApp: invite them to do so and reply to enquiries as soon as you can, because customer service starts before the first visit. How to talk about prices

You don't need to publish fixed prices, and you should never invent them: every piece has its own measurements. Explain what a quote depends on (size, materials, hardware and fitting), what warranty you give and how quickly you reply, so that each customer sees the value behind the price. If your quotes are free and without obligation, say so clearly.

Reviews, trust and legal texts

Genuine feedback and clear legal information show visitors that a real, reliable business stands behind the website.

Reviews: ask every customer, then show them honestly

Ask each customer for a review while the result is still fresh, in the first days or weeks after the job: a short message with a direct link to your Google profile is enough. Comments that mention the quality of the finish, punctuality and tidiness help other customers choose you. See how to get more Google reviews and how to respond to negative ones.

If your website displays reviews, EU consumer rules, which apply in Spain, require you to tell visitors whether and how you check that they come from customers who have actually hired you. Show them word for word, say where they come from, keep the negative ones visible and never invent or buy them. On the websites we build, we add this notice for you.

Legal texts and data protection

A business website in Spain needs a legal notice, a privacy policy and a cookie policy under Spanish law (LSSI) and the GDPR, and we include these legal texts with every website. Use the messages you receive only to answer the enquiry they relate to, and ask for consent before publishing pictures in which someone can be recognised.

Common mistakes on joinery and renovation websites

Watch out for these mistakes, which can turn away enquiries on the websites of carpenters, joiners and renovation firms. All of them are easy to fix:

  • Stock photos instead of your own work.
  • No mention of the towns you cover.
  • A phone number tucked away where nobody sees it.
  • Empty phrases such as “extensive experience” or “we take care of every detail”, with no finished work to show for them.
  • Invented prices, or promises you can't keep.
  • Made-up testimonials, or only the favourable ones.
  • Town pages that are identical apart from the name.
  • Keyword stuffing in place of useful content, which does nothing for readers or SEO.
  • A site that is hard to use on a mobile.
  • No legal texts.

How can you get your joinery website built?

There are three ways to get a website for your carpentry business or renovation company: build it yourself, hire a web design freelancer or agency, or use a done-for-you service. Each one takes a different amount of money and time. Whichever you choose, gather these first:

  1. Your best pictures of finished jobs, grouped by type.
  2. A list of your services and the towns you cover.
  3. How you want to be contacted: by call, WhatsApp, email or all three.
  4. Your Google Business Profile, created or claimed, with the same information as your website.
  5. Feedback on recent jobs: ask your last clients for a review before you go live.

Build it yourself

Online website builders and platforms such as WordPress, Wix or Squarespace let you create a site yourself, with templates and features designed for many kinds of businesses. They are cheap to start with, but you have to choose a design, learn the tool, write the content, take the pictures and look after updates and security; a WordPress site in particular has to be updated regularly, themes and plugins included. If you're weighing them up, read Wix or WordPress.

Hire a web design freelancer or agency

In Spain, professional website design costs around €800 to €2,000 from a freelancer and €1,500 to €4,000 from an agency, before maintenance (Social Media Pymes, 2026). Before you hire a web design studio, ask to see websites it has built for a joiner or another trade, check whether basic SEO is included and find out who keeps the domain if you leave.
