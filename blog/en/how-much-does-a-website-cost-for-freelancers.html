What affects the cost of a website?

Several things set the cost, from the size of the job to what it takes to keep it running afterwards. Knowing them helps you avoid extras you won't use:

  • Type of site: a few pages presenting your services cost far less than an online shop with a product catalogue and a payment gateway.
  • Pages and content: every extra page means more texts and photos, which you either prepare yourself or delegate.
  • Template or custom design: adapting a well-made template is quicker and cheaper than a website design created from scratch for your brand.
  • Features: a booking system, several languages or connections with other tools add hours to the build, and some add to your monthly bill.
  • Who builds it: someone you hire, or you, with a website builder.
  • Running costs: hosting, maintenance and edits after launch, which many quotes leave out.

Hiring a web design agency: €1,500 to €4,000

In Spain, an agency-built website comes to around €1,500 to €4,000, before maintenance, according to Social Media Pymes (2026).

What you get:

  • a team that covers design, development and marketing;
  • a style made to measure for your brand;
  • capacity for complex sites and integrations;
  • support set out in a contract.

What to watch:

  • a large upfront budget for a one-person business;
  • delivery that often takes several weeks;
  • every change goes through them.

Hiring a freelance web designer: €800 to €2,000

Hiring a freelancer is usually the more affordable route: according to the same source, a website built by a freelancer costs around €800 to €2,000 in Spain, also excluding maintenance.

What you get:

  • lower rates;
  • a direct line to the designer;
  • more flexibility and quicker decisions.

What to watch:

  • quality varies a lot from one designer to another;
  • hosting and the domain are often arranged and paid separately;
  • if the freelancer becomes unavailable, support and updates can be hard to get;
  • not every designer handles SEO or marketing.

Building it yourself with a website builder

A platform such as Wix, Squarespace or WordPress.com lets you create a site yourself with a visual editor, without coding. The cost is a monthly subscription that rises as you add options, such as a higher plan or paid apps and plugins, plus your own time. To weigh up two popular choices, read Wix or WordPress.

What you get:

  • a cheap start, with many free templates;
  • full control: you change what you want, when you want.

What to watch:

  • it takes time to learn the platform and build everything;
  • without design experience, the result can look amateur;
  • you handle the wording, photos, legal notices and security yourself;
  • the total cost grows with premium plugins and higher plans.

Ongoing costs to budget for

The build is only part of the cost. Whoever creates your website, these recurring items keep it working, secure and up to date:

  • Domain: your web address, usually renewed every year; make sure it's registered in your name (see how to choose your web address).
  • Hosting: the server space where your site lives, paid monthly or annually, which affects its speed and performance.
  • SSL certificate: the padlock that keeps the connection secure, bundled by some hosts and paid with others.
  • Maintenance: software, plugin and security updates, done by you or by someone you hire.
  • Backups: copies of your site so it can be restored if something breaks.
  • Changes after launch: new prices, photos or opening hours; some providers include them, others quote each one.

Add these to the upfront price to see the total over two or three years: that figure, not the first invoice, is what you actually spend.

A cheap build that leaves everything else to you can end up costing more than a dearer one that covers it.

Getting a website without overpaying

Whoever does the work, a few habits keep the bill under control:

  1. Define the goal: calls, WhatsApp messages, bookings or sales.
  2. List the sections you need: for many tradespeople, home, services, about and contact pages are enough at first.
  3. Prepare your content: texts, photos of your work, prices and the areas you cover. Our tips on writing content when you're not a writer can help.
  4. Ask every provider the same questions: what the quote covers, who owns the domain, what modifications cost after launch and what happens if you cancel. Check the terms before you sign.
  5. Compare the total cost over two years, not just the first payment.
  6. Once your new website is live, complete your Google Business Profile with the same details and share the link with your customers.
