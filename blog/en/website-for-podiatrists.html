Your foot clinic may run well on word of mouth, but many potential patients will look you up online before they call. A good website tells them which services you offer, who will see them, where you are and how to get in touch. This guide, for podiatrists in Spain from Torrevieja to Valencia, covers the web design choices and content that work, the Spanish rules your site must follow, local SEO, marketing and costs.

Why do podiatrists need a website?

Because people with a foot problem often search online before they pick up the phone. Today, your website is where they see what your practice does, who works there and how to reach you, at any time of day, including weekends.

What patients want to know before they call

  • which treatments you offer and how a session works;
  • who will see them, and with what qualifications;
  • where you are, your opening hours and how to get there;
  • how to contact you or book.

A clear site that answers these points quickly helps potential patients decide; if they can't find them, they may simply call another clinic.

Web design best practices for podiatrists

Good web design for a healthcare practice is not decoration: it helps patients find information quickly and get in touch with confidence.

A calm, clean design

Choose one or two colours from your brand, a readable font and plenty of white space. Photos of your own waiting room, consultation room and staff show patients what to expect; stock images could belong to any clinic.

Simple navigation

Keep the menu short (Services, About us, Contact) and your phone number and WhatsApp button visible on every page, so visitors who arrive from a search engine never have to hunt for them.

Mobile-friendly design, quick to load

Many of your patients will look you up on their mobile, so your site should be designed for small screens, with large buttons and light images. A page that loads slowly spoils the user experience and can hold back your search rankings: see our article on website speed and search rankings.

Accessible design and languages

Some of your patients may be older or have reduced mobility: use a large font, strong contrast and alternative text on images, and say whether the clinic has step-free access. What should a podiatry practice website include?

Like any healthcare practice, you need a simple design and a handful of clear pages, free of medical jargon (see our checklist of essentials):

  • Services, with a section for each main treatment
  • Team and qualifications, with each colegiado number
  • First visit: how it works and what to expect
  • Appointment requests: a link to the tool you already use
  • Contact, opening hours, social media links and directions
  • Legal notice, privacy policy and cookie policy

Your team and qualifications

Introduce each person with their name, training and years of experience, with their permission, and show each colegiado number, which patients can check with the Colegio, the official association of podiatrists. Qualifications show your expertise, and an About us page can tell your clinic's story.

Your first visit: what to expect

Explain step by step who welcomes the patient, how long the session lasts and what happens. New patients then know what to expect and arrive more relaxed.

How should you describe your podiatry services?

Describe them in plain terms: what each treatment involves, who carries it out and what a session is like. Results depend on each patient, and diagnosis and treatment are decided in person, so describe the care you provide, not an outcome.

  • Chiropody and common conditions: explain what chiropody involves (routine foot care for nails and hard skin) and name the foot conditions you treat, such as ingrown toenails, plantar fasciitis or verrucas, without suggesting they will clear up or promising an end to pain.
  • Biomechanics: present a gait analysis as an assessment of how the foot and ankle work as someone walks, and explain how you make custom insoles, with no promises attached.
  • Children and athletes: explain how you adapt sessions for younger patients, and what you assess in a runner's foot and ankle.
  • Diabetes and surgery: if you see patients with diabetes, describe how check-ups work and whether you work with their doctor; if you perform podiatric surgery, such as nail surgery, say who carries it out.

What rules apply to a healthcare website in Spain?

Podiatry is a regulated healthcare profession, so you must say who you are and follow the health advertising rules.

Colegio, colegiado number and qualification

Spain's e-commerce law (the LSSI) requires your site to show:

  • your name or company name, NIF (tax number), address and email;
  • if you practise a regulated profession, your Colegio, your colegiado number and your qualification;
  • the details of your clinic's health authorisation, if it requires one.

Health advertising: truthful and prudent

Spain's Royal Decree on health advertising rules out promises of relief or a cure, so leave out before-and-after photos and wording that promises an outcome. Some regions, such as the Región de Murcia, also require prior authorisation of health advertising, including websites: ask your Colegio and your regional health department.

Patient data in your forms

Health data needs extra protection: ask only for what you need to reply, with no questions about medical history or why someone is coming in. WebAutonomos includes the legal texts in both plans. This is general information, not legal advice.

Digital marketing for podiatrists

Your website is the base of your marketing, not the whole plan, and the same health advertising rules cover all your marketing content: posts, articles and ads. A few simple steps help new patients find you and your practice grow:

  1. Use the same clinic name, location and opening hours on your website and every online profile.
  2. Share clinic news on social media with a link to your services page (how to choose your social media platforms).
  3. Send occasional news to patients who have asked for it (our beginner's guide to email marketing).
  4. Try content marketing: short articles that help patients understand their feet, such as one on how to cut toenails (see our writing tips for non-writers).

Common mistakes and how to fix them

These mistakes undermine trust, and each one is easy to fix:

Mistake What to do
Promising outcomes or timeframes Describe what you do, from a check-up to surgery, without promises
Using before-and-after photos Show your premises, with the permission of anyone who appears
Asking in the form why someone is coming Ask only for a name and one way to reply
Hiding the Colegio and colegiado number Show them next to your qualification
Ignoring mobile design and accessibility Use a readable font, large buttons and fast loading
