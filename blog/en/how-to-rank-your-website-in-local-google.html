You have a website and a Google Business Profile, yet your business doesn't show up when someone nearby looks for what you do. Often your pages don't make clear what your business offers and where. This guide covers how to rank a local business website, with local SEO best practices for small businesses in Spain and examples from Valencia, Alicante, Elche and Elda. For local SEO in general, see our guide to local SEO for freelancers.

How does Google choose local search results?

Google ranks local search results on three main factors: relevance, distance and prominence. You can't change distance, but your Google Business Profile and your pages influence the other two; improving them is what local SEO is about:

  • Relevance: how relevant a business is to the search. Complete, accurate information about your products or services makes that clear.
  • Distance: how far your business is from the searcher, or from the location in the search, such as “electrician Elche”.
  • Prominence: how well known your business is, based on reviews, links, articles, directories and how your pages rank in organic search. SEO best practices help your local rankings and online visibility.

The Local Pack and the organic results

For many local searches, Google shows a Local Pack near the top: a few businesses with a map, with the organic results below.

Your Business Profile can appear in the Local Pack and on Google Maps, and your pages in the organic results.

Align your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile helps customers find you on Google Maps and in local search results; your website gives them complete information about your business and a way to get in touch.

Together they shape your online presence and visibility, and for local SEO both should tell the same story.

Link them to each other

  • Add your website address to your Business Profile.
  • On your contact page, link to your profile to help visitors read your customer reviews.
  • With multiple locations, point each listing to the matching location page.

Consistency matters too: your brand name, categories, opening hours and products or services should match. A plumber whose profile says “Plumber” but whose homepage says “integral home solutions” sends mixed signals.

Many local businesses update one and forget the other; see our tips to optimise your Google Business Profile.

Choose the right local keywords

Local keywords are what customers type in a local search: usually what you do plus a place, such as “plumber Elche” or “bathroom renovation Valencia”. They help new customers and search engines understand what your business does and where, and they're the foundation of your local SEO strategy.

How people search locally

  • What you do plus a town: “electrician Alicante”, “locksmith Elda”.
  • “Near me” searches, where Google uses the searcher's location.
  • Questions: “how much does a new bathroom cost in Valencia?”
  • Other languages: your target audience may search in Spanish (“fontanero Elche”) or Valencian.
  • Related searches at the bottom of the search results.

One keyword to target per key page

Create a list of local keywords, then give each important page a relevant main keyword: the homepage for your main trade and town, then one per type of job or town.

Use specific terms that best describe the job, such as “boiler repair” rather than “heating solutions”. For a complete method, read how to choose local keywords.

On-page local SEO

On-page SEO covers the elements you control on your pages; for local SEO, each element should include what you offer and where. Here is an example for a plumbing business in Elche:

ElementWhat to includeExample
Title tagWhat you do, the town and your brand nameEmergency plumber in Elche | [Business name]
Meta descriptionWhat you offer, where and a reason to clickLeak and drain repairs in Elche. Call or WhatsApp us.
H1 headingThe main keywordEmergency plumber in Elche
URLSimple and readable/plumber-elche
Image alt textWhat the photo showsPlumber fixing a kitchen leak

Title tags and meta descriptions

Your title tag is a key SEO element for ranking and clicks: Google often uses it as the clickable title in search results, so put the keyword first.

The meta description isn't a direct ranking factor, but a specific, relevant one helps people choose your business over other businesses.

Headings and content

Use a single H1 and subheadings that follow the questions customers ask, then give them the information they need:

  • what's included;
  • the areas you cover;
  • how to ask for a price;
  • photos of real jobs.

Mention the town where it's relevant; keyword stuffing doesn't help.

Images and internal links

Compress your photos and add descriptive alt text, which is important for users of screen readers and helps search engines understand the image.

Link related content, such as an article and the job it mentions, so visitors and Google can follow your structure.

Keep your NAP consistent

In local SEO, NAP stands for name, address and phone number. It's important to show these details on your website exactly as on your Google Business Profile, with the same brand name and no extra keywords, so customers and search engines find the same information everywhere.

Where to show your NAP

  • In the footer, so it appears everywhere.
  • On a contact page, with a map, opening hours and a form.
  • On each location page, if you have several locations.
  • As a number customers can tap to call, next to a WhatsApp button.

Local citations and consistency

A local citation is any online mention of your business with its name, address and phone number, for example in online directories.

Keep your citations accurate: if one listing says “C/ Mayor” and another “Calle Mayor”, choose one version. Our article on local citations and NAP has tips on Spanish directories.

LocalBusiness structured data

Structured data is code, invisible to visitors, that gives search engines key information about your business: name, address, opening hours and the area you serve. LocalBusiness from Schema.org is the usual type for local businesses; our Schema LocalBusiness guide shows how to create it.

Pages for each type of work and town

Small businesses that offer several services or cover several towns can't make one page relevant to every local search. From a local SEO point of view, a dedicated page gives each search a specific answer and can attract its own traffic.

What to include

  • What the service involves and what the customer gets.
  • The towns and areas you cover.
  • Photos of jobs you've done.
  • Key information: prices, a price range or how to ask for a price.
  • A clear way to get in touch: call, form or WhatsApp.

For example, a plumbing business in Alicante could have one page for leak repairs, one for water heaters and one for blocked drains. A small retail business can do the same for each type of product.

Location pages for the towns you cover

Before creating a page for a nearby town, check that you take jobs there and have something specific to say: an electrician in Elda who also serves Petrer can describe the jobs completed locally.

Copies that only change the town name don't help visitors, and Google's spam policies treat such pages as doorways. If you serve several nearby towns, one page covering your whole area is often enough.

Local content for customers in your area

Local content answers the questions customers in your area ask, using your own experience. It can help your business appear in more local searches and give you something to share in your other marketing.

Topic ideas for your blog

  • Specific questions customers ask you by phone or WhatsApp.
  • Seasonal tips, such as servicing air conditioning before summer in Valencia.
  • What changes the price of a job in your area.
  • Completed jobs, with photos and the town's name.
  • Community events you take part in.

Quality over quantity

Write relevant posts about what you know best, with your audience in mind, and don't copy other sites. A few useful posts kept up to date can do more for your local SEO than many thin ones.

Building a library of articles takes time, so create a content strategy you can keep up. If writing isn't your thing, read how to write content when you're not a writer.

Build trust with reviews and local links

In local SEO, prominence and visibility also depend on what others say about your business online: customer reviews, links from other local businesses and mentions in directories or the press.

Show your Google reviews

Google says review count and score factor into local search ranking. Ask every customer for a Google review, not only the happy ones, and reply to every one; see our tips on how to get more Google reviews.

Include a link to your profile so visitors can read every review. If you display reviews on your own pages, EU consumer rules say you should explain whether and how you check that they come from real customers.

Local link building

Links from relevant local sites show Google that people in your area know your business. Good places to start include:

  • your regular suppliers;
  • a business or community association;
  • local blogs and newspapers;
  • a club or event you sponsor.

Building them takes time, so make it part of your strategy; see how to get local backlinks.

Technical local SEO: mobile and speed

Technical local SEO is about making sure search engines can find, read and load your content; issues here can hold back your rankings and visibility in local search.

Design for smartphones first

Many of your customers will look your business up on their phone, and Google mainly uses the mobile version of your content for indexing and ranking. Check that users can:

  • read the text on a small screen without zooming;
  • tap the call and WhatsApp buttons easily;
  • fill in the contact form with one thumb.

Speed, HTTPS and indexing

Compress images, use formats such as WebP and avoid plugins you don't need. Google tools such as PageSpeed Insights show where to improve; see why speed matters for your Google ranking.

You also need an SSL certificate (HTTPS): without one, browsers flag your site as not secure. Submit an XML sitemap in Google Search Console and use URL Inspection to check that your important URLs are indexed.

Measure your local SEO progress

Three free Google tools are enough to track local SEO for small businesses: Search Console for your visibility in local search, the Performance section of your Business Profile, and Google Analytics, which shows what users read and which marketing channels bring your traffic (see basic metrics for freelancers).

Google Search Console

Its Performance report shows:

  • Queries: the search terms that bring up your business.
  • Pages: which ones get clicks.
  • Clicks and impressions.
  • Average position, to follow your local rankings.

For example, a plumber can see that the business already appears for “emergency plumber Valencia” and improve the content that targets that search.

Business Profile Performance

The Performance section of your Google Business Profile shows:

  • how many people viewed your profile;
  • the searches that led to it;
  • calls, direction requests and website clicks.

Your local SEO checklist

Follow this local SEO strategy step by step:

  1. Start with your NAP: make it match everywhere.
  2. Choose a main keyword for each key page.
  3. Review your title tags, meta descriptions and H1s.
  4. Create pages for your main services and, where it makes sense, your towns.
  5. Add LocalBusiness structured data.
  6. Publish local content and ask customers for reviews.
  7. Test your site on a smartphone.
  8. Track progress in Search Console and your Business Profile, then improve what isn't working.

Local SEO needs steady effort and is one part of your online marketing.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take for a local business website to rank on Google?
There is no fixed timeframe: it often takes from several weeks to several months, depending on the competition in your area and industry, your starting point and how regularly you improve your local SEO. Track your rankings in Google Search Console.
Do I need a blog for local SEO?
It isn't essential, but it helps. Blog posts answer your potential customers' questions, such as how much a bathroom renovation costs in Valencia, and each one is another way for people searching online to find your business.
Is it better to invest in Google Ads or local SEO?
It depends on your business goals and budget, and many businesses combine the two. Ads stop showing when you stop paying; local SEO takes longer, but the content, links and reviews you build stay in place.
Which matters more: my website or my Google Business Profile?
Both, because they do different jobs. Your Google Business Profile can appear on Google Maps and in the Local Pack, which shows a few businesses with a map; your site can show up in the organic results and describes your products or services. Link them and keep the same information on both.
Can I do local SEO myself?
Yes, you can handle the basics of local SEO yourself: title tags and headings with your trade and town, consistent contact details, a page for each main service, local content and reviews. Structured data or speed improvements may need help from a developer or an SEO agency.
