Social media for the self-employed: which ones should you actually use

"You need to be on Instagram. And Facebook. And TikTok. Post videos. Publish every day." If you are self-employed in Spain, you have surely heard this more than once. But the truth is that social media for the self-employed doesn't work the same way as it does for big brands. An electrician in Elda or a hairdresser in Alicante doesn't need a million followers: they need real customers who call and hire them. In this article, we help you choose the networks that truly work for your type of business.

The truth about social media for the self-employed: what nobody tells you

For most self-employed tradespeople and local service providers, social media is not the main client acquisition channel. 87% of consumers use Google to find local businesses, compared to 48% who check social media. On Google, the person already has a specific need; on social media, they are just browsing. Social media should be a complement, not the foundation of your digital strategy.

Facebook for local businesses: is it still worth it

Facebook has 22 million active users in Spain, with the 35-55 age group being the most active. Local neighbourhood and city groups are a goldmine of clients. Low-cost local advertising (3-5 euros per day) can reach thousands of people in your area. However, organic reach is only 2-5% of your followers, so posting without promoting has limited impact.

Instagram for the self-employed: when yes and when no

Instagram works very well for visual businesses: hair salons, renovations and decoration, landscaping, carpentry and custom furniture. It is not a priority for plumbing, electrical work, accountancy firms or emergency services. Reels generate 3 times more reach than static photos. A 15-second video showing a before-and-after transformation can reach thousands of people in your area.

WhatsApp, Google and your website: the trio that actually generates clients

The winning trio is: Google Business Profile as your storefront on Google Maps, your professional website where you build trust and close the sale, and WhatsApp Business as your direct customer service channel. The ideal flow is: the client finds you on Google, visits your website, messages you on WhatsApp, and you close the deal. This funnel works better than any social media strategy.

A realistic social media marketing plan for the self-employed

Priority 1 (30 min/week): respond to Google reviews, post on GBP with a photo, and manage WhatsApp Business. Priority 2 (30 min/week): post on Instagram or Facebook and participate in local groups. Raul, a painter in Elche, receives 12 monthly contacts from Google (60% conversion) and 3-4 from social media (20% conversion). The data confirms where to invest more time.

Don't build your business on rented land. Your website and Google listing are yours; social media platforms are a complementary window. Prioritize a professional website, Google Business Profile and WhatsApp Business. Social media adds value, but it's not the foundation of your digital strategy as a self-employed professional.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be on every social network as a freelancer?
No, and trying is a mistake. It is better to be on one or two networks and run them well than on five with abandoned profiles. Choose the networks where your customers are and where you can be consistent. For most tradespeople in the Valencian Community, Instagram and WhatsApp Business are enough alongside your website and Google Business Profile.
How much time a day should I spend on social media?
Between 15 and 30 minutes a day is enough for a freelancer. Spend ten minutes posting content — a photo of a finished job, a useful tip — and five to ten answering comments and messages. The most efficient approach is to prepare a whole week of content on Sunday evening and schedule it with free tools such as Meta Business Suite.
Instagram or Facebook for a local business?
It depends on your audience. Facebook remains the most used social network in Spain among the over-40s, who are the main buyers of services such as plumbing, electrics and renovation. Instagram works better for visual businesses — hairdressing, interiors, gardening. The good news is that you can post to both at once from Meta Business Suite. If you can only pick one, Facebook usually performs better for trade services.
Is paying for social media advertising worth it?
It can be, if you know how to target your local audience properly. For €50 to €100 a month you can reach thousands of people in your area. For most freelancers, though, the return is higher on Google (SEO plus Google Ads) than on social media. The reason: on Google people are actively searching for your service, whereas on social media you interrupt them while they look at photos of their friends.
Does TikTok work for tradespeople?
Surprisingly, it can. Short before-and-after videos, tricks of the trade or answers to common questions have real viral potential. TikTok generates more visibility than direct conversions, though. If your website, GBP and Instagram are already well managed and you have time to spare, it can be an interesting channel. Do not prioritise it over the channels that bring customers directly.
