“You need to be on Instagram. And Facebook. And TikTok. Post videos every day.” If you're self-employed in Spain, you've probably heard this advice more than once. But social media marketing doesn't serve an electrician in Elda or a hairdresser in Alicante the way it serves big brands: they don't need a million followers, they need clients nearby who call. This guide compares the main social media platforms, who each one suits, what content it needs and how much effort it takes.

What are the best social media platforms for a local business?

The best platforms for you are the ones your target audience already uses and that you can keep up with every week. For many freelancers and local businesses in Spain, that means one or two social media platforms, often Facebook or Instagram, on top of a strong online presence: a website, a Google Business Profile and WhatsApp Business.

Here is how they compare, to help you find the right mix for your local market. The last two rows aren't networks in the usual sense, but they're often where local enquiries arrive.

Instagram Hair, beauty, food, interiors Photos, Reels, Stories Medium

Whichever social media platform you choose, the same strategy applies: publish useful content regularly and make it easy for users to contact you.

Why social media is a complement, not the foundation of your marketing

For many tradespeople and local businesses, social media isn't the main source of new work. Someone with a burst pipe looks up a plumber online; they don't wait for one to appear in their feed. The person searching already has a specific need, while users scrolling are browsing for fun.

The platform sets the rules

Each platform decides which content its users see and can change its rules overnight. Unpaid posts often reach only part of your followers, and an account built over years can be restricted without warning: you're building on someone else's terms. Your own domain and web pages are channels where you set the rules, making them the place to begin: here are five reasons to get your business online.

What it does well

Social media still earns its place in your marketing strategy. It keeps you in the minds of people nearby between jobs, lets happy clients recommend you and puts a human face on your trade. Satisfied customers also post content about you, tagging your business in photos of a new kitchen or a fresh haircut: valuable marketing you don't pay for. Big brands can pay for reach and publish every day; a one-person business can't, and doesn't need to.

How to choose the right social media platform

Before making a decision, answer these five questions. They help you pick the one or two social media platforms worth your marketing effort instead of trying to be everywhere.

  1. Who are your customers? Their demographic (age, area) and whether they're households or other businesses define your audience.
  2. Can your work be shown? Haircuts, cakes and gardens make great images; tax advice is easier to explain in words.
  3. How many hours can you give it? A profile whose latest update is months old can make users wonder whether you're still trading.
  4. Are you happy on camera? TikTok and YouTube users expect video, while Facebook, LinkedIn and X (Twitter) also suit photos and text.
  5. What do you want from it? Enquiries, bookings, trust or sales of products each point to a different platform.

Platforms like to announce their monthly active users, but for a local trade the only audience that counts is the one in your area. If you can only manage one platform, start where your existing customers already are: ask a few of them which apps they open every day.

Facebook: local groups and a broad adult audience

Facebook is still a widely used platform in Spain, especially among adults, and its users rely on it for neighbourhood groups, local events and community pages. For a trade that serves one town, that local side is the key.

  • Who it suits: trades and shops serving adults nearby, such as plumbers, electricians, gardeners and restaurants.
  • Content: photos of finished jobs, opening hours, seasonal reminders and helpful answers in local groups.
  • Effort: low to medium, since a business page with regular updates and quick replies is enough to look active.

Get recommended in local groups

Neighbourhood groups and groups for English speakers on the Costa Blanca are full of people asking questions such as “Does anyone know a good electrician near Elda?” Join a few groups with active users, read their rules on promotion and answer with practical advice rather than adverts: it's word-of-mouth marketing in digital form. A past customer tagging you often counts for more than anything you publish yourself.

Events and recommendations

You can also create events, such as an open day or a tasting, and collect recommendations from customers, which helps new visitors trust your business. Pin a post with your services and areas to the top so visitors find the key information first.

Instagram: a showcase for visual trades

Instagram is visual by nature and popular with younger adults, which makes it a great fit for businesses and brands whose results can be seen. Reels, its short clips, can reach users beyond your followers, while Stories, which disappear after 24 hours, keep your regular audience up to date.

  • Who it suits: hair and beauty salons, tattoo studios, bakeries, restaurants, interior designers, landscapers and carpenters.
  • Content: before-and-after photos, clips of the process, behind-the-scenes Stories and carousels that explain a service.
  • Effort: medium, since a profile needs regular, engaging content and strong photos, which your phone can take.

It's rarely a priority for plumbing, electrical repairs or emergency call-outs, where clients only start searching when something breaks.

Make your profile clear to new visitors

A professional account allows you to see insights on each post. Write a bio that says what you do and where (“Cakes to order in Valencia”) and pin your favourite posts to the top: that's how a pastry chef in Valencia can start building a following and turn a casual visit from a potential customer into a message. Save useful Stories, such as prices or recent projects, as Highlights on your profile.

LinkedIn: the professional network for selling to companies

LinkedIn is the professional network, used worldwide by professionals who follow an industry, find suppliers, hire and build connections. If you sell to other businesses, it's the platform to put near the top of your list, because its users come to it for expertise in a specific field.

  • Who it suits: consultants, translators, accountants, architects, designers and developers.
  • Content: practical advice from your field, short project write-ups (with your client's permission) and articles that show your expertise.
  • Effort: medium, and networking, such as commenting and keeping in touch with your connections, matters as much as publishing.

Start with your own profile

For a freelancer, a personal profile does the selling more than a company page. Give it a clear headline (“Freelance English–Spanish translator in Valencia”), a photo and your services. LinkedIn also allows you to request recommendations from people you've worked with and to publish longer articles, and our guide to personal branding for freelancers explains how to build that reputation.

TikTok: short videos that can reach new audiences

TikTok is a short-video platform, popular with younger users, whose main feed can show your clips to anyone who doesn't follow you yet. That means an opportunity for a new profile, but a million views mean little if none of the viewers live near you.

  • Who it suits: businesses at ease on camera with younger clients, such as hairdressers, barbers, fitness trainers, cafés, bakeries and mechanics.
  • Content: quick tips, tricks of the trade, the process behind a job and answers to frequent questions, filmed in a creative, informal style.
  • Effort: high, because the platform runs on frequent, original videos and fast-changing trends.

When TikTok makes sense for a trade

A clip of a repair or a transformation can catch people's attention, but TikTok tends to bring visibility more than direct enquiries. Many brands chase trends and popular sounds; for a local trade, a clear tip filmed on your phone can be enough. The platform also sells advertising, but unpaid content is where to begin. Treat it as an extra marketing channel once the basics are in order, and only if you enjoy filming.

YouTube: how-to content that keeps being found

YouTube is the platform where users go to learn how to bleed a radiator, prune an olive tree or plan a kitchen renovation. A useful video can keep attracting views for months or even years, which makes YouTube a great marketing tool for trades that can teach.

  • Who it suits: anyone who can explain a skill, from a plumber describing common breakdowns to a Spanish teacher for expats.
  • Content: how-to videos, answers to frequent questions, project walkthroughs and Shorts, the platform's vertical clips.
  • Effort: high, since making each clip takes hours of planning, filming and editing, even if a phone and a quiet room are enough to start.

Use each video in more than one place

A YouTube video that answers a common question can also go on your site, into a newsletter or into a WhatsApp reply: that way, one recording keeps paying off across your marketing.

X (formerly Twitter): news and conversation

X, still widely called Twitter, is a platform for short posts, breaking news and public debate. Its users include journalists, politicians and tech professionals, and some brands use it for marketing and customer service, but it isn't where neighbours go to find a tradesperson.

  • Who it suits: consultants, journalists, tech freelancers and businesses whose audience follows their sector on Twitter.
  • Content: short comments on industry updates, replies to questions and links to your articles.
  • Effort: medium, because the conversation moves fast and a profile only stays visible if you take part regularly.

Should a local trade be on Twitter?

For many local trades, no. Reserve your name as a handle so nobody else takes it, then put your effort where your customers are.

Pinterest: where clients collect ideas

Pinterest behaves more like a visual search engine than a place to chat: users save images, called pins, to plan weddings, homes, gardens or recipes, and the platform allows each pin to link to a web page. Many of its users are still collecting ideas, which makes it a great marketing channel for brands whose products are planned ahead.

  • Who it suits: wedding photographers, florists, interior designers, furniture makers, cake makers and shops selling home or fashion products.
  • Content: photos of your strongest projects or products, each with a clear description and a link.
  • Effort: low to medium, because you can reuse images you already have, and pins can keep being found long after you add them.

Reach clients at the planning stage

A florist in Altea or an interior designer in Valencia can pin finished projects so that couples and homeowners planning a wedding or a renovation find them while they're still deciding. Shops can do the same with products such as furniture, cakes or decorations. Name your town in each description, using the terms locals type.

Where do WhatsApp and your Google profile fit in your marketing?

They come first. Together with your website, they form the ideal path: a customer finds you on Google, checks your site, messages you on WhatsApp and you send a quote. Any social media platform can feed this path, but none replaces it.

Google Business Profile: your shop window on Maps

This free profile shows your hours, photos, reviews and updates on Search and Maps. It's often the first thing people see when they look up a local trade, so keep it complete and active. WhatsApp Business: fast replies

The free WhatsApp Business app allows you to add a profile with your details, a catalogue, quick replies, labels and automatic greeting and away messages. Clients can write to you in seconds, so answering each customer quickly is crucial; see how to set up WhatsApp Business for free.

What content should you share?

Share engaging content that answers a question, shows your work or reminds your audience that you're nearby. Your phone and the jobs you do every week provide plenty of effective marketing material, especially photos of finished projects:

  • Before and after: show a bathroom renovation in Elche, a garden in Jávea or a new haircut, which make great Reels.
  • The process: film a cake being decorated or a piece of furniture taking shape.
  • Frequent questions: explain prices, timings or what to do while waiting for the plumber.
  • Seasonal tips: help homeowners get their air conditioning serviced before summer.
  • Reviews: thank a customer for a review, with their permission.
  • News from your business: announce holiday opening hours or a new service.

Make every update lead somewhere

Whatever the platform, end with a clear call to action: a link, your WhatsApp number or a phone call. A line such as “Message us on WhatsApp for a quote” gives potential customers an easy way to act.

Plan a month of content

A simple monthly content plan keeps your marketing consistent on any platform without making it a daily chore:

  1. Week one: show a finished project in a photo or a short clip.
  2. Week two: give a practical tip or answer a frequent question.
  3. Week three: take your audience behind the scenes, for example into your workshop or your van.
  4. Week four: publish a customer review or an update such as new opening hours.

How much time does social media marketing take?

Less than you might think, if you stick to one or two social media platforms and an easy routine that keeps your profiles active. The key is a short, regular slot each week, which works better than a burst of activity followed by months of silence.

  1. Priority one: reply to reviews, add an update to your profile on Maps and answer customer messages on WhatsApp.
  2. Priority two: publish on your main platform and take part in one or two local groups.
  3. Priority three, if you have hours to spare: try video on TikTok or YouTube, or a second platform.
  4. Monthly: check where your enquiries came from and adjust your marketing strategy.

Schedule a week of content in one sitting

Meta Business Suite, Meta's free tool, allows you to schedule posts for Facebook and Instagram from one place, so you can plan the week on a Sunday evening and only deal with comments and messages after that.

Reuse each piece of content

A single photo of a finished kitchen can become a Reel, a Facebook update, a pin and an update on Maps. Adapt the text to each platform's different style rather than starting from scratch.

Paid social media advertising: is it worth it?

It can be, once you know which content gets a response on each platform and you can target the right local audience. Set a modest monthly budget, measure the enquiries each campaign brings and keep only what pays off.

Facebook and Instagram ads let you choose the area, age range and interests of the users who see them, which suits an opening or a seasonal offer in your local market. LinkedIn advertising can reach people by job and industry, and TikTok and YouTube advertising is built around video.

Paid posts or search ads?

On any feed-based platform, you interrupt users while they browse photos of their friends; on Google, they're actively searching for your service. Social media mistakes that waste your effort

Avoid these common marketing traps:

  • Opening a profile on every platform, from TikTok to Twitter, then abandoning them.
  • Publishing only promotional content, with nothing useful for your audience.
  • Leaving comments and messages unanswered for days, each one a missed opportunity.
  • Buying followers, which adds numbers but no customers.
  • Using a different name or phone number on each profile, which confuses clients.
  • Copying the strategies of big brands, which have marketing teams and budgets you don't need to match.
  • Switching strategies every few weeks, before any of them has had a chance to show results.
  • Judging success by followers instead of enquiries.

More pitfalls are covered in these digital marketing mistakes and how to avoid them.

How to measure what each platform brings you

Note where each enquiry came from (Google, a social media platform, a recommendation, WhatsApp) and whether it led to a sale. A painter in Alicante who keeps that note for a few months will know which channel deserves a bigger place in their strategy. Four monthly figures help you decide:

  • messages and calls from people who mention a profile;
  • clicks from each profile to your pages;
  • quotes requested and jobs won, by channel;
  • reactions and shares, a good sign of which content your audience values.

Prioritise that base in your strategy, choose one or two platforms that suit your customers and your week, and let your own results show you where to go next.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be on every social network as a freelancer?
No. It's better to run one or two networks well than five abandoned profiles. Choose the platforms where your customers are and where you can stay consistent: for many tradespeople in the Valencian Community, one platform plus WhatsApp Business is enough alongside a website and a Google Business Profile.
How much time should I spend on social media each week?
A short, regular routine is enough for many freelancers and local businesses: share a photo of a recent project or a useful tip, then answer comments and messages. Preparing a week of content in one sitting and scheduling it with a free tool such as Meta Business Suite makes the routine easier to keep.
Instagram or Facebook for a local business?
It depends on your audience. Facebook is still widely used in Spain, especially by adults, and its local groups suit trades such as plumbing, electrics and renovation. Instagram suits visual businesses such as hairdressing, interiors and gardening. You can publish content on both from Meta Business Suite.
Is paying for social media advertising worth it?
It can be, if you target people in your area and measure the enquiries each campaign brings. For urgent services, search ads are often worth testing first, because the users who see them are actively looking for a service rather than browsing photos of their friends.
Is TikTok worth it for tradespeople?
It can be. Short videos of a repair, tricks of the trade or answers to common questions can reach younger users who don't follow you yet, but TikTok tends to bring visibility more than direct enquiries, so treat it as an extra once your main channels are in order.
Is LinkedIn useful for a freelancer?
Yes, if you sell to other businesses. Consultants, translators, accountants and designers use LinkedIn to share their expertise, keep in touch with past contacts and be found by companies looking for a supplier. For a trade serving households, it's rarely a priority.
Which networks suit a business that sells products?
Photo-led networks suit products that photograph well, such as cakes, jewellery or furniture: Pinterest lets each pin link to the page where people can buy, and a WhatsApp Business catalogue lets clients browse your products before they message you.
Do I still need a website if I'm active on social media?
Yes. Each platform decides which users see your content and can change its rules at any time, while your own site is a space you manage, which search engines can find and every profile can link to.
