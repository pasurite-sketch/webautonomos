Google Ads for beginners: a step-by-step guide for freelancers and small businesses

Google Ads can put your business above the organic search results at the moment someone looks for what you sell. Set up well, it can bring in enquiries without waiting months for your website to rank; set up badly, it simply burns money. This Google Ads guide for beginners is written for freelancers and small businesses in Spain that want to add paid search to their marketing: you'll learn everything you need to get started, from how the auction works to keywords, ads, budget and results.

What is Google Ads and how does it work?

Google Ads (formerly Google AdWords) is the digital advertising platform run by Google. You choose keywords or an audience, write your ads and set a budget; each time a user searches, Google Ads runs an auction to decide which ads appear, and in what order. With text ads you pay only for each click on your ad, hence the name pay-per-click (PPC) advertising, a form of search engine marketing (SEM).

The ad auction and Quality Score

Google Ads doesn't simply pick the highest bidder: it calculates an Ad Rank from your bid, the quality of your ads and landing page, the context (location, device, time of day) and the expected impact of your assets. Ads need a high enough Ad Rank to appear, and a higher Ad Rank wins a better position, so a relevant ad can beat a competitor who bids more.

You rarely pay your full bid: the auction charges what's needed to keep your position, so the actual cost per click is often below your maximum. Quality Score, a diagnostic rating Google Ads gives each keyword, sums up expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience; a low score shows where to improve.

Is Google Ads worth it for freelancers and small businesses?

Google Ads is worth it when there's demand for your product or service in your area, when each job leaves enough margin to pay for the clicks, and when you need enquiries and sales now rather than waiting months for SEO results. Unlike social media marketing, it doesn't interrupt anyone: your ad appears when someone is already searching, which suits urgent or high-value services. As a marketing channel, Google Ads is also flexible: pause it or cut the budget whenever you're fully booked. It's not the right moment, though, if nobody searches for what you do.

Do the maths before you spend

Compare the cost of a click or a lead with what a client is worth. Imagine a dentist in Valencia who charges 60 euros a consultation: at around 3 euros a click, twenty clicks cost as much as the fee, so if a new patient takes more clicks than that, the first visit doesn't cover the advertising unless the patient returns. For a plumber charging 50 euros for a one-off job, every wasted click hurts more.

How to set up your Google Ads account step by step

Opening Google Ads costs nothing: you only pay once your ads go live, and getting started takes a few minutes.

  1. Log in with a Google account linked to your business email and go to the Google Ads site.
  2. Enter your business name and site; you can create your first campaign straight after.
  3. If you're shown a simplified setup, switch to Expert Mode to choose the campaign type, keywords and bids yourself.
  4. Set your billing country, time zone and currency (euros: it can't be changed later).
  5. Add a payment method (if you're self-employed, ask your adviser about the invoices) and complete advertiser verification if Google Ads asks for it.

Smart campaigns, the simplified option, are quick to set up but give you little control over the searches you pay for; Expert Mode, used in this guide, gives access to every campaign type and setting, including match types, negative keywords and bidding strategies.

Then connect your other Google tools: Google Analytics, to see what visitors do after the click, and your Google Business Profile, so your ads can show your address (here are some tips to optimise your Google Business Profile).

Google Ads campaign types: which one should you start with?

For your first campaign in Google Ads, choose Search: it shows your text ads to people who already have a problem to solve, gives you full control over keywords and budget, and shows which searches led to each click. Google Ads offers several campaign types, each built for a different goal:

Campaign type Where your ads appear Best suited to
Search Google search results, marked as sponsored, plus partner sites if you allow them Customers looking for a business like yours
Performance Max Every digital channel from Google in one campaign, from YouTube to Gmail and Google Maps Advertisers with reliable conversion tracking
Display Partner websites and apps of the Google Display Network Brand awareness and remarketing to past visitors
Video YouTube Showing your work and building your brand with video content
Shopping Google results and the Shopping tab, with photos and prices Online shops with a product catalogue in Google Merchant Center

When to explore Performance Max and video

Performance Max relies on machine learning from Google and works best with conversion data, so it suits a second stage, once you master Search and track enquiries reliably. Display and video campaigns are brand marketing tools: their visual content reaches an audience that isn't searching yet, which suits brand awareness more than an electrician who needs calls today.

Keyword research: find the searches that bring clients

Keyword research, the basis of search marketing, means listing the words people type when they look for what you sell and keeping the relevant ones, with clear buying intent. Begin with your trade and your town; the next step is to explore Keyword Planner, the free keyword tool inside Google Ads, which shows related keyword ideas with their monthly searches and a top-of-page bid range, a rough guide to the cost per click.

Add the more specific words your customers use on the phone, and see our guide on how to choose local keywords for the full method. If you also serve English speakers, create a separate keyword list and campaign for each language, since Google Ads sets language targeting per campaign. The same words can guide your site, your SEO and your other digital channels.

Choose keywords with buying intent

Keywords such as “emergency plumber Elche” or “boiler repair Alicante price” come from someone ready to hire; “how to unblock a drain”, “what does a plumber earn” or “plumbing course Valencia” come from someone who wants information, a job or a course. Build your campaign around the first kind and turn the second into negative keywords.

Keyword match types and negative keywords

Match types tell Google Ads how closely a search must match your keyword for your ad to show. There are three:

  • Broad match (plumber Elche): searches related to your keyword, even without its words. It reaches more searches and needs close watching.
  • Phrase match ("plumber Elche"): searches that include the meaning of your keyword, such as “emergency plumber in Elche”.
  • Exact match ([plumber Elche]): searches with the same meaning or intent, such as “Elche plumber”.

Google Ads recommends pairing broad match with automated bidding that learns from your conversions. With a small budget and no conversion history yet, phrase and exact match are a safer first step, give you more control over your best keywords and help you learn which ones convert; broad match can wait until your conversion tracking works.

Negative keywords and the search terms report

A negative keyword stops your ads from showing on searches you don't want: a plumber might add “jobs”, “course”, “free” and “how to become” from day one. Negatives don't cover close variants, so add plurals separately.

Then check the search terms report in Google Ads every week. It shows the actual searches behind each click, so you can exclude irrelevant ones and spot new opportunities.

How to structure your account

With your keywords ready, the next step is to build the structure. Google Ads has three levels: campaigns, ad groups and ads. Budget, location, language, schedule and bidding strategy are set per campaign; each ad group, based on one topic or type of job, gathers closely related keywords and their ads.

Create one ad group per type of job, so every ad shows the right message, and a separate campaign only when you need a different budget, location, language or schedule, such as a 24/7 emergency service.

Example: an electrician in Elche

  • Campaign: “Electrician Elche”, with its own budget, location, schedule and negative keywords.
  • Installations ad group: keywords such as “electrical installation Elche”, with ads about new installations.
  • Repairs ad group: “electrician repairs Elche”, “power cut electrician Elche”, with ads that stress a quick response.
  • Certificates ad group: “electrical certificate Elche”, “boletín eléctrico Elche”, with ads about certificates.

The three jobs are ad groups inside one campaign, not three campaigns competing for one small budget. Keeping each ad group to one topic improves ad relevance and Quality Score, and lets you compare the performance of each job at a glance.

How to write Google Ads that bring results

Good ads speak to what the user is looking for, give a concrete reason to choose you and end with a call to action. In Search campaigns, you write responsive search ads: several headlines and descriptions that Google Ads combines.

Headlines and descriptions

Each ad needs at least 3 headlines and 2 descriptions and accepts up to 15 headlines and 4 descriptions. Google Ads keeps testing combinations and favours those that perform best, while Ad strength, from Poor to Excellent, shows whether you've given it enough variety. Pinning a headline, such as your brand name, to a position reduces the combinations Google Ads can test.

What to say in your ad copy

  • A relevant headline: someone searching “boiler repair Alicante” should see that keyword in a headline.
  • A specific benefit that's true: a free quote, a reply within 1 hour, 20 years of experience.
  • A direct call to action: Call now, Request a quote, Book a visit.

Treat each ad as a short piece of marketing copy and avoid claims you can't prove: the advertising policies of Google Ads don't allow misleading ads, and exaggerated copy attracts the wrong clients. Write different versions of your ad copy and keep the ones that bring enquiries.

Assets: the new name for ad extensions

What Google used to call ad extensions are now assets: sitelinks to specific pages, callouts such as “English spoken”, a call button, your address from your Google Business Profile, lead forms and images. They make your ad more visible in the search results at no extra cost (a click on an asset costs the same as a click on the headline), and Google Ads decides when to show each one.

Location targeting and scheduling

Digital ads can target a precise audience, so show yours only where you work and when you can answer. Google Ads lets you target a country, province, city, postcode or a radius around your address, and exclude the areas you don't cover.

Presence or interest: a setting worth checking

Google Ads recommends “Presence or interest”, which also reaches users elsewhere who have shown interest in your area. For a local business, “Presence” (people in, or regularly in, your target locations) often makes more sense: a plumber in Elche gains nothing from traffic in Madrid.

With ad scheduling in Google Ads, choose the days and hours your ads run, based on your account's time zone. If you can't answer the phone while on a job, limit call-focused ads to the hours that suit you best, and remember that many of your customers will look for you on their mobile.

How much does Google Ads cost? Budget and bidding

Google Ads has no minimum spend: you set an average daily budget for each campaign and, in Search campaigns, pay only for clicks, whose price depends on your sector, area, competition and bidding. On some days Google Ads may spend up to twice your daily budget to catch extra traffic, but over a month it won't charge more than the daily budget times the average number of days in a month.

Start small and give it time

A modest advertising budget is enough to learn, for example 5 euros a day, about 150 euros a month. At 3 euros a click that buys about fifty paid clicks, so check the bid estimates for each keyword first. Once your campaign is live, wait for 2-3 weeks of performance data before raising the budget, longer if few people search for your service. To decide how much of your marketing budget goes to ads, read how much a freelancer should spend on digital marketing.

Which bidding strategy to choose

  • Manual CPC: you set the maximum cost per click yourself, for full control.
  • Maximise clicks: as many clicks as your budget allows, ideally with a maximum bid limit, for example 2 euros (a higher limit wins more auctions, at a higher price).
  • Maximise conversions: automated bidding for as many enquiries as possible, with an optional target CPA; it needs working conversion tracking.
  • Maximise conversion value: the same idea for the value of sales (optional target ROAS), better suited to online shops.

Begin with Maximise clicks and a bid limit, or Manual CPC bidding, and move to Maximise conversions once you record enquiries reliably. After a change of strategy, Google Ads may show a “Learning” status: don't judge results during that learning period.

Your landing page: where visitors become clients

Your landing page is the last step between a click and an enquiry, so it plays a decisive role in any digital campaign. Send each ad group to the right service page rather than your home page. It should:

  • repeat the service and town from your ads;
  • load fast on mobile, because slow loading wastes the click you've just paid for;
  • show your contact details, a WhatsApp button and a short form without scrolling;
  • provide clear content about your prices, the areas you cover and your work, with real photos and reviews;
  • include the legal texts required in Spain: legal notice, privacy policy and cookie policy.

Landing page experience is part of the Google Ads Quality Score, so improving its content can raise your Ad Rank and lower what you pay per click. Speed matters for SEO too: see why your website's speed affects your Google ranking.

How to set up conversion tracking

Conversion tracking tells Google Ads which clicks turned into enquiries, and turns your ads from a cost into a measurable marketing investment. Without it, you can't measure success, only what you spent, so set it up step by step before your campaign goes live:

  1. Decide what counts as a conversion: a call, a form, a WhatsApp message or, for a shop, online sales.
  2. Create a conversion action for each one in your Google Ads account.
  3. Install the Google tag on your whole site, directly or with Google Tag Manager.
  4. Connect Google Analytics 4 (GA4) to Google Ads to see what visitors do after the click.
  5. Test each conversion yourself and check that it's working in Google Ads.

Count form submissions with a form event, and clicks on your phone number and WhatsApp button as separate actions, so every lead is recorded and you see which keywords bring results; calls from your ads' call button count too. In Spain and the rest of the EU, measurement cookies need consent: your cookie banner records each visitor's choice and Google Consent Mode passes it on to your Google Ads and Analytics tags; without that link, your conversion data may be incomplete.

Google Ads metrics: how to read your results

Unlike printed flyers or local media, digital advertising shows what each click costs and brings. To judge your Google Ads performance, focus on a handful of figures and one question: how much does each enquiry cost, and is it worth it?

  • Clicks and CTR: the click-through rate is clicks divided by impressions and plays a role in Quality Score; a high CTR shows your ads match the search, a low one that they aren't relevant to what the user is searching for.
  • Average CPC: what you paid per click.
  • Conversions and conversion rate: the leads received, and the share of clicks that became one.
  • Cost per conversion: total cost divided by conversions, to compare with what a new client is worth.
  • Impression share: how often your ads appeared when they were eligible; a low share means your budget or Ad Rank is the problem.

There's no universal “good” CTR or cost per click: success depends on your margin, so compare your figures with your own previous weeks, not with averages found online. For your website as a whole, see how to know if your website works.

A simple weekly routine

  1. Review the search terms report for each campaign and add negative keywords.
  2. Check performance by keyword and pause the keywords that spend without bringing enquiries after a fair trial.
  3. Keep your best headlines and test new ideas to see what works.
  4. Take action on bids or budget only when the data is clear, and note every change.

This short check keeps you in control of what's happening in your Google Ads campaigns and lets you take action early.

Common Google Ads mistakes beginners make

In online marketing, expensive mistakes are often about control: paying for the wrong searches, sending visitors somewhere that doesn't match and not measuring results.

  • Broad match keywords without negatives: bidding on “plumber” alone means competing for traffic from all over Spain, much of it not relevant to you.
  • Unchecked network settings: a new campaign may also run on partner sites and the Display Network; keeping a first campaign on Google Search makes the data easier to read.
  • No conversion tracking, and deciding by gut feeling which keywords to keep.
  • Applying every recommendation: the suggestions in Google Ads can help, but read each one first and check whether auto-apply is on.
  • Generic ad copy: “Quality service, great prices” says nothing your competitors don't.

Free Google Ads training and marketing courses

Google offers free Google Ads training on Skillshop, its digital learning platform: explore its on-demand courses and certifications at your own pace. You don't need technical knowledge or special skills to start, just time and a live account to practise on.

  • Google Skillshop: courses and Google Ads certifications that you can earn online; it replaced the former Academy for Ads.
  • Google Ads Help Centre: step-by-step articles on every setting and report.
  • Google Ads Community: the official forum, where advertisers and Google Product Experts answer questions and offer support.
  • Google Analytics and Keyword Planner: digital tools to practise with your own data.

Other marketing courses exist, from HubSpot Academy to YouTube tutorials, but check their date: the platform and its tools change often. Learning by doing is how you master the basics and build your marketing skills. Google Ads and local SEO: two sides of your marketing

Paid digital ads bring visits fast, but the traffic stops when your advertising does; local SEO takes months to show results but brings organic traffic without a cost per visit. They work in different ways, and both belong in your digital strategy, alongside social media and email marketing.

Your Google Business Profile, which costs nothing, can also get you into the Map Pack (see our guide on how to appear in the Google Map Pack), and the search terms that bring enquiries in your Google Ads campaigns show which keywords to use in your organic content and in local SEO for freelancers.

Google Ads works for freelancers and small businesses when it's set up with care: a Search campaign, specific local keywords with the right match types, ads that fit each query, a clear landing page and conversion tracking from day one. Start small, check your results every week and grow step by step, scaling only what works. Google Ads complements SEO and your other marketing rather than replacing them: together, they help clients find you now and in the long run.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need to start with Google Ads?
Google Ads has no minimum spend: you choose the average daily budget of each campaign. You can start small, for example with 5 euros a day, about 150 euros a month, and increase it only when your results show which keywords bring enquiries.
Does Google Ads work for every business?
Google Ads works best for services that people actively search for and that leave enough margin to pay for clicks. If nobody searches for what you offer, your ads won't bring you clients.
Can I manage Google Ads myself?
Yes, for a simple local campaign. Google offers free training on Skillshop and step-by-step articles in the Google Ads Help Centre. For more complex campaigns, or if you don't have time, it's worth working with a professional or an agency.
How long does it take to get results?
Your ads can start showing as soon as Google Ads approves them, so the first clicks can arrive quickly. To know which keywords bring enquiries, give your campaign at least 2-3 weeks of data, and longer if few people search for your service.
What is Quality Score in Google Ads?
Quality Score is a diagnostic rating that Google Ads gives each keyword, based on expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience. Rather than chasing a particular score, use it to spot keywords whose ads or landing pages need improving.
Can I advertise in English to reach English speakers in Spain?
Yes. Google Ads can target users by language and location, so you can show ads in English to English speakers in Alicante or Valencia, for example. Create a separate campaign for each language, with keywords and ads written in that language.
Is Google Ads or SEO better?
They're complementary marketing channels. Google Ads brings visits quickly, but you pay for each click while your ads run. SEO takes months, but you don't pay for each visit. The ideal is to combine both.
