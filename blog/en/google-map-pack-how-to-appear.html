Search for “plumber in Valencia” on your phone and Google often shows a map with a few nearby businesses above the organic search results. That block is the Map Pack, and being in it can bring a freelancer or small business in Spain more calls and visits. Here is how to rank in the Google Map Pack, step by step.

What is the Google Map Pack?

The Google Map Pack, also called the Local Pack, is the block of results with a map and a few nearby businesses that Google shows for local searches. Each entry comes from a Google Business Profile, formerly Google My Business.

Each business in it usually shows its name, its stars, whether it's open and buttons to call or get directions; tapping one opens its full listing. To show up on Google Maps as well, see how to appear on Google Maps.

Which searches trigger it?

It appears when someone is looking for a place or a business nearby: “dentist Alicante”, “electrician near me” or “English-speaking lawyer in Benidorm”. Informational searches such as “how to bleed a radiator” rarely trigger it, and in local search the businesses shown change with the searcher's location.

How does Google decide who ranks in the Map Pack?

According to Google's help pages, local search results are based mainly on three factors: relevance, distance and prominence. There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

  • Relevance: how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for. Detailed categories, services and description help Google match you to relevant searches such as “emergency plumber in Elche”.
  • Distance: how far each business is from the searcher's location or the place named in the query.
  • Prominence: how well known a business is, including how many sites link to it and how many reviews it has. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking.

Distance is the factor you can do least about. The other two you can influence, though not control, through signals such as accurate information, consistent details, genuine reviews and a site that confirms what you do and where.

Paid ads are a separate route

Google Ads can show your business as a sponsored result in Google Search and on Google Maps, and in some countries and sectors Google offers Local Services Ads. Ads stop when the budget does; see our beginner's guide to Google Ads.

Create, claim and verify your Google Business Profile

Creating a profile is free with a Google account; if your business already shows on Google Maps, claim the existing one rather than starting a duplicate.

Then verify it. Google says verification tells it you're authorised to represent the business, so it's more likely to show up in search results. Depending on your business, you may be asked for a short video as proof.

Use your real business name

Write the name customers know, as on your sign and invoices. Google's guidelines forbid adding keywords, towns or slogans, and breaking them can lead to suspension: your trading name on its own is fine, the same name followed by “cheap plumber Valencia” is not.

Address or service area

If customers visit your location, enter the exact address used on your site, with the postcode.

If you travel to them, as many plumbers or mobile hairdressers do, Google asks you to hide the address and set a service area. Choose the towns you really cover, such as Elche, Santa Pola and Crevillent, rather than the whole province.

Fill in every section of your profile

Google states that businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to show up in local search results. Our ten optimisation tricks go into more detail.

Primary category and secondary categories

Your category tells Google what kind of business you are. For the primary category, choose the option that best describes your main activity, as specific as possible; then add secondary categories only for other work you really do. Our guide on choosing your business categories has examples by trade.

Services, hours and description

  • Services and products: add the services you offer in the words customers use, such as “boiler repair” or “gel nails”.
  • Hours: regular opening times plus special hours for public holidays, the Fallas or an August closure.
  • Phone and website: a local number you answer and the most relevant page of your site.
  • Description: what your business does and where, without links or price offers; say so if you serve customers in English.
  • Attributes: such as wheelchair access or accepted payment methods.

NAP consistency and local citations

NAP stands for name, address and phone number. Write them exactly the same everywhere:

  • your Google listing and your site's footer and contact page;
  • Facebook, Instagram and other social media platforms;
  • listings on directories such as Páginas Amarillas and on trade association sites.

Google gathers information about each business from several sources. If one says “Calle Mayor 12”, another “C/ Mayor, 12, bajo” and an old listing shows a previous number, it's harder to know which details are right. Consistent details send a clear signal to Google and stop customers calling a number that no longer works.

A citation is any mention of your business with its NAP on another site, such as directories and business listings. Links from a club you sponsor or a regional newspaper also build prominence. Our guides on local citations and earning links in your town explain how.

Reviews: ask honestly and reply to every one

Reviews feed prominence, and replying shows customers you value their feedback.

How to ask for a review

  1. Copy your review link, or turn it into a QR code for the counter or invoices, so customers can leave their opinion in seconds.
  2. Ask every customer once the job is done, not only those you think are happy.
  3. Send it the same day by WhatsApp or email and let them write in their own words.

Google's policy on fake engagement forbids:

  • incentives such as discounts, gifts or free services in exchange for a review;
  • content that doesn't reflect a genuine experience, such as fake or bought opinions;
  • discouraging negative reviews or asking only satisfied customers.

Google can remove content that breaks these rules. For more ideas, see how to get more Google reviews.

Respond to reviews, including negative ones

Thank each customer briefly; replies build trust. With a negative one, stay calm, acknowledge the problem, give the facts without arguing and offer to sort it out by phone or email. See how to reply to negative Google reviews.

Photos and updates that show an active business

Google's tips include adding photos and videos that show what you offer. Use real photos, without stock images or heavy filters:

  • your shopfront as seen from the street;
  • the team and your van;
  • finished jobs, products or dishes.

Updates about news, offers or events are a different matter: Google doesn't include them among its ranking tips, but they show customers that the business is active. Keep a regular rhythm you can sustain; see how to publish Google Posts and photos and videos on your Google listing.

What role does your site play?

A site isn't required to have a profile, but for local businesses it helps: Google also uses information from a company's official site, and links to it count towards prominence.

Your site also competes in the organic results, so organic SEO and local SEO belong in the same strategy.

Pages for each service and town

Give each of your main services its own page, with the service and town in the title, such as “Boiler repair in Alicante”. Create a page for another town only where you really work and have useful content to offer.

Consistent details and LocalBusiness markup

Show the same contact details and opening times as your profile. LocalBusiness structured data, a short block of JSON-LD code, describes those details for search engines; it helps them understand your business but won't put it in the Map Pack on its own. How to measure your progress

Each month, check the Performance section of your profile:

  • views on Google Search and Google Maps;
  • the search terms that showed your business;
  • calls, direction requests and website clicks.

Also look up your service and town on Google Maps from your phone, in different neighbourhoods, because the businesses shown depend on location.

Daily changes in rankings are normal; over time, the trend shows whether your local search visibility is growing. For your site, see these basic metrics.

Mistakes that hold back your local rankings

Avoid these common mistakes:

  • keywords or town names in the business name;
  • duplicate profiles, or an old one nobody manages;
  • a mailbox or an address where you don't receive customers;
  • an old phone number on your site or in online listings;
  • paid-for or rewarded ratings, and complaints left unanswered;
  • opening times not updated for holidays;
  • town pages with copied content.

Be wary, too, of anyone who promises a top-three place by a fixed date: Google doesn't sell these positions.

A plan for the next few months

Getting into the Map Pack takes time, and there's no official timeline: it depends on your competitors, your town and your trade. This order keeps things manageable:

  1. Month 1: create or claim your listing, verify and complete it, and upload real photos.
  2. Month 2: ask every client to rate you on Google, reply to what they write and make your details identical everywhere.
  3. Month 3: give each service its own section on your site and look for a few links from nearby organisations.
  4. Month 4 onwards: check your Performance figures, post regular updates and fix what isn't working.

Frequently asked questions

Can I pay to appear in the Map Pack?
No. Google states that there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Google Ads are a separate, paid route: the ads are labelled as sponsored and stop when you stop paying.
How long does it take to rank in the Map Pack?
There's no official timeline. It depends on competition in your town and trade, how detailed your Google Business Profile is and the reviews you receive. Treat it as a medium-term local search project and check your profile's figures every month.
Do I need a website for the Map Pack?
No, but it helps. Google also uses information from a company's official site, and links from other sites show how well known your business is. Show the same details and services on your site as on your profile.
Why do some people see my business in the Map Pack and others don't?
Distance is one of Google's three local ranking factors, so results change with the searcher's location. Check from several neighbourhoods and nearby towns, not only from your premises.
Can I get into the Map Pack without premises?
Yes. If you work at customers' homes, you can have a Business Profile as a service-area business: Google asks you to hide your address and add the areas you serve. Keep them to towns you really cover.
