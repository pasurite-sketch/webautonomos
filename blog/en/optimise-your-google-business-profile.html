To optimise your Google Business Profile, verify it, fill in every section, show real photos, collect reviews and keep it up to date. It's free, and it can increase your visibility in local search and on Google Maps. Here are 10 steps, with examples for freelancers and businesses in Valencia, Alicante, Elche and Elda.

What does it mean to optimise your Google Business Profile?

It means completing your profile, keeping it accurate and using its features, so that potential customers understand what you do and where, and can contact you directly. Google Business Profile, formerly Google My Business, is the free tool that controls how your company appears online, in Google Search and on Maps.

How Google ranks local businesses

According to its help pages, local search results depend mainly on three factors:

  • Relevance: how well your listing matches the search, based on what you fill in, such as your categories.
  • Distance: how far you are from the searcher, or from the place they name.
  • Prominence: how well known your business is, including its reviews, its rating and links from other websites.

You can't pay for a better local ranking, and businesses with complete information are easier to match with relevant searches, Google explains. It adds that your position in web results counts towards prominence, so the SEO of your site matters too. That's why this work is central to local SEO.

Claim and verify your profile

Sign in at business.google.com and search for your business name. If it already appears on Google Maps, for example because someone suggested it, claim it instead of creating a duplicate: Google allows only one listing per location. See our guide on adding your business to Google Maps.

Verification methods

Verification proves that you run the business; until it's done, you can't manage your profile fully. Depending on your type of business, the options may include:

  • a phone call or a text message with a code;
  • an email with a code;
  • a video showing your location and proof that you manage the business;
  • a postcard with a code.

If an employee or another company helps you, add them as a manager rather than sharing your password, so that ownership stays with you.

Complete your business information

This basic information is important: customers use it to reach you, so fill in every field that applies.

Use your real business name

Write your name exactly as it appears on your sign and your invoices. Adding keywords or a town is a tempting SEO shortcut, but Google's guidelines don't allow it, and it can get your profile suspended: “Peluquería Carmen” follows the rules, “Peluquería Carmen cheap haircuts Valencia” doesn't.

Address or service area

If customers visit you, at a shop in Elda or an office in Valencia, enter your full address and check that the pin is properly placed on your building. Service-area businesses, such as electricians and plumbers who travel to their clients, can hide their address and list the towns or postcodes they really cover, such as Alicante, Sant Joan d'Alacant and El Campello.

Phone number, website and booking link

Use a phone number you answer, the same one as on your site, and link to the page that best matches the listing. Where available, you can also link to the booking tool you already use, so visitors can book online.

Opening hours and special hours

Enter your regular hours, then set special hours for public holidays, including fiestas such as the Fallas or the Hogueras, and any August closure. If you close for a while, mark the business as temporarily closed rather than deleting the listing.

Choose your primary and secondary categories

Your primary category tells Google what your business is and strongly influences the searches you can appear in, so it's worth getting right. Pick the one that best describes your business as a whole, as specific as possible: “Electrician” rather than “Contractor”, or “Nail salon” rather than the broader “Salon”.

Add other categories only for related work you really do, because Google asks businesses to use as few as possible. Before adding one, check that:

  • it's a service you offer regularly, such as air-conditioning installation for an electrician in Alicante;
  • it describes what your business is, not something it has, such as parking or Wi-Fi, which belong in the attributes;
  • it isn't just a broader version of your primary category.

Your category also changes the features available to you, such as booking options. Check what competitors near you have chosen, and see our guide on choosing your categories, with examples from Spain.

Write a clear business description

You have up to 750 characters to explain what you do, for whom and where. Write for readers first: a plain summary naturally contains the relevant words they search for. Include:

  • your services, named the way customers would name them;
  • the area you cover, such as Valencia city and l'Horta Sud;
  • what sets you apart, such as same-day call-outs or written quotes;
  • the languages you work in, which is important if you serve English-speaking clients.

Links and promotional offers aren't allowed in the description, and keyword stuffing, an old SEO habit, only makes it harder to read. A plumber in Elche might write: “Family plumbing business in Elche covering leaks, blocked drains and boiler repairs across the Baix Vinalopó. We work in Spanish and English.” Our guide to choosing local keywords can help you find the right terms.

Fill in services, products and attributes

These sections tell visitors what to expect before they call.

Services with descriptions and indicative prices

List each specific service separately, starting from Google's suggestions for your category and adding your own. Give each one a short summary and, where it helps, an indicative price. A locksmith in Valencia might list emergency door opening, lock changes and security doors as separate entries rather than one vague one.

Products, if you sell them

Shops and other businesses can show what they sell, with a photo, a price and a few lines of text; Google includes in-store products in its tips for retailers. If you don't sell anything, skip this part.

Attributes that customers check at a glance

Attributes are short labels, and the ones available depend on your category and country, for example:

  • a wheelchair-accessible entrance;
  • payment methods, such as cards or mobile payments;
  • Wi-Fi, parking or outdoor seating;
  • online appointments or on-site work.

Select only what's true, and check them again when something changes.

Show your business with real photos

Pictures show potential customers what to expect before they call or visit, they make your listing stand out in search results, and adding photos is one of Google's own tips. Use real, good quality images rather than stock shots or heavy filters, including:

  • your logo and a cover photo;
  • the outside of your premises, especially the entrance;
  • the inside, if customers come to you;
  • your team at work and your van;
  • finished jobs or what you sell.

A short video of your workshop or a job in progress works too. Upload new pictures when something changes, and report anything customers add that breaks the content policies. For formats and ideas, read our guide on what to upload to your Google listing.

Get more reviews and respond to every one

Online reviews are free marketing: they help people choose between businesses, and Google says more reviews and positive ratings can improve your local ranking. Two habits make the difference: asking everyone and always replying.

Ask every customer

The ideal moment is just after a job well done. Copy the review link from your profile and send it by WhatsApp or email, or print it as a QR code for your counter or invoices. Then follow Google's rules:

  • ask everyone, not only those you expect to be happy, because asking selectively for positive ratings breaks its policies;
  • offer nothing in exchange, not even a discount;
  • never write or buy reviews.

Our guide on how to get more Google reviews has ideas for each channel.

How to reply

Replies are public and help build trust, so write with the next reader in mind. Thank reviewers for kind words, ideally mentioning the job. With a critical review, stay calm, acknowledge the problem, give the facts and offer to talk privately; see our advice on replying to negative comments.

Publish posts for your visitors

Posts are short updates with a photo and a button that people see when they find you on Google. They add fresh content and can encourage engagement, but Google doesn't mention them in its ranking tips: use them to inform customers who are already looking at your listing, not to rank higher. Share something relevant:

  • Updates: news, a finished job or a new service;
  • Offers: a promotion with start and end dates;
  • Events: an open day or a stand at a town fair, on a specific date.

A simple strategy is to pick a rhythm you can keep, for example one post a week, and schedule them in advance. A carpenter in Elda, for instance, might share a finished kitchen. Our guide to publishing Google posts explains each type.

Connect your profile with your website and local SEO

Google uses information from across the web to understand businesses like yours, and many visitors check your site before calling, so make sure your profile and your site say the same thing.

Keep your NAP consistent

NAP stands for name, address and phone number, and keeping it identical everywhere is a basic SEO rule: on your listing, your site, your social media pages and directories such as Páginas Amarillas. When something changes, update all your listings. Read more in our guide to citations and NAP.

Make your site match your profile

Include the same services, towns and timetable on your site, and make it easy to contact you. LocalBusiness schema can help search engines read those details, and our guide on how to rank your website on Google explains how to make your site rank too.

How do you know if your changes are working?

Once a month, open the Performance section of your profile, which replaced the old Insights reports. It shows:

  • how many people viewed your listing on Search and Maps;
  • the searches that led them to you, a good source of keyword ideas;
  • calls, direction requests and website clicks.

Use these insights to see whether your visibility is growing and what to change: if you appear in searches for a service you don't list, add it. Figures move with the seasons, so it's important to compare each month with the same month a year earlier. Every so often, also search for your business on your phone, as a customer would, compare it with your competitors and update anything that looks wrong.

What about messaging and Questions and Answers?

Google has removed messaging from Business Profiles and is gradually removing Questions and Answers, so skip those steps if an older guide recommends them.

  • Messaging: customers can no longer send you messages there, so make your phone number easy to find and, if you use WhatsApp, show it on your site; here's how to set up WhatsApp Business.
  • Q&A: Google is phasing out this public section. Move the content you published there to the fields of your profile and to an FAQ page on your site; we explain what changes with Q&A in a separate guide.

Optimisation checklist

Follow this list once everything is set up, and again every few months:

  • Profile claimed, verified and owned by you.
  • Name, location, phone, site and timetable correct.
  • A precise primary category, with only relevant extra ones.
  • A description naming your services and area.
  • Services, products and attributes filled in.
  • Real, recent photos of your premises, team and work.
  • Review link shared after each job; all feedback answered.
  • A post published regularly.
  • The same details on your site, social media and directories.
  • Performance checked every month.

After that, a short weekly routine keeps it up to date:

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to see results from optimising my Google Business Profile?
There's no fixed timeframe. Google checks some edits before showing them in search, and the effect depends on your competition, your location and how consistently you keep it up to date.
Can I have a Google Business Profile without premises?
Yes. If you travel to customers, as many electricians and plumbers do, you can hide your address and set a service area with the towns or postcodes you cover. It then stays private.
Is optimising my profile free?
Yes. Creating and optimising your profile costs nothing, and Google says you can't pay for a better ranking. What it takes is time. If you'd rather hand it over, WebAutonomos manages your profile for €29/month, plus €49 to create it if you don't have one yet: optimisation, 4 posts a month and replies to your reviews.
How many photos should I add to my profile?
There's no required number. What matters is that each picture is real and recent: your premises, your team and your work. Add new ones when something changes.
What do I do if someone leaves a fake negative review?
Reply politely and stick to the facts, then report the review if it breaks Google's policies, for example if it's spam or the author was never a customer. Reports are checked, which can take several days. You can't delete a review yourself.
What's the difference between Google My Business and Google Business Profile?
None: it's the same tool under its new name, and many people still use the old one.
