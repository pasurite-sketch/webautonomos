LocalBusiness schema is structured data that describes your business to search engines: its name, address, phone number, opening hours and the area you serve. Customers never see it, but it helps search engines understand who you are and what you offer. Whether you run a shop in Valencia or work as a plumber around Alicante, this guide explains which properties to include, how to add it to your website and how to check it, with no coding needed.

What is LocalBusiness schema?

LocalBusiness schema, or local business schema markup, is a type of structured data from the Schema.org vocabulary, designed for local businesses with premises or a service area. It's usually written in JSON-LD and sits in the source code, where search engines read it but visitors don't.

It's part of technical SEO, the side of SEO that deals with how search engines crawl and interpret your pages. Think of it as a data sheet any search engine can read, as useful for a dental clinic in Valencia as for a shoe-repair workshop like the one in the photo.

Structured data, schema markup and JSON-LD: the key terms

  • Structured data: information organised in a standard format that machines can process reliably.
  • Schema.org: a shared vocabulary of types and properties, from a project founded by Google, Microsoft, Yahoo and Yandex.
  • Schema markup: the code that applies this vocabulary to your content.
  • JSON-LD: the format Google recommends, rather than microdata or RDFa; it sits in a separate block instead of mixing with your visible content.
  • Rich results: search results enhanced with information from structured data, sometimes called rich snippets.

Which businesses can use it?

Any business that customers visit, or that provides services at their homes or premises: shops, restaurants, salons, clinics, tradespeople or a removals company. LocalBusiness is a subtype of both Organization and Place, so it suits businesses with a physical location; one that only sells online fits Organization better.

What can local business schema do for your business?

It helps search engines interpret your business information without ambiguity, which supports your SEO. It is not a direct ranking factor, though, and Google doesn't guarantee that any feature based on it will appear in search results.

What it can help with

  • Your name, phone number and hours are stated as data, not guessed from your text.
  • You keep one precise version of your NAP (name, address and phone number), identical to your listing and directory entries.
  • Google's documentation links this structured data to features such as the knowledge panel that may appear when people search for a business.

What it does not guarantee

Valid markup makes you eligible for rich results, not certain to get them. It won't push you higher in local search results or bring traffic by itself: your local visibility depends on other factors too, such as your listing, your reviews and your content.

Nor will it get you review stars: Google doesn't show star ratings for reviews that a business publishes about itself on its own website, known as self-serving reviews. Ask customers to review you on your listing instead; see how to get more Google reviews.

Schema markup and your Google Business Profile

Your listing in Maps and Search is managed from your Google account. LocalBusiness markup describes the same business from inside your pages, so the two complement each other: keep your NAP identical in both. No listing yet? See how to add your business to Google Maps.

Which LocalBusiness subtype fits your business?

Choose the most specific subtype possible for your main activity, such as Plumber, Dentist or HairSalon, as Google recommends. If none fits, use LocalBusiness itself.

Subtypes for small businesses

  • Plumber, Electrician and Locksmith: tradespeople.
  • Dentist and MedicalClinic: dental and medical clinics.
  • HairSalon and BeautySalon: hair and beauty salons.
  • Restaurant and CafeOrCoffeeShop: places to eat and drink.
  • AccountingService and LegalService: professional services, including accountants and law firms.
  • Store, or a narrower option such as ShoeStore: shops.

A shoe-repair workshop has no dedicated subtype, so use LocalBusiness. As with the categories of your listing, pick what best describes your main activity, not every service.

Several activities or several locations

If your business fits two subtypes, such as a hair and beauty salon, JSON-LD lets you list both. With several premises, describe each location separately, ideally on its own page, with its own phone number and hours.

Which properties should your local business schema include?

Google's documentation lists only two required properties for LocalBusiness: name and address. It recommends others, such as telephone, url, geo, openingHoursSpecification and priceRange, filled in with as much accurate information as possible.

Property What it describes Tip
name Business name As on your sign, without extra keywords
address Where your premises are Street, town or city, province, postcode and country
telephone Main phone number With the country code, so people can get in touch
url Web link Your homepage, or the location's own URL
openingHoursSpecification Opening days and hours The same as on your listing
geo Latitude and longitude The exact position of your premises
priceRange Price level Symbols such as € or €€
image Photo A real photo of your premises or your work
areaServed Area you cover The relevant towns or region
sameAs Other profiles Your social media profiles, such as Facebook, Instagram or YouTube
email For enquiries One you check every day

Required and recommended properties

Begin with name and address plus telephone and url, then hours, coordinates and a photo. The address itself is split into streetAddress, addressLocality (town), addressRegion (province), postalCode and addressCountry (ES for Spain). Restaurants and other food businesses can also provide menu and servesCuisine.

Hours, coordinates and service area

openingHoursSpecification pairs days with the times you open and close, in HH:MM format. If you close at midday, as many shops in Spain do, use one entry for the morning and one for the afternoon.

For geo, copy your coordinates from Google Maps by right-clicking the exact spot, or by pressing and holding it in the app; Google asks for at least five decimal places. If you work at customers' homes, like a cleaning company covering Benidorm and Altea, list the towns you cover in areaServed and leave out anything you don't show publicly.

What to leave out

  • Ratings or reviews of your own business.
  • Anything visitors can't see.
  • Invented or outdated details, such as an old phone number.
  • Keywords added to your name, such as "cheap plumber Alicante": use the name customers know as your brand.

How to create local business schema markup step by step

You don't need to write code from scratch. Follow these steps:

  1. Choose your subtype.
  2. Gather your business information, including your social media profiles.
  3. Start from the block below.
  4. Replace every placeholder and delete what you don't use.
  5. Validate the block, then publish it.

Sample LocalBusiness schema in JSON-LD

This block uses the Plumber subtype and placeholder values: replace each one with yours.

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Plumber",
  "name": "Your business name",
  "url": "URL of your homepage",
  "telephone": "+XX XXX XXX XXX",
  "priceRange": "€€",
  "address": {
    "@type": "PostalAddress",
    "streetAddress": "Your street and number",
    "addressLocality": "Your town",
    "addressRegion": "Your province",
    "postalCode": "Your postcode",
    "addressCountry": "ES"
  },
  "openingHoursSpecification": [{
    "@type": "OpeningHoursSpecification",
    "dayOfWeek": ["Monday", "Tuesday", "Wednesday", "Thursday", "Friday"],
    "opens": "HH:MM",
    "closes": "HH:MM"
  }],
  "areaServed": ["Your town", "A nearby town"],
  "sameAs": ["Your Facebook URL", "Your YouTube channel URL"]
}
</script>

@context names the vocabulary; the other lines describe the business, its address and its hours. One missing comma or quotation mark can make the block unreadable, which is why validation is important.

Schema markup generators

A schema markup generator turns a form into JSON-LD you can copy, but it doesn't know your business and may default to plain LocalBusiness, so check the result. The same goes for a block drafted by an AI assistant.

How to add schema markup to your website

Paste your schema markup, inside its script tag, into the head or body of the page that presents your business, usually the homepage or the contact page; both positions work.

Static HTML site

Paste the block just before the closing </head> tag of the relevant file and upload it again, or send the validated block to whoever looks after your site.

WordPress

Some SEO plugins take care of creating the block. Rank Math has a Local SEO module where you enter your business details, while Yoast offers LocalBusiness markup in its paid Yoast Local SEO add-on (the free Yoast SEO plugin describes you as an organisation or a person). A plugin that inserts code in the header also works. Either way, make sure only one tool outputs this block.

Wix

In Wix, look for the structured data option in the advanced SEO settings and paste your JSON-LD there; Wix also has a custom code section in its general settings.

Squarespace

Squarespace's code injection feature, available on some plans, lets you paste code into the header of the whole site or of a single page.

Which page should carry the markup?

Choose the page that best represents your business and shows the same NAP. Repeating the block on other pages isn't a mistake if the information matches; conflicting blocks, such as two with different phone numbers, are. Still choosing a platform? Read our comparison of Wix and WordPress.

How to validate your local business schema

Check the block with two free tools, before publishing and again afterwards. Both accept a URL or pasted code.

Check what Google can read

Google's Rich Results Test shows which structured data it detects on a page and can use for its search features, and flags errors and warnings.

Schema Markup Validator

The Schema Markup Validator, hosted by Schema.org, checks your markup against the whole vocabulary, not only the types Google uses, which can help you spot typos.

Errors, warnings and what to fix

  • Correct any error first: it can make the item unusable.
  • Then review the warnings, which often point to missing recommended properties.
  • Run the check again until nothing is flagged; it's important to repeat it whenever your details change.

Common local business schema mistakes

Information that doesn't match what visitors see

The markup must match your visible content. A phone number that only appears in the block, hours that differ from your listing or old details left in a plugin send conflicting signals. Update what visitors see, the block and your listing together.

The wrong subtype or format

  • Using the generic LocalBusiness when a more specific subtype exists.
  • Giving the coordinates of the town centre instead of your premises.
  • Writing your phone number without the international prefix.

Self-serving reviews and duplicate blocks

Marking up your own testimonials won't produce stars. Two blocks describing the same business differently, such as one from your theme and one from an SEO plugin, cause confusion: it's best to keep a single source, and to give each location its own entry.

Where schema fits in your digital marketing

Structured data is a small technical part of search engine optimisation (SEO): it supports the rest of your online presence and digital marketing but can't replace it.

Keep the block up to date

Update it whenever your hours or phone number change and check it again; the URL Inspection tool in Search Console then lets you request a new crawl of your homepage.

Adding LocalBusiness schema is a simple step that makes your business information clear to search engines. Use real data, choose a specific subtype, validate it and keep it updated: it supports your SEO efforts, but it won't replace a well-kept listing or useful content.

Frequently asked questions

What is LocalBusiness schema in simple terms?
LocalBusiness schema is code you add to your website to describe your business to search engines: its name, location, phone number and opening hours. Visitors don't see it, but Google and Bing can read it.
Does LocalBusiness schema improve my rankings?
Not directly: structured data isn't a direct factor in rankings, and rich results based on it aren't guaranteed. It helps search engines understand your business information.
Do I need to know how to code to add schema markup?
Not necessarily. Free generators can produce the JSON-LD from a form, and on WordPress some SEO plugins can create it for you. Either way, validate the result before publishing.
What is the difference between LocalBusiness schema and Google Business Profile?
Google Business Profile is your listing in Search and Maps, managed from your Google account, while LocalBusiness markup describes the same business on your site. Keep your name, address and phone number identical in both.
How do I know whether my website already has LocalBusiness schema?
Enter your URL in Google's Rich Results Test to see the structured data it detects, or open the source code and search for LocalBusiness inside an application/ld+json script.
