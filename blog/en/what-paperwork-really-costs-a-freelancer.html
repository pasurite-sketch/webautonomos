Paperwork costs you 226 hours a year

Nobody invoices you for paperwork. It shows up in no expense line, you never review it at month end, and you leave it out when you work out whether a client is worth having. But you pay for it every day. And now there is a figure that puts numbers on it: 226 hours a year. Close to a full working month, on official paperwork alone.

Where the 226 hours come from

ATA, the Spanish sole traders' federation, published the figure in August 2026. It covers 226 hours a year per person spent purely on dealings with public administrations: quarterly filings, registrations, certificates, formal requests. Converted into eight-hour days, that is 28 days. Nearly a month a year you invoice to nobody and that appears in no set of accounts.

€3,390 you never see leave

ATA values those hours at €3,390 a year per person, costing the hour at €15. That is a cautious figure: if you bill at €35 or €40 an hour, your real paperwork cost is double or triple. Across the 3.4 million sole traders in Spain, the collective bill comes to around €11,770 million a year.

And that is only the official part

The ATA figure measures dealings with the administration only. It does not count the quote you redo for the third time, the invoice you copy off the delivery note, the March email you hunt for over twenty minutes, or the unpaid invoices you chase whenever a spare moment turns up. Add it all together and most small businesses sit well above those 226 hours.

Going digital has not fixed it

This is the part that surprises people. According to the 2026 report by Qonto and IO Investigación, 23 % of the most digitalised Spanish SMEs spend more than ten days a month on administrative work, against 9 % of the least digitalised. The explanation sits in the same data: 75 % run more than one platform to manage their finances, and 29 % run four or more. The time does not go into working. It goes between systems.

How to work out your own figure in two minutes

The 226 hours are an average. Yours will be different, and it is worth knowing. You only need three numbers: how many times a day you do the task, how long it takes each time, and what your hour is worth. Multiply by 240 working days and you have the annual cost of that specific task. No spreadsheets, no odd formulas: three numbers and one multiplication.

Not everything is worth automating

Here is the part almost nobody tells you. A task that takes ten minutes a day does not pay back a custom build: the cost of keeping it running eats the saving within months. Others do pay back, handsomely. The difference is not how much the task annoys you, but three things: the volume, how alike each occurrence is, and where the data sits. If it sits on paper, the picture changes a lot.

Before hiring someone for the paperwork, or paying for a tool that promises to solve everything, it is worth knowing what it actually costs you. Is this useful if I have staff?
Even more so. With a team the hours are spread across several people and the cost rises, because it is no longer your time but salaries with contributions. The free diagnostic covers both cases.
