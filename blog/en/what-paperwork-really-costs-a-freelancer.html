Time spent on admin: what it costs a small business in Spain

Admin is easy to underestimate: nobody sends you a bill for it, and it never appears on an expense line. Yet in Spain, official paperwork alone takes a self-employed worker more than five working weeks every year, according to ATA. Here is what that figure leaves out, how it compares with British studies, what it really costs your business and how to win some of it back.

How much time does a small business spend on admin?

For a self-employed worker in Spain, official procedures alone take 226 hours a year on average, according to ATA. Quotes, invoices, emails and chasing late payers come on top, so the true total is higher. British surveys, which count more than dealings with the authorities, find similar or larger totals for firms in the UK.

Where the 226-hour figure comes from

ATA, the Federación Nacional de Asociaciones de Trabajadores Autónomos (a national federation of self-employed workers' associations), published the figure in August 2026.

It counts only dealings with public administrations: quarterly tax returns, registrations, deregistrations and formal requests.

What it costs: €3,390 a year

ATA puts the cost at €3,390 per person, valuing each hour at €15. If your rate is €35 or €40, your actual cost is two to three times higher.

Across some 3.4 million autónomos, the collective bill comes to around €11,770 million annually.

How does the UK compare?

British studies agree that the load is heavy there too and that it weighs on growth, but each figure changes with what the study counts. They describe the UK, not the Spanish market, so read them as a comparison rather than as a measure of a business in Valencia or Alicante.

Sage: thirteen months of effort, twelve months of income

Research from Sage, the accounting software company, found that the average UK small business loses 24 working days a year to financial administration: in effect, an extra month of unpaid effort.

An earlier Sage study valued one extra hour of productivity a week across SMEs at around £7bn for the UK economy.

HeyBRB: new tools, same workload

In a study published in March 2026, HeyBRB, an automation consultancy, surveyed UK business owners online, through LinkedIn and partner channels. Roughly one in two were sole traders, from industries ranging from trades and property management to accounting and other service businesses. Respondents reported eight hours per week on admin, or ten full weeks annually.

  • Three in four had used an AI tool in the past twelve months, yet the workload had not gone down.
  • Nearly half dealt with it in the evenings, and two in five said it cut into growth activities.
  • Asked which single task they would take off their plate tomorrow, owners named invoice chasing and payment follow-ups first, ahead of repetitive customer queries, reporting and data entry.
  • More than four in five had never calculated what it costs their business.

The authors point out that the sample was limited and self-selected: treat the survey as a signal, not a census.

The UK government's survey of firms

The Business Perceptions Survey, published by the government, is based on 2,000 telephone interviews with a random sample of private-sector firms. They reported spending eight days a month on average on regulatory compliance, and almost half agreed that regulation is an obstacle to success.

The burdens they named included staying up to date with regulation, completing paperwork and records, and having to provide the same information more than once.

Tide: comparing yourself with peers

Tide, a fintech for SMEs, surveyed UK SME owners with Censuswide for its Business Benchmark Index 2026, a tool for comparing working patterns, savings and confidence with peers, including by industry. The index shows that around one in six respondents never take a full day off.

Time etc: the cost of doing everything yourself

Time etc, a virtual assistant company, worked with Censuswide to survey entrepreneurs based in the United States, not the UK, for a study on delegation. Owners said that, on average, more than a third of the week went on administration.

Their to-do list included logging expenses, research, schedule management, invoicing, chasing late payers and writing social media content. The reasons for not delegating will sound familiar: some enjoyed doing it, others felt it was faster to do it themselves, had nobody to hand it to or found it hard to trust someone else with it.

Why the figures do not match

Each study measures something different. ATA counts dealings with Spanish public administrations, Sage counts financial administration, HeyBRB counts everything and the government survey counts regulatory compliance. The methodology differs too, from a random telephone sample to self-selected online responses, and people who feel swamped are more likely to answer a survey about admin. Press stories often quote these figures without that context.

Industry matters as well: a plumber issuing dozens of bills each month carries a different load from a consultant with three monthly clients. Use the studies as orders of magnitude, then write down your own figures for a fortnight.

What the ATA figure leaves out

Running a business also brings chores that never reach a timesheet:

  • Quotes and invoices: the quote that gets redone three times, the bills you retype from delivery notes.
  • Getting paid: late payments you chase whenever a spare moment turns up, while your cash waits.
  • Emails and messages: confirming visits, answering clients, hunting for the agreed price, customer service.
  • Bookkeeping: receipts, bank statements and the bank app to see who has paid.

Each one looks minor. Together, they decide how much is left for paid work. As seen in the UK surveys above, the same chores come up there, so the problem is not specific to Spain.

Why digitalisation has not cut the paperwork

According to a study published in 2026 by Qonto and IO Investigación, 23 % of Spanish SMEs with a high level of digitalisation spend more than ten days a month on administrative tasks, against 9 % of the least digitalised.

The same study points to the cause: 75 % run more than one platform to manage their finances, and 29 % run four or more. Effort is lost between systems, typing the same figures into several places.

The British studies agree: HeyBRB's respondents had tried new tools without lightening the load, and the government survey lists supplying the same information more than once as a drain. More tech does not necessarily mean less admin.

The official paperwork of an autónomo in Spain

For autónomos in Spain, official obligations revolve around the Tax Agency (Agencia Tributaria, often called Hacienda) and Social Security:

TaskBodyWhen
RegistrationAgencia Tributaria and Social SecurityBefore you start trading
VAT (IVA) and income tax (IRPF) returnsAgencia TributariaEvery quarter, plus annual returns
Cuota de autónomos, based on real incomeSocial SecurityEvery month
NotificationsTax Agency and Social SecurityWhenever one is sent
DeregistrationTax Agency and Social SecurityWhen you stop trading

Social Security notifies you electronically, and the Tax Agency can too. A notification you don't open before the deadline is treated as delivered, which means the clock runs anyway, so switch on the email alerts. The Agencia Tributaria publishes the calendar and practical guides on its website.

Still filing in the UK? Making Tax Digital

Making Tax Digital is HMRC's programme for moving tax records and returns into compatible software. It already covers VAT-registered businesses in the UK, and since April 2026 it has also applied to income tax for sole traders and landlords registered for Self Assessment whose qualifying income is above a threshold, which falls over the next years.

If you still file a UK return, for instance on a property you let in Britain, find out on GOV.UK whether Making Tax Digital applies to you, and ask your adviser in Spain for advice so the two calendars do not clash.

What admin really costs your business

It hits you twice: the time it takes, valued at what you could bill, and the paid jobs it pushes out.

How to calculate your own figure

Multiply the number of times you do a task daily by how long it takes and by what your time is worth, then by 240 working days. Start with one task. Value it at what you charge, not at an assistant's wage, because that is what it takes out of your margins. If your partner or other people on your team regularly handle the same task, add their share too. The results show you what to change first; redo the sum every couple of years.

Signs that admin is taking over

  • You bill customers long after finishing the job.
  • Your Sunday goes on catching up with emails.
  • Admin starts to feel like a second job.
  • You never find time for quotes, sales calls and prospects.
  • Bookkeeping spills into your evenings and your work-life balance.

The sales that never happen

The second cost is the money you never earn, and it eats into margins quietly. Admin rarely shows up in the accounts, yet it holds back growth: every evening given to paperwork is one not spent on the sales and marketing that help you grow. Asked how they would use a lighter workload, the entrepreneurs in Time etc's survey put growing sales and marketing first. Those who delegated well were also more likely to see revenue growth over the past two years: a correlation, not proof. Still, the link between this workload and growth comes up in more than one of these studies.

For a plumber in Elche, that could be answering the quote requests that arrive on WhatsApp the same afternoon, following up previous clients before the busy season or updating the content of the business website. These are the things that grow revenue and build customer relationships, and they lose out first when productivity drops.

How to reduce the workload

Organise first, then delegate, and only then hand repetitive tasks to software. The first steps cost little and suit freelancers and teams alike.

Organise your routine

  • Note the tax calendar in January, with reminders before each legal deadline, so nothing gets missed.
  • Book a weekly slot to send bills, file receipts and read notifications.
  • Use a bank account just for the business and photograph each receipt on the spot.
  • Save templates for quotes and repeated emails; WhatsApp Business also has quick replies and away messages.
  • Send reminders for overdue payments on fixed dates; our guide to automated payment chasing explains how.

Good time management is routine, not willpower: batching similar jobs leaves more room for customers.

Use one tool instead of several

One invoicing and accounting program for quotes, invoices and expenses, a folder you share with your gestoría and a backup cover the basics. Search for accounting software built for the Spanish market, which means it prepares IVA and IRPF figures in the format your gestoría expects; some products are designed for particular industries, such as trades or clinics. A common mistake is to subscribe to several apps that do not talk to each other.

Before spending on any new tech products, look at three things:

  • whether the software connects to your bank and exports records your gestoría can read;
  • whether customer support is available in English, if you need it, before you subscribe;
  • whether switching products later would mean retyping everything.

Good software is the one you will actually use: vendors' success stories prove little, so if it offers a free trial, test it with your real billing before making a choice. A digital certificate or Cl@ve lets you complete official procedures online instead of at a local office.

Delegate to a gestoría

A gestoría (an adviser who handles tax and Social Security paperwork) is a service that can handle your registration and file your taxes. Issuing invoices, filing receipts and getting the information to them on schedule remain your job, and so does the responsibility.

When you search for one you can trust, ask a few questions:

  • which services are included, from registration and accounting to quarterly taxes and, if you have staff, payroll;
  • who files each tax and how you get proof of filing;
  • the price of each service, which varies across the market.

If you are just setting up, the Puntos de Atención al Emprendedor (PAE) and your local chamber of commerce offer free advice and support to people starting a business.

When is automation worth it?

When a task meets three conditions:

  • it comes up often;
  • each repetition looks alike;
  • the information is already stored electronically.

A task that takes ten minutes daily does not justify a custom build: the maintenance eats the saving. Fixing the routine first is often faster and cheaper.

Artificial intelligence tools can help write routine replies and first drafts of content, or sort incoming email, as long as someone checks the result before you trust it; they do not replace tax advice. HeyBRB's respondents who had tried these tools also reported their limits: savings limited to minor chores, output too generic for their business, and worries about the GDPR. Judge success by the time you win back, not by the number of tools.

Frequently asked questions

Where does the 226-hour figure come from?
From the Federación Nacional de Asociaciones de Trabajadores Autónomos (ATA), published in August 2026. It covers dealings with public administrations only, not everything else a business has to file, chase and answer.
How much time do UK firms spend on admin?
It depends on what is counted. Research from Sage describes financial administration alone as thirteen months of effort for twelve months of income, and a HeyBRB survey in the UK found eight hours of admin per week. These figures describe the UK, not Spain.
What taxes does an autónomo file every quarter?
In general, a VAT (IVA) return and an income tax (IRPF) instalment, plus withholding returns if they rent business premises, employ staff or hire other professionals. Annual summaries and the income tax return complete the calendar. The Agencia Tributaria publishes the current forms and dates.
