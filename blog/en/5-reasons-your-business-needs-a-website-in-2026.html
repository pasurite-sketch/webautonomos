Does your business really need a website in 2026? Yes, if you want people to find you, trust you and get in touch. Social media and directories help, but your website is the foundation of your online presence: the one place where you decide what customers see. If you're a freelancer or run a small business in Spain, here are five reasons to get online this year, followed by what your website should include and what it costs.

Why does a business need a website?

A business needs a website because it is where prospective customers go to find out who you are, what you offer and how to reach you before making a purchase. That first visit is important: a clear website answers those questions in seconds and turns it into an enquiry, a call or a message. In short, a website for your business brings five things:

  • Visibility: people search online for local businesses, and your website helps you appear in search results.
  • Credibility: a professional website shows that your business is real, active and reliable.
  • Availability: it answers customers and collects enquiries at any time.
  • Competitiveness: a small, local business with a clear website can stand out against bigger companies.
  • Cost: it's affordable marketing that keeps working long after you publish it.

1. Customers search online before they buy

Today, people look for almost everything online, from a plumber in Alicante to a hairdresser in Valencia who opens on Saturdays. If your business has no website, those potential customers simply find your competitors instead, even when you are the better choice.

Show up in search results for what you sell

Search engines show the pages that clearly explain what a business does and where it does it. When your website names your services and your town in plain words, it becomes relevant for the searches your target customers type, not just for your business name. Every website we build includes basic SEO: page titles and texts that mention your service and area, with details that match your business listing on Google.

Turn search traffic into leads

Traffic only matters if visitors take the next step. A visible contact form, a WhatsApp button and a phone number that is easy to find make it simple for people to get in touch, so more of your website traffic turns into leads and, eventually, into sales and revenue.

2. A website gives your business credibility

Before they call, people want to know that your business is real and still trading. A professional website with your name, your area, photos of jobs you've done and clear information about your services builds credibility and trust in your brand.

First impressions and user experience

Design and user experience shape that first impression. A website that loads quickly, reads well on a small screen and makes the next step obvious feels reliable; a slow, cluttered site sends visitors back to the search results. Many of your customers will look you up on their mobile, so we design every website to be easy to read and navigate on any device.

Show your expertise

A website is the place to explain how you do things, which jobs you take on and what customers can expect. That can help people choose you for your expertise rather than for the lowest price.

3. Your business stays open online 24/7

Your website keeps going while you sleep, drive to a job or take a day off. Visitors can read about your services, see the areas you cover and send you a message at any time, which means you don't lose enquiries in the evening or at weekends.

Answer customers without picking up the phone

A short FAQ on your website answers what customers ask you every week: prices, areas covered, how long a job takes. That is good customer service: it saves time for you and your customers, and the people who contact you already understand what you offer. With the contact form, each request reaches your email; with the WhatsApp button, customers can write to you straight from their mobile.

4. Small businesses can stand out against bigger competitors

Online, a local business appears on the same screen as large companies. A neighbourhood electrician with a clear, fast website that explains exactly what they do and where can win the job against a bigger company with a generic page. You don't need a huge budget, just a website that is relevant to the people searching near you.

Website or social media? Your business needs both

Social media is a great way to post news, show your brand and stay in touch with your customer base, but the platform decides who sees your posts and can change its rules at any time. On your website, you decide what visitors see, search engines can find it and it gives every social media post somewhere to send people. We add links to your social media profiles on your website, so visitors can follow you there too. To choose your channels, see our guide to social media for the self-employed.

5. A website is cost-effective marketing

Compared with paid digital ads or printed flyers, a website is a low-cost way to reach people who are already looking for what you offer, and it keeps working for your business month after month.

What should a small business website include?

Your website doesn't need dozens of pages. It needs the essential information customers look for before they get in touch (our guide to what every freelancer's website must have goes into more detail):

  • What you offer: your main services or products, described in plain words.
  • Where you are: your town, the areas you cover and, if you have premises, your address and timetable.
  • How to reach you: your phone, contact form and WhatsApp button, easy to find on every page.
  • Proof: photos of jobs you've completed and a link to your customer reviews.
  • A mobile-friendly design that loads quickly.
  • Legal texts: legal notice, privacy policy and cookie policy.

How to get a website for your business in Spain

There are three main ways to get your website, each with a different cost in money and effort:

Build it yourself

Creating a website yourself with a builder or a platform such as WordPress, Wix or Squarespace is cheap to start with, but you have to learn the tool and look after hosting, updates and security yourself. If you're weighing them up, read Wix or WordPress.

Hire a freelancer or an agency

In Spain, a professional website costs around €800 to €2,000 from a freelancer and €1,500 to €4,000 from an agency, before maintenance (Social Media Pymes, 2026).

Use a done-for-you service

With WebAutonomos, we prepare a free demo of your website in less than 24 hours. You see it before paying anything and only pay once you approve it; if it isn't right for you, it costs nothing and commits you to nothing. Request your free demo.

Legal requirements for freelancers and business websites in Spain

To trade as a freelancer (autónomo) in Spain, you must register with the Tax Agency, using form 036, and with Social Security. Registering properly also opens the door to support schemes for new self-employed workers, and Chambers of Commerce offer free training and advice; our guide on becoming an autónomo in Spain as a foreigner explains the steps. Your website also needs a legal notice, a privacy policy and a cookie policy under Spanish law (LSSI) and the GDPR. We include these legal texts on every website we build; for anything about your own tax or legal situation, ask an adviser.

Where your website fits in your digital marketing

Your website is the base of your online marketing, not the whole plan. A simple way to grow your business online, step by step:

  1. Publish your website and keep the important details, such as prices and services, up to date.
  2. Create or complete your Google Business Profile with the same name, address and phone number as your website.
  3. Use social media to share recent jobs and link back to your website.
  4. Collect the email addresses of customers who agree to it and send useful news now and then.

In 2026, a website is no longer optional for a business that wants to be found. Whatever your industry, from electricians to consultants and local shops, a clear website helps people find you, trust you and call you. The important thing is to start with the essentials, keep your website up to date and let it work for your business.
