Automated payment chasing and what you never get round to

We always measure what we do: the hours spent, the jobs repeated, the time we would like back. But there is another category, invisible to every calculation: what you never do. Not through neglect, but because there was never time. That work costs no hours; it costs revenue that never comes in. Unpaid invoices are a clear example, and automated payment chasing is a good place to start: each customer gets the right reminder at the right moment, until the money arrives.

No payback calculation can see them

An automation calculation always starts from the same place: how many times, how long, what the hour costs. A task that does not exist has none of those three numbers.

Its current cost is zero, so its gain is zero, so it appears nowhere. That is a structural flaw in the method, ours included, and it leaves out precisely the seams that can be very profitable.

Four jobs that get put off

There are four of them, and it is easy to recognise yourself in at least one:

  • Chasing unpaid invoices, done whenever a spare moment turns up, never methodically.
  • Monitoring tenders or grants, never started.
  • Getting back in touch with former customers, put off for two years.
  • Publishing regularly (articles, posts), abandoned after three weeks.

If the first one is on your list, the second half of this article is for you.

What they actually cost

A reminder that goes out on time, without relying on your memory, helps you get paid faster. A former client you call again already knows your work: winning them back takes less effort than convincing a stranger.

Tender monitoring puts you in front of contracts you would never have bid for. None of this is time saved: it is revenue for your business.

An unpaid invoice also hurts twice: you have already done the job and often already paid the VAT (IVA) on it. And the older a debt gets, the less likely the money is to be recovered.

What overdue payments do to your cash flow

Every invoice you issue after the job is credit you give your customer until the due date; accountants call the total accounts receivable, and the more it grows, the longer you wait for income already earned. Following up overdue invoices on a fixed schedule can help improve your cash flow and make it more predictable.

They are easier to automate than you would think

It may come as a surprise, but there is a logic to it. Automating existing processes means reproducing them faithfully, with their habits, exceptions and special cases piled up over years.

A task that does not exist has none of that: no prior process to respect, nobody to persuade to change their ways, no regression to fear. You start from a blank page. Debt collection is a case in point: if you chase payments whenever you remember, there is no existing process to copy.

How to spot yours

Ask yourself one question and write down the first answer that comes: what do you know you ought to be doing for your business, and never do for lack of time?

Many owners answer within seconds. They know. It is not a diagnosis problem but a prioritisation problem, and the priority goes to whatever is urgent.

For the finance side, there is a more concrete test: count your overdue invoices. If you cannot give the figure without looking it up, you have found your first candidate.

Why the usual calculation falls short here

For an existing task we price it, compare it, and say yes or no against a threshold. For a task that does not exist there is nothing to compare: you have to estimate future revenue, which is judgement rather than arithmetic. We would rather say so than dress an intuition up as a table.

How automatic payment chasing works

Automatic payment chasing means a system watches your unpaid and overdue invoices and sends each customer, at each stage, the reminder you have prepared.

What matters is that it stops as soon as the payment arrives and alerts you when a debt needs your attention: one reminder sent to a client who has already paid can damage a business relationship. Many English-language guides on chasing late payments are written for UK businesses, with UK law in mind; this one is for freelancers and small businesses based in Spain.

What you need before automating anything

  • Your invoice data in one place (amount, due date, payment status), so the system can follow each bill until it is paid.
  • For business clients, the contact details of the people who approve payment, often the accounts department.
  • Your payment terms in writing from the quote, within the legal payment periods that apply in Spain.

Keep that data up to date, partial payments included, then set your credit control policy: when to remind, how firmly, when to escalate.

Which tools can handle the reminders?

Many invoicing programs and accounting software packages include the management of unpaid invoices: they record payments and can issue reminders on a schedule you set. If yours offers it, start there.

If not, simple tools connected to your list of invoices can handle the friendly stage, as our guide to how far no-code tools can take you explains. An AI assistant can also draft each reminder for you to check (see AI tools for freelancers).

Reminders before, on and after the due date

An effective sequence begins before the due date and raises the pressure step by step. This basic approach suits any type of client:

Moment What you send Tone
A few days before the due date An email with the amount, the date and how to pay Informative
On the due date A message with the invoice attached Neutral
A few days later A second reminder asking whether there are any problems Friendly
If the invoice is still overdue A call from you and, if appropriate, a payment plan Personal
Final notice A formal demand in writing Formal

The first three stages can go out automatically. The last two depend on the client's reply: you decide them case by case.

What each payment reminder should say

  • The key details: invoice number, amount owed and due date.
  • What is needed to pay straight away: your bank account or a payment link.
  • A contact person, in case of questions or mistakes.

Email, SMS or a messaging app?

Email is the usual channel: the invoice travels as an attachment and leaves a written record. An SMS or a messaging app suits a friendly reminder for customers who already write to you that way (see how to set up WhatsApp Business for free). Regardless of the channel, keep a copy of everything you send.

A firm tone that keeps the business relationship

A late payment is not always bad faith: sometimes the email ended up in spam, or the client is going through a difficult patch. The first reminders assume an oversight and make it easy to pay quickly; the wording gets firmer only if there is no reply, never with threats.

A plumber in Valencia does not write to the company that sends him jobs every month as he would to a private customer.

Manual chasing vs automatic reminders

By hand, reminders depend on your memory and your mood; automatic ones follow a schedule. In a larger business, a finance team handles this every week. As a freelancer, it competes with everything else on your list. Here is how the two compare:

  • Timing: manual reminders go out when you find a moment; automatic ones go out on the planned day.
  • Wording: a message written in a rush may sound too sharp; a prepared text stays professional and protects your client relationships.
  • Follow-up: a system can match incoming payments with invoices and spot the overdue ones; by hand, it is easy to lose track.
  • Judgement: a person can tell an oversight from a genuine difficulty and negotiate; software cannot.

Many freelancers struggle to ask for what they are owed, which is often why the follow-up never happens.

Automating the first reminders removes the awkward part, and over a year the record of payments shows each client's payment patterns.

From friendly reminders to court

When late payments persist, claiming the debt starts to have legal consequences. From this point, nothing should go out automatically.

The formal demand, the last step before court

The out-of-court stage ends with a formal demand: a letter identifying the outstanding debt, the invoices it covers and your deadline for payment, sent by a method that proves content and delivery, such as a burofax.

The monitorio procedure

If the formal demand gets no result, the courts remain. In Spain, monetary debts you can prove with documents (invoices, delivery notes, signed quotes) can be claimed through the procedimiento monitorio: the creditor asks the court to require payment from the debtor.

If the debtor neither pays nor objects, you can ask the court to enforce payment; if the debtor objects, the case becomes a trial. You can file the initial application yourself with those documents, but if the debtor objects, it is advisable to have a lawyer.

When to refer the debt to a lawyer

Ask a lawyer for advice when the debtor disputes the service, when the amount justifies a court claim, or when you are unsure of the steps. They will also check whether the debtor has assets: a judgment is worth little if there is nothing to collect.

Debt collection agencies also take on debt recovery for a commission; research and compare their fees and options before you sign.

What the system must not decide for you

Hand over the reminders, not the decisions.

A tool cannot tell whether a late payment hides a difficult patch or a simple mistake, or whether your business is better off agreeing to payments in instalments than opening a dispute. You stay in control of these:

  • Negotiating a deferral with the client, in writing.
  • Escalating from the friendly stage to the formal demand, then to court.
  • Stopping the reminders if the client disputes the service, until it is resolved.
  • Protecting the debtor's privacy: never publicise a debt or discuss it with third parties, as you could infringe their rights.

Frequently asked questions

Which of the four jobs should I start with?
With your unpaid invoices, in general: a reminder schedule helps you get paid faster and works from documents you already have. The other three jobs can come afterwards.
Can you chase overdue payments automatically without losing the customer?
Yes, if only the friendly reminders run on their own and you watch the tone: a first late payment is often an oversight. What damages trust is pressing for a debt already paid, or getting stern too soon.
What documents do I need to claim an unpaid invoice?
The invoice, the signed quote with your payment terms or the accepted order, proof of delivery, the emails in which the customer acknowledges the service and copies of your reminders. Together, they help you prove the debt if the case goes to court.
When should I refer a debt to a lawyer?
When the customer ignores the formal demand, disputes the service, or the amount justifies the cost of court proceedings. The lawyer will say whether a monitorio claim fits and whether the debtor has assets to cover the debt.
Why doesn't your free diagnostic price the jobs you never do?
Because it starts from the current cost of a task, and a task that does not exist has none: the result would be zero gain and an infinite payback period. Rather than rig a coefficient, we prefer to say this category falls outside the tool's scope.
Does automating a job you never do cost more than ordinary automation?
Not necessarily: there is no existing process to respect, so you build the one you want from scratch. The technical effort is similar and the analysis phase is shorter.
How do I measure results if I'm starting from zero?
With a business indicator, not hours saved: the average time it takes to get paid for payment reminders, the number of bids submitted for tender monitoring, the replies to a reactivation campaign. Set your baseline before you begin.
What if I have none of these jobs pending?
Then your organisation is ahead on this, and the ordinary diagnostic will do: it prices a repetitive task you already do and tells you whether automating it pays off, which is not always the case.
