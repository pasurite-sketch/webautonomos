Creating a coaching website comes down to a few steps: define your niche, choose between a website builder and a done-for-you service, plan a handful of key pages, write your content in your own voice and give every visitor one clear next step. As a coach, what you sell is trust: today, many potential clients look you up online before booking a first conversation and decide whether you're the right person for them. Below you'll find each step, for coaches based in Spain who work in Valencia, in Alicante or online.

Why does a coach need their own website?

Your own website matters because potential clients check you out before they book, and it's the one place online where you decide what they find: your approach, your programmes and how to get started. Instagram and LinkedIn make you visible; your website is where your online presence comes together.

Social media is rented ground

On a social network, you don't control the algorithm, your followers or the rules, and a platform can decide overnight who sees your posts. On your own site, you decide what visitors read first: your full method, your services and one clear action, without distractions.

Your site answers questions while you're in sessions

While you're with a client, your site keeps answering what potential clients ask before they book: how sessions run, what they cost and whether they're in English or Spanish. Someone trying to find a coach late in the evening can explore your programmes and message you directly.

How to create a coaching website, step by step

Seven steps take you from a blank screen to a site that brings in enquiries. None of them needs technical skills; what takes time is getting clear on your audience and your message.

  1. Define your niche: your ideal client and whether you meet in person, online or both.
  2. Choose how to build it: website builder, freelancer, agency or done-for-you service.
  3. Plan your pages: home, about, coaching services, testimonials, resources and contact.
  4. Create your content and choose a design that reflects your brand.
  5. Show your credibility with real testimonials and clear limits.
  6. Add a call to action everywhere: WhatsApp, a discovery call or a booking link.
  7. Publish your site, help people find it and keep it up to date.

Step one: define your niche and your coaching format

Before you create anything or browse templates, write down who your ideal client is, what they want to achieve and how you'll support them. A website that tries to speak to everyone connects with no one.

Choose your niche and your ideal client

Life coaching, business coaching, executive coaching and career coaching attract very different audiences. Describe your ideal client in one or two sentences: their situation, their goal and what holds them back today.

It could read: 'professionals who have moved to Valencia and want to relaunch their career in Spain'. Everything you write afterwards should speak directly to that person.

In person, online or both?

Your format shapes what your site has to explain. If your sessions take place in Alicante or Elche, show where and how to get there. If you coach online, reassure someone who has never met you: how video sessions run, which languages you use and which time zones you cover.

Many coaches in Spain offer both, along with workshops or online courses, for an audience here or abroad, in English or Spanish. Each format raises different questions, so your site should answer them clearly:

Format What your website should explain
One-to-one coaching in person Where sessions take place, how to get there, session length and price
Online coaching The video call tools you use, what participants need and your time zone
Group coaching and workshops Dates, places available and who it's for
Online courses What participants will learn, how long it takes and a link to the platform where the course is hosted
Executive and team coaching The format and how you coordinate with the organisation

Step two: choose how to build your coaching website

There are three ways to build a coaching website: do it yourself with a website builder, hire a freelancer or an agency, or use a done-for-you service. The right choice depends on your time, your budget and how much you enjoy technology.

Build it yourself with a website builder

Popular website builders such as Wix and Squarespace let you start from a template and edit your pages in the browser, with hosting included in the subscription. WordPress is open-source software that you install on hosting you rent and extend with themes and plugins. These tools often include templates for coaches, which can save time at the start.

When you browse templates, look past the demo photos: pick one with a clear home page, room for reviews and a visible booking button.

What to keep in mind before building a coaching website yourself:

  • You write every text, choose every photo and connect your domain yourself.
  • You need time to learn the tools; with WordPress, you also look after updates, backups and security.
  • Templates are only a starting point: adapt the colours, photos and wording so your site doesn't look like other coaches' websites.

Step three: plan your pages

A coaching website doesn't need dozens of pages. A handful of well-organised pages is enough, whether you run your practice alone or as part of a team:

  • Home: your ideal client, what you offer and the next step.
  • About: your story, your training and your experience.
  • Coaching services: each programme, with what it includes.
  • Testimonials: what real clients say, published with their permission.
  • Blog or resources: articles, videos and free tools that showcase your expertise.
  • Contact: your phone number, WhatsApp and, if you use one, a link to your booking tool.

Home: a clear promise

Your home page should make clear at a glance who you support, with what and what visitors can do now. A line such as 'I help new managers in Valencia lead their team with confidence' connects better than 'Coaching for your transformation'. Below it, place one button with the next step, such as booking a first conversation.

About: your story, training and experience

Tell your personal story and share your education, your coaching certification and where you trained, and the areas you focus on, such as leadership, communication or career development. If you belong to a professional body, say which one.

Add a recent, natural photo of yourself and end with a link to your services, so readers know what to do next.

Programmes and prices

Showcase each programme in its own section: who it's for, what's included, how many sessions, how long it lasts and, if you can, an approximate price. If your coaching business offers several lines, such as business coaching and career coaching, explain the benefits of each in plain words.

Contact details and frequent questions

Answer the usual questions (price, duration, online or in person, how to reschedule) in a short FAQ, and make sure anyone can find your phone number and WhatsApp from any part of the site.

Step four: create your content and choose your design

Your content makes visitors feel understood; your design makes it easy to read. Write first, then choose a look that fits your words and your brand.

Write in your own voice

Write the way you talk in your sessions, and use your clients' words rather than jargon. Avoid generic lines that any other coach could sign. Short paragraphs, clear headings and one idea per section make your pages easy to scan, especially on a mobile, where many of your clients will look you up.

If writing isn't your thing, these tips on creating website content when you're not a writer will help.

Explain your coaching process

Describe your process from start to finish, so the first step feels less daunting:

  1. A first conversation to understand the client's goals.
  2. A series of sessions, with their approximate number and frequency.
  3. Exercises or reflection between sessions.
  4. A final review of progress and next steps.

Photos, colours and branding

As a coach, your name is part of your business, so your personal brand matters as much as a logo. Use recent, professional photos of you coaching. Keep the same colours, fonts and tone on your website and your social profiles so your branding can stay consistent, and make your Instagram bio link to your main programme.

Step five: show your credibility with real testimonials and clear limits

People who hire a coach aren't buying hours; they're looking for support with something that matters to them. Building their trust comes down to details: your voice, your experience on show and clear limits.

Real testimonials, published with permission

Testimonials from real clients are valuable social proof. Ask each person to share a few lines about working with you, tell them where their words will appear and save their permission in writing. Publish what they wrote without editing it, with the name or initials they agree to, and remove a testimonial if the person asks. Never invent one, and leave out figures or results you can't prove.

EU consumer rules also ask you to explain whether and how you check that the reviews on your site come from real clients, for example in a short note next to your testimonials. On the sites we build, we add that note.

Coaching is not therapy

Coaching isn't a health profession: don't say you treat anxiety or any other disorder, and don't talk about therapy, patients or healing. If someone needs psychological help, refer them to a health professional. Our article on websites for psychologists and therapists shows how different their rules are.

No promises you can't keep

Avoid lines such as 'transform your life in a month' or 'success assured'. Explain your approach and how your sessions run instead: each person moves at their own pace, and the outcome depends on them as much as on you.

Step six: turn visitors into clients

Everything on your site should lead to one action: booking a discovery call, downloading a free resource or messaging you on WhatsApp. A clear call to action, repeated several times, is easier to follow than a single button hidden at the bottom.

One call to action, repeated

Use the same button text everywhere, such as 'Book your first conversation', and place it near the top of your home page, after your services and at the end of your articles. Make the first step easy and low-pressure: a short call, not a long commitment.

A link to the booking tool you already use

If you already manage your calendar with an online booking system such as Calendly, a button that gives direct access to it is all your site needs: clients pick a slot and book directly. With WebAutonomos, we add it as a simple link, and enquiries still reach you by email and on WhatsApp. To compare booking tools, read our article on online booking systems for small businesses.

A free first conversation and a short enquiry form

If you offer a free discovery call, let people know how long it lasts and what happens next. Keep the booking process light and ask only for what you need: a name, how to reach them and, if they like, the programme they're interested in. Each extra field can put people off.

A lead magnet to grow your mailing list

A lead magnet is one of those free resources: an exercise, a checklist or a short video that visitors receive in exchange for their email address. A thank-you page delivers it and suggests the next step, and email marketing tools can send it automatically.

Building a list is slow, but it lets you build a relationship, stay in touch and send offers to those who agreed to receive them; our beginner's guide to email marketing explains how to start.

Step seven: publish, get found and keep your site up to date

Publishing is the start, not the end. Once your coaching website is live, help search engines understand it and update it whenever something changes.

Basic SEO: your service and your area

Give each page its own title and description, a main heading with your service and texts that answer what your clients look for. For in-person sessions, include your town in titles and texts: it helps you appear when someone types 'life coach Valencia' or 'executive coaching Alicante'.

If you coach online, pick a few keywords by niche, such as 'career coach for expats', and give each one its own page. Every website we build includes basic SEO: titles and texts with your service and your area, and details that match your Google listing.

Data protection and cookies

Coaches hear about personal matters, and your site collects people's details when they write to you or download your lead magnet. Common website mistakes coaches make

These mistakes are common among coaches and push away people who were about to get in touch:

  1. Trying to speak to everyone and connecting with no one.
  2. Filling the site with text and no clear call to action.
  3. Copying other coaches' messaging instead of using your own voice.
  4. Hiding your prices or the way to book.
  5. Leaving workshop dates that have already passed.
  6. Publishing testimonials without permission, or promising results that don't depend on you.
  7. Using templates without replacing their stock photos and placeholder text.
  8. Forgetting to check the site on a mobile.
