Mandatory e-invoicing in Spain: what freelancers need to know

E-invoicing (electronic invoicing) is becoming mandatory in Spain. Under the Crea y Crece Law, companies and freelancers will gradually have to switch to electronic invoices when they bill other businesses and professionals; sales to private customers are not covered by this mandate. This guide explains what changes for freelancers and small firms, when it will apply to you, which free tools can help with compliance and which mistakes to avoid.

What is e-invoicing in Spain?

An e-invoice is an invoice in a structured, machine-readable format, such as Facturae or UBL, that the client's system can record without anyone retyping the data. In Spain, the Crea y Crece Law, approved in 2022, will make e-invoicing mandatory between businesses and professionals.

What Crea y Crece requires

It sets three requirements:

  • a machine-readable electronic format;
  • delivery through a digital channel;
  • reporting the status of each e-invoice, such as accepted or paid.

The aim is to improve transparency, reduce fraud and fight late payment.

Who has to comply

  • Covered: businesses and professionals of any size, including freelancers, in their dealings with one another; only the start date changes. If you are still setting up, see our guide to becoming an autónomo as a foreigner.
  • Not covered: invoices to private customers, although a separate rule for billing systems can still apply to them.

Why PDFs don't count

A PDF sent by email is not an e-invoice under Crea y Crece, even with a digital signature. It works like a photo of the document: a person can read it, but a computer cannot reliably extract its content.

Timeline: when will e-invoicing be mandatory?

E-invoicing will become mandatory in two phases, based on annual turnover:

  1. first, large companies, with a turnover above 8 million euros a year;
  2. one year later, all other businesses and freelancers.

The rollout is phased: the compliance deadlines count from the date the implementing regulations take effect, which has been postponed more than once. Check the latest dates on the website of the Spanish Tax Agency (AEAT) or with your gestor, and bear in mind that some business clients may ask you to switch sooner.

Verifactu vs e-invoicing: two separate compliance obligations

Verifactu sets requirements for the invoicing software you work with, whoever your client is; the e-invoicing mandate sets how invoices between businesses must be produced and exchanged. You may have to comply with both.

What Verifactu asks of your software

It comes from a royal decree on invoicing systems. A compliant system:

  • creates an unalterable billing record for each invoice, chained to the previous one, and adds a QR code to the document;
  • either sends the records to the AEAT or keeps them under stricter security requirements;
  • comes with a declaración responsable in which the manufacturer self-certifies compliance.

It does not apply to taxpayers who keep their VAT records through the SII, or to those taxed under the Basque or Navarre regional systems, which have their own rules (TicketBAI in the Basque Country). It also has its own calendar, which has moved too, so check it before planning.

The SII: real-time VAT reporting

Spain's SII (Suministro Inmediato de Información) is a separate VAT reporting system that works in near real-time: billing records go to the AEAT within four days, weekends and national holidays excluded. The SII is mainly mandatory for large companies and a few other groups; other taxpayers can join voluntarily. If in doubt, ask your gestor.

What does a valid e-invoice need?

A valid e-invoice must use an accepted format, include the usual details, ensure its authenticity and integrity, and be kept for the required period.

How machine-readable formats work

A structured format stores each piece of data (issuer, tax ID, description, taxable base, VAT and total) in its own field, usually inside an XML file. Facturae, Spain's own format, also used for public administrations, and UBL, an international standard, are both XML formats.

Required details and storage

Like any invoice, an e-invoice must include:

  • its number and date;
  • the tax ID (NIF) and address of the issuer and the client;
  • a description of the service;
  • the taxable base, the VAT rate and amount, and the total.

You have to keep it for at least four years for tax purposes; ask your gestor whether you need to keep it longer.

How e-invoicing between businesses will work

The system works like this:

  1. the issuer creates the e-invoice in its system;
  2. it sends the file through a digital channel, such as an e-invoicing platform;
  3. the client receives it and records it without retyping.

The law provides that platforms from different providers must be interoperable, so that an e-invoice issued on one platform can be received on another; the technical requirements will be set by the implementing regulations. With public administrations, the circuit is different: the e-invoice goes through FACe, the official platform for the public sector, as a Facturae file with an electronic signature.

Reporting the status of each e-invoice

As well as delivering it, you will have to report its status, for example whether it has been accepted or paid; the regulations will detail how this communication works.

Free e-invoicing tools and how to choose one

With a low volume, free solutions can be enough to start.

Free options

  • FACe: free and secure, but only for public administrations.
  • The AEAT's free app: the Tax Agency offers a free invoicing application designed for professionals and freelancers with few invoices. It is designed for Verifactu compliance, but check whether it will also support e-invoicing between businesses before you rely on it.
  • Programs with free plans: limited in documents, users or features, they may be enough for a freelancer.

Compliance checklist for your invoicing tool

Before choosing, check that the tool:

  • can support the accepted formats;
  • lets you deliver the files to clients and public bodies;
  • has its manufacturer's declaración responsable and adds the QR code;
  • keeps you informed of the status of each e-invoice;
  • lets you export your data if you ever change tools.

How to issue your first e-invoice

The process, from certificate to storage:

  1. Get your digital certificate (for individuals, the FNMT issues it free of charge).
  2. Choose a tool that meets the checklist above.
  3. Set up your tax details and those of your clients.
  4. Create the e-invoice with the description, amount and VAT.
  5. For a public administration, sign it electronically from the tool itself.
  6. Send it: through FACe to a public body, or through the digital channel you agree on with a business.
  7. Keep it for the required period.

Common mistakes that can invalidate an e-invoice

  • Treating signed PDFs as e-invoices: they are not machine-readable.
  • Forgetting the electronic signature, or emailing the file, when you work for a public body.
  • Using incomplete or incorrect tax details (tax ID, address), which forces you to correct the document.
  • Not keeping your invoices for the required period, which can lead to penalties in an inspection.
  • Assuming that a system that meets the software requirements already handles e-invoicing: the two are separate.

What happens if you don't comply?

The rules provide for penalties for failing to meet the e-invoicing obligation; check the current amounts with the AEAT or your adviser. Other consequences:

  • a public administration may delay payment until you submit the e-invoice properly;
  • business clients may ask you to correct invoices that don't comply.

How to prepare for Spain's e-invoicing mandate

You don't need to wait for the final dates. A short compliance checklist:

  • list who you bill: businesses, private customers or public bodies;
  • ask your provider whether its program is ready for e-invoicing and has its declaración responsable;
  • get your digital certificate if you don't have one yet;
  • ensure each client's tax ID and address are correct;
  • agree with your gestor on when to switch, based on the latest official dates.

Clients in France and other EU countries

Spain is not the only country adopting e-invoicing. France has its own mandate, explained in our guide to e-invoicing in France, and the EU's VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package will introduce digital reporting, based on e-invoicing, for cross-border trade within the EU.

Adapting to e-invoicing is not complicated: with your certificate and a suitable tool, you can prepare today, while there is no pressure.

E-invoicing in Spain: FAQ

Is a digitally signed PDF invoice valid?
Not between businesses once the e-invoicing mandate reaches you: you need a structured file, such as Facturae or UBL, that a program can read.
When does e-invoicing become mandatory for freelancers in Spain?
In stages: first large businesses, with a turnover above 8 million euros, and one year later everyone else, including freelancers. The dates depend on implementing regulations that have been postponed more than once, so check them with the AEAT or your gestor.
Do I need to send e-invoices to private customers?
No. The Crea y Crece mandate covers invoices between businesses and professionals, not sales to private customers. If you use a billing program, Verifactu, a separate rule, can still apply to them.
Which free e-invoicing tool can I use?
With a low volume, a program with a free plan may be enough, or the Spanish Tax Agency's free app. If you only work for public administrations, FACe is free. Check that the tool can produce a machine-readable e-invoice.
Do I need an accountant or adviser for e-invoicing compliance?
It is not mandatory, but an adviser can support you at first, for example to check which obligations apply to you. Once the tool is set up, issuing an e-invoice is as fast as a traditional one.
What about PDF invoices I have already issued?
They remain valid under the previous regime. The obligation only applies to new invoices from the date that corresponds to you.
