Google is removing Questions and Answers from your Business Profile: what changes and what to do

Have you opened your Google profile and can no longer find the Questions and Answers? It's not your fault. Google first withdrew the API that let other software manage them and then began gradually removing that public section from Google Business Profile. It is now turning to answers generated by artificial intelligence. Your customers, however, still have the same questions before they call you. In this article we explain exactly what has changed and what you should do on Google and on your website so that they keep finding clear information at the moment they make their decision.

In short

  • First, Google withdrew the API that let other software manage this section and announced that it was updating the feature.
  • Then the public section began to disappear gradually: you can no longer add or manage questions, and the existing ones are no longer shown.
  • Google is now focusing on artificial intelligence (Gemini), with a feature called Ask Maps that answers searchers' questions and is not available everywhere.
  • Your part: complete your details on Google and use your website to answer the questions that used to be answered there.

What has happened to Questions and Answers on Google Business Profile

Google is phasing them out: first it shut down the API that other software used to manage this section, and then it began removing the section bit by bit. Here is what happened, step by step.

How the section worked

It was a section of Google My Business, now called Google Business Profile, where any user could ask a public question about a business: the opening hours, whether there was parking, whether you needed an appointment… The tricky part was that anyone could answer, not just the owner. That's why many businesses posted their own frequently asked questions and answered them directly, so that the first answer on view was the right one.

First, the API was withdrawn

The first thing Google did was withdraw the Questions and Answers API from its business platform, in other words the technical route through which other software, such as the tools some agencies use, could read and answer those questions. In its official change log, which gives the exact date, Google announced that it was updating this feature. If an agency that manages your Google Business Profile used an external tool to answer questions, that route no longer exists.

The public section is gradually disappearing

Next, Google began gradually removing the public Questions and Answers section from listings. Businesses can no longer add or manage questions, and those already published are no longer shown. Because the change is rolling out bit by bit, don't be surprised if a few traces remain in some cases and nothing at all is left in others.

What was already published

Everything that was published stops being shown, both what you wrote and what other users left. If you had useful answers (your summer opening hours, whether you make home visits, where to park), don't write them off: if you can still see them, copy them, and in any case move that information to your website and to the fields of your listing, as we explain below.

Why it affects you if you run a local business

It affects you because your customers still have questions before they get in touch. What changes is where they find the answer when they search: in the details you publish on Google, on your website and, wherever it has already arrived, in the answers Google's AI gives.

Your customers' questions haven't gone away

Before calling a plumber in Elche or booking at a hairdresser's in Alicante, people want to know the usual things: whether you work in their area, what time you open, whether you take cards, whether they need an appointment or how long a service takes. They search, compare the top results and choose. The section disappearing doesn't make those questions go away: if they can't find the answer quickly, it's easy for them to move on to another result without ever contacting you.

What people see when they look you up on Google

When someone searches for something like "electrician in Valencia", Google shows them business listings in the search results and on Google Maps, with opening hours, photos and customer reviews. It's often the first thing your potential customers see of you, even before your website, so what they find there should be complete and relevant.

The information you publish yourself carries more weight

What other users replied wasn't always reliable or up to date: old opening hours or a service you no longer offer could stay on view for a long time. With AI-generated content, what counts is the information it starts from. If your details are incomplete or contradict each other, nobody is going to correct them for you; if they are complete and up to date, they work in your favour.

What is replacing Questions and Answers on Google

Google is now relying on artificial intelligence to answer people who search. And if you have a shop, there's something else worth knowing: Google may ask you about what you sell.

Gemini takes over

Instead of waiting for another user to reply, Google is betting on answers generated by Gemini, its artificial intelligence. The feature is called Ask Maps and it answers users' questions based on the information in the business profile, its reviews, its photos and its website. If someone asks, for example, whether your workshop in Elda opens on Saturdays, the answer will be based on that information.

It isn't available everywhere

The feature was first launched in some countries and has not been rolled out everywhere. To find out whether you can already use it, open Google Maps on your phone, run a search and check whether it appears; bear in mind that Google changes its platform from time to time and that button names may vary. Even if you don't see it, get your details ready anyway: they are the same details read by anyone who finds you on Google.

What you control and what you don't

You don't decide what the AI says, but you do decide what information you publish: if your details are complete and your website is clear, you give it accurate material; if they are half-finished or out of date, quite the opposite. You don't write the reviews, but you can still influence them by providing a good service and asking satisfied customers for a rating.

Yes-or-no questions about your products

If you have a shop, Google may ask you directly, as the owner, quick yes-or-no questions about what you sell; for example, whether you stock a particular product. They appear in the Products section of your profile, as Google explains on its official help page. Only answer what is true: if you say you have hiking boots that you don't actually sell, anyone who comes in to buy them will leave disappointed.

What to do now on your Google Business Profile

Complete your categories and add your products and services, move common questions to your website, reply to reviews, keep your photos and posts up to date, check that your details match everywhere and see what Google shows about your business. Here are the steps, in order.

Complete your categories, services and products

Your primary category tells Google what you do, and the secondary ones what else you do; we explain how to choose the right categories. Then add your services with names anyone can understand ("boiler repair" rather than trade jargon like "SAT") and, if you sell products, add what you have in store as well, with a photo and price. In local searches, a complete listing helps make it clear what you do and where.

Check your attributes, opening hours and description

On the platform you can also add attributes (accessibility, payment methods, whether there's Wi-Fi…), which answer at a glance questions people used to have to ask; the ones Google offers depend on your category. Also give your real opening hours, including special hours for public holidays, and write a clear description: what you do, who you do it for and which area you cover. If you want to go through it all calmly, follow our tips for optimising your listing.

Create an FAQ page on your website

What you used to be able to answer on Google now has its natural home on your website: there, no other user answers for you, you can go into as much detail as you need and respond directly to common questions. Your website is also one of the sources Google's AI relies on, so explaining yourself clearly there can help it get things right when it talks about your business. We explain how to do it further down.

Reply to every review, including the good ones

Your customers' reviews are another of those sources, and something people read before making up their minds. When you get a notification about a new one, reply straight away or as soon as you can: thank the person who shares their experience, with a personal touch, and when faced with criticism reply calmly and stick to the facts; you'll find ideas in our guide to replying to negative reviews. If your reply can clear up a common question ("Yes, we're also open on Saturday mornings"), make the most of it: anyone reading the reviews will see your clarification too, and every public interaction is a shop window. And to keep new reviews coming in, follow these strategies for getting more reviews.

Keep your photos and posts up to date

Photos show what a question can't make clear: what your premises look like, where the entrance is, what your best work looks like. Upload real, recent photos to improve first impressions, and use posts to announce news, seasonal opening hours or offers. You'll find more ideas in our guides on which photos to upload to your listing and on Google posts.

Check that your details match everywhere

Your business name, address and phone number (what is known in local SEO as NAP) must be identical on Google, on your website, on your social media and in the directories where you appear. If a customer finds two different phone numbers, they won't know which one is right, and that affects your reputation. We explain this in detail when we talk about local citations and NAP.

Search for your business the way a customer would

Every so often, spend a few minutes searching for your workshop, shop or office on Google and Google Maps from your phone, and go through the results putting yourself in the shoes of someone discovering you for the first time: is everything clear? Is anything missing? If Ask Maps is already available where you are, ask it what your customers ask you and pay attention to what it replies. If something isn't right, correct the information at its source. And if someone else manages your presence on Google, make it clear who is in charge of this check. If you're looking for more local visibility, other articles explain how to appear on Google Maps and what the Google Map Pack is.

How to prepare your website to answer your customers' questions

Prepare your website with a clear FAQ page, details identical to those on your listing and text that makes sense on its own. It should answer users' basic questions without anyone having to call you, and it offers something Google doesn't give you: space to explain yourself. It's the part of your online presence that you control completely; if you're just starting out, first go over the elements every freelancer's website should have.

Get ideas from what people already ask you

  • The questions you answer over and over every week by phone or on WhatsApp, such as "I'd like to know if you work on Saturdays".
  • The enquiries you receive by email or through the form on your website.
  • What people ask you at the counter before buying.
  • What comes up again and again in your customers' comments.
  • What you had published on your listing, if you still have it: you can reuse the same text.

Ideas by type of business

Adapt them to what you do and add your own; if you need more ideas, think about what anyone should know before hiring you: all of that should be just a click away.

How to write the answers

Write each question the way a caller would ask it or the way they would type it into a search ("Do you do emergency repairs in Valencia?") and answer in the first sentence with the concrete information (times, areas, payment methods), giving the same level of detail you would over the phone. You can use the words your customers use to find you instead of your own jargon; our guide to local keywords explains how to choose them. If you get several enquiries about the same thing, or simply notice that a question keeps coming up, give it a fuller answer. Make sure your website and Google say the same thing

If you ever change your opening hours, a service or your phone number on Google, change it on your website too, and vice versa: if the two say different things, anyone comparing them is left unsure and stops trusting you. To help search engines read your details correctly, your website can also include LocalBusiness structured data with your name, address and opening hours. And if you want it to gain visibility in local searches, follow these steps to rank your website on Google.

End every answer with a way to contact you

End each answer by saying how to contact you: a WhatsApp button, your phone number or the contact form. That way, users can message you directly. If lots of questions still reach you by message, save quick replies in WhatsApp Business so you can answer in seconds.

Mistakes to avoid

With this change, some habits no longer work. Watch out for these mistakes: avoiding them takes little time and helps you stand out from the competition in your area.

On Google

  • Still looking for a place to post your FAQs on Google: that option no longer exists.
  • Still paying for a tool to manage that section of your listing: Google withdrew the API that made it possible.
  • Filling in only the basics: a generic category, no services or products, no attributes or no public holiday hours.
  • Answering "yes" to a question about something you don't sell.
  • Ignoring reviews or replying to them in an unfriendly tone.
  • Never checking what Google shows about you in the results.

On your website

  • Not having an FAQ page, or filling it with vague phrases such as "it depends, give us a call".
  • Having opening hours, a phone number or an address that differ from those on Google.
  • Repeating your trade and your town in every sentence until the text sounds artificial.

Questions and Answers are disappearing from Google, but your customers' questions remain, and your listing is still a free tool for answering them. What used to be answered there now depends on complete details, a clear website and everything matching everywhere. Set aside an afternoon to improve your details and, after that, a few minutes every now and then to review them. Frequently asked questions

Have Questions and Answers disappeared from Google Business Profile?
They are disappearing gradually. Google is phasing out the Questions and Answers section from profiles: businesses can no longer add or manage questions, and those already published are no longer shown. Before that, it had withdrawn the API for this feature.
What happens to the questions already on my listing?
They stop being shown as Google removes the section, and you can no longer manage them. If any of them answered a common question, move that information to your website and to the details Google displays, such as your opening hours, services or attributes.
What is Ask Maps?
It's a Google feature that answers users' questions with responses generated by Gemini, Google's artificial intelligence, based on each business's profile information, reviews, photos and website. It was launched first in some countries and can't be used everywhere, so check on Google Maps whether it already appears for you.
How can I influence what the AI says about my business?
You don't decide what it says, but you do decide what information you publish: a complete, up-to-date profile, real photos and a clear website with your details and answers to common questions. Your customers' reviews count too, so reply to all of them and ask satisfied customers to share their experience. If you spot incorrect information, correct it at its source.
Should I have an FAQ section on my website?
Yes, it's highly recommended. Your website is the place where you can answer in writing the questions you used to be able to answer on Google, without other users deleting them or answering for you. It's also one of the sources that Gemini, Google's artificial intelligence, relies on to answer.
What questions does Google ask me about my products?
If you have a shop, Google may ask you quick yes-or-no questions about what you sell, which appear in the Products section of your profile. Only answer what is true, because people may come to your shop relying on that answer to make their purchase.
Can I still reply to my customers on Google?
Yes, through reviews: the platform lets you reply to each one. What you can no longer do is use the old Questions and Answers section, which Google is phasing out.
