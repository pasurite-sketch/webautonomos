Have you opened your Google Business Profile and can no longer see the Q&A section? It isn't your fault: Google is removing it from Business Profiles and now relies on answers generated by artificial intelligence. Your customers still need the same information before they call you. Here is what has changed and what to do on your Business Profile and your website.

In short

  • Google withdrew the Q&A API, then began removing the public section gradually: you can no longer add or manage content there, and existing entries are no longer shown.
  • Google now relies on Gemini, its artificial intelligence, with a feature that responds to searchers and isn't available everywhere.
  • Your part: complete your Business Profile, keep it updated and cover the usual customer questions on your site.

What happened to Google Business Profile Q&A?

Google is phasing the feature out: it first shut down the API that other software used to manage Q&A, then began removing the section itself. If this is news to you, here is the sequence.

How the Q&A section worked

In this part of Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business), any user could post a public question about a business and anyone could reply, not just the owner, so many businesses published their own frequently asked questions and posted the answers themselves.

First, the API was withdrawn

The first step was the withdrawal of the Q&A API, through which other software, such as the management tools some agencies use, could read and reply to that content. In its official change log, which gives the exact date, Google announced that it was updating this feature. If your agency used such a tool for profile management, that route no longer exists.

Then the public section began to disappear

Next, Google began removing it gradually from Business Profiles: businesses can no longer add or manage questions, and those already published stop showing. If the previous section held useful content (summer opening hours, home visits, where to park), copy it while you can still see it and move that key information to your website and your Business Profile.

Why does the end of Q&A matter for your business?

Because your customers still want answers before they get in touch. What changes is where they look for the answer: on your Google Business Profile, on your site and, where it has arrived, in content generated by artificial intelligence.

Your customers still want to know the same things

Before calling a plumber in Elche or booking a hairdresser in Alicante, customers check:

  • whether you work in their area;
  • what time you open;
  • whether they need an appointment;
  • how long a service takes.

If they can't find that quickly, they move on to other search results without ever contacting you.

What people see when they search for you

For a local search like "electrician in Valencia", Google shows business profiles in Google Search results and on Google Maps, with opening hours, services, photos and customer reviews. It's often the first thing customers see of your company, so your Business Profile is a key part of your marketing and should be complete and relevant.

What it means for your local SEO

According to Google's help pages, local search results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence: a complete Business Profile signals what services you offer and where, while reviews and mentions of your business across the internet count towards prominence. Your local SEO strategy and your visibility rest on information you control.

What is replacing Q&A on Google?

Artificial intelligence, through new Google features that reply to customers who search. If you have a shop, Google may also send you questions about what you sell.

Ask Maps: answers generated by Gemini

Google is rolling out a new approach based on Gemini, its artificial intelligence. To respond, the feature draws on:

  • the information in each Google Business Profile;
  • the business's reviews and photos;
  • its website.

Not available everywhere

The feature was launched first in some countries and has not reached all of them. To check, open Google Maps on your phone and run a search; button names may vary, as Google updates its features from time to time. Even if you don't see it, prepare your Business Profile anyway: any customer who comes across you online can read it.

What you control and what you don't

You don't decide what the AI says, but you decide what content you publish: a complete profile and a clear website provide it with accurate material. You don't write what customers say either, but you can influence it with good service and by requesting a review from every customer.

Yes-or-no questions about your products

If you have a shop, Google may ask you directly, as the owner, to confirm with a quick yes or no whether you stock a specific product. These questions appear in the Products section of your profile, as Google explains on its official help page. Only reply with what is true, or customers will come in for something you don't sell.

What to do now on your Google Business Profile

Adapt your strategy and use the features that remain to do the job Q&A used to do: complete every relevant field, respond to reviews, keep posting and check regularly what Google shows about your company.

Complete your categories, services and products

Your primary category tells Google what you do and secondary categories what else you do; see how to choose from the categories Google offers. Then add each service with a name customers understand ("boiler repair" rather than trade jargon) and, if you sell products, add them with a photo and price. In local search, a complete profile can help your visibility for the services you actually offer.

Check your attributes, opening hours and description

Attributes provide quick replies to what customers used to find out by asking; the options depend on your category and industry, for example:

  • accessibility, such as a wheelchair-accessible entrance;
  • the payment methods you accept;
  • amenities such as Wi-Fi.

Add your real opening hours, with specific hours for public holidays, and a clear description of your services and area. Our tips for optimising your Google Business Profile cover each field.

Respond to every review, including the good ones

Customers read online reviews before deciding, and the AI draws on them too. Review management is part of good customer service: thank each customer and, when faced with criticism, respond calmly and stick to the facts (see our guide to replying to negative reviews). When you post a reply that clears up a common query ("Yes, we're also open on Saturday mornings"), future customers reading your reviews will see it too. To keep new reviews coming in, try these strategies for getting more reviews.

Keep your photos, videos and posts up to date

Photos and a short video help customers picture your premises, the entrance and your best work; upload real, recent images to improve first impressions. Use posts (updates, offers and events) to share news, seasonal opening hours or a promotion: this fresh content is a simple form of digital marketing, and regular updates, once a week for example, show that your business is active. Our guides explain which photos and videos to upload and how to publish Google Business Profile posts.

Make sure your NAP matches everywhere

Your company name, address and phone number (known in local SEO as NAP) should be identical on Google, on your website, on your social media profiles and in directory listings; two different phone numbers undermine customer trust. See our guide to local citations and NAP.

Search for your business the way a customer would

Every month or so, search for your business from your phone and look at the search results as a first-time customer would. If the AI option is available where you are, put to it the questions your customers put to you and check its answer. Correct anything wrong at its source, and if someone else manages your digital presence, agree who handles this review.

The Performance section of your Business Profile also provides key clues. It shows:

  • your views and the searches that found you;
  • calls and direction requests;
  • clicks to your site.

Those search queries give you ideas for your FAQ page and your posts, and comparing performance month by month helps you see whether your changes are working. For more visibility, related guides explain how to add your business to the map and what the Google Map Pack is.

How to prepare an FAQ page on your website

A clear FAQ page, consistent with your Google Business Profile, is useful marketing: it builds trust, saves you calls and provides something Google doesn't, a space to explain your services as the expert. It also supports your local SEO and your online presence. If you're starting out, first check the elements every small business website should include.

Start from the queries you already receive

  • What you're asked every week by phone or through messaging apps like WhatsApp.
  • Enquiries that arrive by email or through the form on your site.
  • What customers want to know at the counter before buying.
  • Comments and reviews that come up again and again.
  • What you had published in the old Q&A section, if you kept a copy.

Ideas by type of business

Type of business Questions worth covering
Shop Do you have this product in store? Can I buy online and collect in store? Do you accept exchanges and returns?
Hairdresser's or beauty salon Do you take walk-ins? How long does the treatment take? Can I pay by card?
Plumber or electrician Are you available at weekends? Which areas do you cover? Is the quote free?
Restaurant or bar Do you have a terrace? Are there gluten-free options? Are you open all year?

How to write each reply

A few rules make your FAQ useful for customers and easy for a search engine to understand:

  1. Write each question the way a caller would put it ("Do you do emergency repairs in Valencia?").
  2. Answer in the first sentence with the specific information (times, areas, payment methods) and provide the same details you would give on the phone.
  3. Use your customers' words rather than trade jargon; our guide to keyword research explains how to choose them.
  4. Group related questions and put the most relevant information first.
  5. End each reply with your contact details: a WhatsApp button, your phone number or a form.

Every reply is also reusable marketing content: in posts, in replies to reviews or as quick replies in a messaging app such as WhatsApp Business. If writing isn't your thing, here are ideas for creating content without being a writer.

Tip

If your site is connected to Google Search Console, its Performance report lists the queries for which your pages appear: another source of real questions.

Keep your site and your profile consistent

When you update your opening hours, a service or your phone number on Google, update your site too, and vice versa. Mistakes to avoid after the change

Some habits no longer fit your strategy. Avoid these mistakes, on top of the usual digital marketing mistakes.

On your profile

  • Looking for a place to post FAQs: that option no longer exists.
  • Paying for a tool to manage the old Q&A section: its API has been withdrawn.
  • Leaving out the features that remain: a generic category, no services, no attributes, no holiday hours.
  • Saying "yes" to a product question about something you don't sell.
  • Ignoring reviews, or requesting them only from customers you expect to be happy: Google's policy doesn't allow selective requests.
  • Posting news once and then forgetting your profile for months.

On your site

  • Having no FAQ page, or filling it with vague phrases such as "it depends, give us a call".
  • Displaying opening hours, a phone number or an address that differ from your profile.
  • Repeating your trade and your town in every sentence.

Q&A is disappearing, but your customers' questions remain, and your Business Profile is still a free marketing tool for responding to them. Frequently asked questions

Has Q&A disappeared from Google Business Profile?
It is disappearing gradually. Google first withdrew the API for this feature, then began removing the Q&A section from business listings: businesses can no longer add or manage questions, and those already published are no longer shown.
What happens to the questions already on my profile?
They stop showing as Google removes the section, and you can no longer manage them. Move any useful content to your site and to the fields Google displays, such as opening hours, services or attributes.
What is Ask Maps?
It's a Google AI feature that responds to users with answers generated by Gemini, based on each business's profile information, reviews, photos and website. It was launched first in some countries and can't be used everywhere, so check whether it appears for you.
How can I influence what the AI says about my business?
You decide what you publish: a complete, up-to-date Business Profile with regular posts, real photos and a clear site that covers what customers want to know. Reply to every review, invite all your customers to leave one, not only those you expect to be happy, and correct any wrong information at its source.
Should I have an FAQ page on my website?
Yes. On your own site you can answer in writing what customers used to raise on Google, without other users editing your replies or answering for you, and it's one of the sources Gemini, Google's artificial intelligence, relies on.
