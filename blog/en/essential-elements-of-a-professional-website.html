What should a small business website include? Four core pages (home, services, about and contact), proof that people can trust you, an easy way to call or message you, a mobile-friendly design, basic SEO and the legal texts required in Spain. This guide covers each element with tips for freelancers and small businesses in Valencia, Alicante and Elche, and ends with a checklist.

Which pages does your website need?

Many small businesses start with four pages, plus the legal texts, and add more when the business needs them. A freelancer can sometimes fit everything on a single page, while businesses with several services should give each one its own page.

  • Homepage: what you do, where, and how to reach you.
  • Services page: what each service includes and how to ask for it.
  • About page: the people and experience behind the company.
  • Contact page: every way to get hold of you.
  • Legal pages: legal notice, privacy and cookie policies.
  • Blog (optional): useful articles, if you have the time.

Still not sure you need one? Read why your business needs a website.

What should your homepage include?

Your homepage should let visitors know in a few seconds what you do, where you work and how to reach you, without making them scroll.

A clear headline with your service and town

“Welcome to our website” says nothing; “Painter and decorator in Alicante: interiors, façades and small repairs” tells potential customers looking for a painter exactly what you provide.

A line below can add what makes you different, such as weekend availability. If many of your clients speak English, consider an English version too.

One call to action and links to your services

Make your main action, usually a call or a WhatsApp message, the most visible button at the top. Below it, add a summary of each service with a link to its page, a few photos and your rating.

The homepage is the hub of your digital presence: it doesn't need to say everything.

Services, about, contact and blog pages

Services page: describe each one clearly

For each service, explain what it includes, who it's for, the areas you cover and how to book. Provide a “from” price if you can: it saves time on both sides.

It's best to describe three to five services well rather than list twenty names, and if you sell products, give each product a photo, a short description and a price.

About page: who you are

People hire people. A photo of you and your team, your experience, the languages you speak and why you started tell new clients who will turn up at their door.

Contact page: every way to reach you

Include everything a customer needs to reach you: your phone number, WhatsApp, email, opening hours and the towns you cover. If customers visit you, provide directions and a map to help them find you.

Blog: only if you can keep it up

A blog isn't essential, but articles that answer what clients ask you can bring relevant traffic and show your expertise.

A blog whose last post is two years old, though, makes a business look closed. For the writing, see how to create web content without being a writer.

Trust signals: reviews, photos and a secure site

Before they call, potential customers look for signs that your business is real and active. Three elements do much of that work, and they're where small businesses can stand out.

Customer testimonials

Show your rating and a few testimonials, with a link to your Google Business Profile. Never invent or rewrite what clients wrote, and ask permission before you publish a customer's name.

If you display reviews, Spanish consumer rules also expect you to explain whether and how you check that they're genuine; in healthcare, check the rules on health advertising first. Here's how to get more reviews.

Photos of your own work

Photos of jobs you've done, your premises or your team build more trust than stock images. A photo of a kitchen you fitted in Elche supports what you say better than any adjective.

A secure site on your own domain

A domain such as yourbusiness.es or yourbusiness.com looks more serious than a free subdomain, and it stays yours if you change provider.

Browsers also warn visitors when a page isn't secure, so HTTPS is essential. Here's how to choose your domain name.

Calls to action that turn visitors into enquiries

A call to action tells visitors what to do next. Three are enough, and they should be easy to find on every page:

  • Phone: a tap-to-call link, so mobile users can ring you straight away.
  • Short form: four or five fields at most (name, a way to reply, message); each extra one is a reason to leave.
  • WhatsApp: a floating button that opens a chat. For small businesses in Spain, it's a natural channel for quick enquiries and customer support; see how to set up WhatsApp Business for free.

Use simple labels such as “Call now” or “Ask for a quote”, and check every month that messages still arrive.

Mobile-friendly web design and fast loading

Many of your customers will look you up on their mobile, so your design needs to work on a small screen first:

  • Text you can read without zooming.
  • Buttons big enough to tap with a thumb.
  • A simple menu with your main pages.
  • No pop-ups hiding the content.

Good web design is about a consistent brand, not features: your logo, two or three brand colours and photos of your work are enough.

Speed is just as important for the user experience: visitors in a hurry leave a slow site before the page appears, and slow loading can hold back your rankings. Compress your photos and test your pages with a free tool such as PageSpeed Insights; here's why speed affects your ranking.

Basic SEO: help search engines understand your business

Basic SEO, or search engine optimisation, means making it clear on every page what you do and where, so that people nearby who search for what you offer can find you.

Titles and headings with your service and town

Give each page a unique title and main heading that combine the service and the place, such as “Electrician in Elche”, and write content in the words your customers would type.

Consistent details everywhere

Use exactly the same business name, address and phone number on your website as on your Google Business Profile and in directories. Structured data such as schema LocalBusiness can also present this information in a format machines read easily.

An FAQ for your visitors

Answer the questions customers ask you every week, such as prices, areas covered and how long a job takes. Don't expect it to win extra space on the results page, though: Google now shows FAQ rich results only for a few well-known government and health sites.

A clear FAQ still helps visitors, and it can help search engines and AI assistants understand the page. To go further, read our local SEO guide.

Accessibility: make your website easy for all users

An accessible design works for people with disabilities, older users and anyone reading in bright sunlight. The basics:

  • Alt text on each image that carries information, so screen reader users know what it shows.
  • Enough contrast, and links that don't rely on colour alone.
  • Headings in order, and link text that says where it leads, not “click here”.
  • A visible label on every field, and a site that works for keyboard users.
  • Captions or a text summary for videos.

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) are the usual reference. If you sell online, ask an adviser whether the European Accessibility Act applies to your shop.

Legal texts for a website in Spain

Under Spain's e-commerce law (LSSI), the GDPR and Spanish data protection law, a company or freelancer's website usually needs three legal texts:

  • Legal notice (aviso legal): who runs the site, with your name or company name, NIF, address and email, plus your Registro Mercantil or colegio details where they apply.
  • Privacy policy: what personal data you collect, why, who you share it with, how long you keep it and how people can exercise their rights, with a link next to the send button.
  • Cookie policy: which cookies you use. Analytics or advertising cookies need consent first, and the Spanish data protection agency (AEPD) expects a banner where rejecting them is as simple as accepting.

If you show prices to consumers, they must include VAT. Templates copied from another site may not match how you work, so for obligations specific to your industry, consider asking an adviser.

Your small business website checklist

Run through this list on a mobile, step by step:

  • The homepage says what you do and where within a few seconds.
  • A call or WhatsApp button is visible on every page, and messages reach you.
  • Each service has its own section or page.
  • The about page shows who you are, with a real photo.
  • Testimonials and photos are genuine and up to date.
  • Your name, address and hours are identical everywhere.
  • The design works on a small screen, and photos have alt text.
  • The legal notice, privacy and cookie policies are in place.

A website isn't a project you finish and forget: every few months, add new photos, update your services and prices, and check that the buttons still work. A few basic metrics show whether it brings in enquiries.

Frequently asked questions

How many pages should a small business site have?
Four pages are a good start: a homepage, a services page, an about page and a contact page, plus the legal pages required in Spain. A freelancer with a single service can fit everything on one page.
Do I need a contact form if I already have WhatsApp?
Ideally you have both: some customers prefer a quick WhatsApp message, others a longer written message with the details of the job, especially when they want a quote.
Do I need testimonials on my website?
They aren't a legal requirement, but genuine testimonials help visitors trust a business they don't know yet. Ask permission before publishing a customer's name and never invent or edit one; in healthcare, check the rules on health advertising first.
How do I add a map to my website?
Find your business on Google Maps, click Share, choose Embed a map and paste the HTML code into your page. Also write your address as text, as it appears on your business profile.
