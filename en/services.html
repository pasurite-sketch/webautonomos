What to look for in a web design service

Not every web design service works the same way, and the difference often only shows up after you have signed up. A few questions can help you compare before you choose a website design company, whatever you decide in the end:

  • Can you see the actual website online before you pay anything?
  • Is the price you are quoted the price you will pay, or does support, hosting or a "bespoke" design push it up later?
  • What happens after launch — is there any ongoing support, or are you on your own from day one?
  • Who owns the domain name if you ever want to leave?
  • Is there a minimum contract, or can you cancel whenever you want?
  • Are the legal pages required in Spain included, or an extra you have to source yourself?

A modern, mobile-friendly design is table stakes at this point — many of your customers will look you up on a phone rather than a desktop. What separates a good service from an average one is what happens once the site is live: whether changes are quick and included, whether the people who built it are easy to reach, and whether the company behind it still exists in a couple of years to keep it running.

How the main options compare

  • A website builder (Wix, WordPress and similar platforms): you handle the design, the content, the SEO and the ongoing technical work yourself, and the cost depends on the plan you choose and the time you put in.
  • A freelance designer: around €800 to €2,000 up front for the design and development (Social Media Pymes, 2026), often bespoke to a written brief, with support after launch billed separately.
  • An agency: around €1,500 to €4,000 up front (Social Media Pymes, 2026), often starting with a discovery phase, with support after launch also often billed separately.
  • WebAutonomos: €15/month + VAT or €349 + VAT once, with the design, the content, hosting, the technical work and one change a month included — no discovery phase or written brief needed to start.

Done-for-you website or a website builder?

A website builder such as Wix or Hostinger sells you a platform: you choose the template, write the copy and adjust the layout yourself. A web design service like ours sells you a finished website: you send your details, we build it, you approve it. Neither is wrong — each suits a different kind of business owner, depending on whether you have the time and enjoy doing it yourself.
